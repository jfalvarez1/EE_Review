SPMI, I3C and Bus Ownership

A clean digital waveform needs an electrical agreement: who can drive each wire, at what voltage, during which part of the transaction, and what happens when nobody drives. Start with that agreement before interpreting a decoder's error message.

Evidence trail: this lesson uses public protocol material and device datasheets. The next lessons contain three annotated LTspice plots of contention and Hi-Z and two plots explaining negative voltage readings, with circuits, CSV data and reproduction files embedded beside them.

Driver state is different from bus voltage

A three-state output can drive HIGH, drive LOW, or turn off its output stage (Hi-Z). Hi-Z does not command a midpoint voltage: pull resistors, leakage, bus keepers, capacitance and other enabled devices determine the pin voltage. An open-drain output only sinks or releases; a pullup supplies the HIGH. A push-pull output can source and sink. Those are circuit properties, independent of the protocol decoder.

SPMI: a public PMIC implementation you can inspect

MIPI describes SPMI as the CMOS control interface between power controllers and power-management devices. The full SPMI specification requires MIPI membership; this course does not invent its normative timing or claim compliance from a generic simulation. MIPI SPMI overview and specification access.

A useful public example is TI TPS6594-Q1, §8.4.2 and Figure 8-49. In that implementation, a primary PMIC coordinates power-state transitions with secondary PMICs. The figure explicitly distinguishes driven SDATA, peripheral responses, and undriven intervals with pulldown; SCLK is driven by the bus owner. Its pin-function table identifies SDATA's push-pull/Hi-Z behavior and configurable pin functions. These details belong to this device, not every PMIC.

Do not add I²C pullups or send I²C bus-clear clocks to an SPMI interface simply because it also has two wires. Obtain the specific controller/PMIC timing, I/O voltage, allowable loading and recovery sequence. For proprietary timing you cannot access, record the missing requirement instead of making up a limit.

I3C and I²C: where a familiar wire changes behavior

MIPI's public FAQ describes SDA arbitration in open-drain mode and faster transfers in push-pull mode. The active controller manages the open-drain pullup; SCL remains push-pull. A legacy I²C translator or buffer therefore needs explicit compatibility with the actual I3C mode. A part being bidirectional is insufficient evidence. See the FAQ sections on pullups, legacy coexistence and voltage translation.

For debugging, align the analog trace with an ownership table: controller transmitting, target transmitting, acknowledgment, turnaround, idle. If the trace becomes abnormal only when the owner changes, investigate enable overlap and loss of synchronization before changing termination. If it rings on every fast edge, investigate the interconnect and measurement setup as well.

Lab — make a bus ownership worksheet

Time: 35 minutes. Use a board schematic plus public datasheets, or the TPS6594-Q1 example above. Draw each device, voltage domain, buffer and bias resistor. For each signal list the reset state, normal driver, receiver, release condition and disabled behavior. Leave undocumented entries explicitly unknown.

Check your reasoning: is a midpoint evidence of Hi-Z?

No. A disconnected line can retain a previous HIGH, decay toward a bias rail, or pick up interference. A persistent midpoint can also come from two enabled drivers. The decisive evidence is the enable state and current path, not a third-looking voltage alone.
