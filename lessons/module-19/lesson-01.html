Logic Level Fundamentals

Digital interfaces require precise voltage thresholds to distinguish logic states. Understanding logic levels is fundamental when interfacing devices with different voltage standards—a common challenge in modern mixed-voltage systems.

Logic Families and Their Voltage Levels

Different logic families define HIGH and LOW thresholds differently:

FamilyVCCVOH minVIH minVIL maxVOL max
5V TTL5.0V2.4V2.0V0.8V0.4V
5V CMOS5.0V4.4V3.5V1.5V0.5V
3.3V LVTTL (JESD8-B)3.3V2.4V2.0V0.8V0.4V
3.3V LVCMOS (JESD8-B)3.3V3.1V2.0V0.8V0.2V
2.5V LVCMOS (JESD8-5)2.5V2.0V1.7V0.7V0.4V
1.8V LVCMOS (JESD8-7)1.8V1.2V1.17V0.63V0.45V
LVTTL and LVCMOS are not the same thing, and the row you use changes the answer. Both run on 3.3 V and both are defined in JESD8-B, but they specify the output differently: LVTTL promises only 2.4 V at its rated 2 mA, while LVCMOS promises VDD − 0.2 at 100 µA. The inputs are the same 2.0/0.8 V pair in both, which is why the distinction is easy to miss.

It decides real cases. Driving a 5 V CMOS input, which wants 3.5 V, the LVTTL number (2.4 V) fails by 1.1 V and the LVCMOS number (3.1 V) still fails, by 0.4 V — so that direction always needs a translator. Driving a 5 V TTL input, which wants only 2.0 V, both pass. And a part that quotes VOH = 2.4 V is telling you it was characterised at milliamps: draw microamps from it and you will measure something much closer to the rail.

Level Shifting Requirements

When interfacing different voltage domains, verify compatibility:

  • High-to-Low compatibility: 5V → 3.3V often works if input has 5V tolerant I/O
  • Low-to-High challenge: 3.3V → 5V TTL may work (2.4V > 2.0V threshold), but 3.3V → 5V CMOS often fails (2.4V < 3.5V threshold)
  • Always check: Absolute maximum ratings for input overvoltage

Key Takeaways

  • Modern systems often mix 1.8V, 2.5V, 3.3V, and 5V logic domains
  • TTL thresholds (~30% of VCC) differ from CMOS (~50% of VCC)
  • Noise margin determines immunity to interference—larger is better
  • 5V-tolerant inputs are common on 3.3V devices; check the datasheet
  • Level shifters are required when voltage domains are incompatible

Sources

  • JEDEC JESD8-B — Interface Standard for Nominal 3 V / 3.3 V Supply Digital Integrated Circuits. Defines BOTH the LVTTL and the LVCMOS level sets in the table above, which is why parts on the same rail can quote different VOH.
  • JEDEC JESD8-5 (2.5 V) and JESD8-7 (1.8 V) — the supply-voltage and interface standards for those rails. Where the 0.65/0.35 VDD input thresholds and the VDD − 0.45 output level come from. JEDEC standards are free to download from jedec.org after registration.
  • On reading a VOH number. Every one of these is specified AT A STATED OUTPUT CURRENT, and the two families differ mostly in that current: LVTTL at milliamps, LVCMOS at microamps. A level that looks incompatible on paper often is not, and one that looks fine often is not either. The datasheet condition column is the part that matters.
