Sample and Hold Circuits

Sample and Hold (S/H or SHA) circuits capture an analog voltage at a precise instant and hold it constant for subsequent processing. They're fundamental to ADC front-ends, data acquisition systems, and any application requiring synchronized sampling of time-varying signals.

Sample Mode
Output tracks input (switch closed)
Hold Mode
Output remains constant (switch open)

Charge Injection

When the switch opens, charge stored in the switch channel is dumped onto CH, causing a "hold step" or "pedestal":

Hold Step: ΔV = Qinj / CH
Source Typical Charge Mitigation
MOSFET channel charge 0.1 - 10 pC Use dummy switch for cancellation
Gate-drain capacitance ~Cgd × ΔVgate CMOS transmission gate
Clock feedthrough Variable Careful layout, shielding

Aperture Jitter and ADC Performance

Aperture jitter limits the effective resolution when sampling high-frequency signals:

SNR Limit from Jitter: SNR = −20 × log10(2π × fin × tjitter)
Jitter (ps RMS) Max Freq @ 12-bit Max Freq @ 16-bit
1 25 MHz 1.6 MHz
10 2.5 MHz 160 kHz
100 250 kHz 16 kHz
1000 25 kHz 1.6 kHz
Critical for High-Speed ADCs: A 14-bit 100MHz ADC needs jitter < 0.5ps to maintain full resolution. This requires careful clock distribution and low-noise clock sources.

Track-and-Hold vs Sample-and-Hold

Sample-and-Hold
Brief sample window, long hold. Used with SAR ADCs.
Track-and-Hold
Continuously tracks input, holds on command. Faster acquisition.

Modern high-speed ADCs often have internal T/H circuits optimized for their architecture.
