Transimpedance Amplifiers (TIA)

Transimpedance amplifiers convert current to voltage, making them essential for photodiode interfaces, current sensing, and DAC output stages. The feedback resistor sets the gain in V/A (ohms).

Transfer Function
Vout = −Iin × Rf
Transimpedance Gain
ZT = Vout/Iin = Rf [V/A]
Key Insight: The input appears as a virtual ground, so the photodiode capacitance sees zero voltage change. All input current flows through Rf.

Stability Challenge

The photodiode capacitance (Cpd) creates a pole that can cause oscillation. The noise gain rises at high frequencies:

Noise Gain Peak
NGpk = 1 + Cpd/Cf
Bandwidth with Cf
BW = 1/(2π × Rf × Cf)
Stability Rule: NGpk × BW must be well below the op-amp GBW (at least 3× margin) or oscillation will occur.
Parameter Effect of Increasing Trade-off
Rf Higher gain, lower BW More noise from Rf thermal noise
Cf Better stability, lower BW Reduced bandwidth
Cpd Higher noise gain peak More instability risk

Design Procedure

  1. Choose Rf for required gain: Rf = Vout/Ipd
  2. Determine Cpd from photodiode datasheet
  3. Calculate Cf for stability: Cf ≈ √(Cpd/(2π × Rf × GBW))
  4. Verify: BW = 1/(2π × Rf × Cf)
  5. Check: NGpk × BW < GBW/3

Noise Analysis

TIA noise has three main contributions:

Noise Source Formula Frequency Dependence
Rf thermal noise en = √(4kTRf) White noise, dominates at low freq
Op-amp voltage noise × NG eop × (1 + Cpd/Cf) Rises at high freq due to noise gain
Op-amp current noise × Rf in × Rf White noise
Noise Optimization: For low noise, use low-noise JFET op-amps (low in) with the smallest Rf that gives adequate signal. The noise gain peaking at high frequencies often dominates in wideband TIAs.
