Analog Multipliers and Modulators

Analog multipliers perform the mathematical operation Vout = k × Vx × Vy. They're essential for RF mixers, power measurement, automatic gain control (AGC), frequency translation, and true RMS detection. Understanding the translinear principle unlocks many analog signal processing techniques.

Transfer Function
Vout = (Vx × Vy) / Vscale
Typical Vscale
10V (for ±10V full-scale inputs)

Gilbert Cell Multiplier

The Gilbert cell is the most common analog multiplier topology, exploiting the exponential I-V relationship of BJTs:

How the Gilbert Cell Works

The lower pair (Q5, Q6) steers current based on Vy. The upper quad (Q1-Q4) multiplies this current by the sign of Vx:

Vx Vy Current Flow Output
+ + Q1, Q3 conduct +
+ − Q1, Q4 conduct −
− + Q2, Q3 conduct −
− − Q2, Q4 conduct +
Translinear Principle: In a loop of BJT junctions with alternating polarity, the product of collector currents in one direction equals the product in the other direction. This enables multiplication without explicit log/antilog stages.

Mixer (Frequency Translation)

When multiplying two sinusoids, sum and difference frequencies result:

cos(ω1t) × cos(ω2t) = ½[cos((ω1−ω2)t) + cos((ω1+ω2)t)]

A filter then selects either the sum (upconversion) or difference (downconversion) frequency.
