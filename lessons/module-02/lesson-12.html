Precision Rectifiers

Precision rectifiers (also called "super diodes" or "ideal diode circuits") use op-amps to overcome the forward voltage drop of diodes. This enables accurate rectification of millivolt-level signals that would be completely lost in a conventional diode's VF drop.

How It Works

  • When Vin > 0: Op-amp output goes positive, D1 conducts, feedback forces Vout = Vin
  • When Vin < 0: Op-amp output goes negative, D1 blocks, Vout = 0
  • The op-amp's gain "absorbs" the diode's VF drop, making it invisible to the output

Bandwidth Considerations

Precision rectifiers have limited bandwidth due to:

  • Op-amp slew rate: Limits how fast the output can transition at zero-crossing
  • Diode switching time: Schottky diodes (1N5711, BAT54) are faster than silicon
  • Op-amp GBW: Closed-loop bandwidth affects small-signal response
