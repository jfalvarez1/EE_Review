Gate Driver Design

Gate drivers are the interface between low-power control signals and high-power switching devices. A well-designed gate driver ensures fast, clean switching while protecting against shoot-through, dV/dt induced turn-on, and noise-induced false triggering. This lesson covers gate driver requirements, topologies, and practical design techniques.

Industry Applications

  • DC-DC Converters - High-side and synchronous FET driving
  • Motor Drives - 3-phase inverter IGBT/MOSFET gate control
  • Solar Inverters - Grid-tied power conversion
  • EV/HEV - High-voltage SiC/GaN traction inverters
  • Welding Equipment - High-current IGBT control

1. Gate Driver Requirements

Why Gate Drivers Are Needed

MOSFET and IGBT gates are capacitive loads. To switch quickly, you need:

  • High peak current to charge/discharge gate capacitance
  • Low output impedance for fast edges
  • Level shifting for high-side switches
  • Isolation for safety and noise immunity

Gate Charge and Switching Time

Gate Current: \( I_g = \frac{Q_g}{t_{sw}} \)

For Qg = 100nC and tsw = 50ns: Ig = 2A peak!

Rise Time: \( t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \times 2.2 \)

Where Qgd is the Miller (gate-drain) charge

Power Dissipation in Driver: \( P = Q_g \times V_{gs} \times f_{sw} \)

Key Gate Driver Specifications

Parameter Typical Range Why It Matters
Peak Source/Sink Current 0.5A - 10A Determines switching speed
Rise/Fall Time 5ns - 50ns Affects EMI and dV/dt stress
Propagation Delay 10ns - 100ns Critical for dead-time control
Delay Matching 1ns - 10ns Prevents shoot-through in half-bridge
UVLO (Under-Voltage Lockout) 8V - 12V Prevents partial turn-on
dV/dt Immunity 50V/ns - 200V/ns Prevents false triggering from switching node

2. Low-Side Gate Driver

The simplest configuration - driver ground is common with MOSFET source:

Gate Resistor Selection

\[ R_g = \frac{V_{driver} - V_{gs,th}}{I_{g,peak}} - R_{g,int} \]

Trade-offs:

  • Lower Rg → Faster switching, higher EMI, higher dI/dt
  • Higher Rg → Slower switching, lower EMI, reduced efficiency

Typical range: 1Ω - 33Ω depending on application

3. High-Side Gate Driver

High-side MOSFETs have their source at the switching node, requiring level-shifted or floating gate drive:

Bootstrap Gate Driver

Bootstrap Capacitor Sizing

\[ C_{boot} \geq \frac{Q_g + I_{leak} \cdot t_{on,max}}{\Delta V_{boot}} \]

Where:

  • Qg = total gate charge of high-side FET
  • Ileak = driver quiescent current + level shifter current
  • ton,max = maximum high-side on-time
  • ΔVboot = allowable voltage droop (typically 0.5-1V)

Rule of thumb: Cboot ≥ 10 × (Qg in nC) → value in µF

Bootstrap Limitations

  • Requires periodic low-side turn-on to refresh charge
  • Maximum duty cycle limited (typically 99%)
  • Not suitable for 100% duty cycle applications
  • Minimum off-time needed for refresh
  • Alternative: Isolated gate driver or charge pump

4. Isolated Gate Drivers

For high-voltage applications, isolation between control and power provides safety and eliminates bootstrap limitations:

Isolated Driver Topologies

Technology Isolation Rating Propagation Delay Notes
Optocoupler + Buffer 2.5 - 5 kV 100 - 500 ns Legacy, slow, LED aging
Transformer-based 2 - 5 kV 20 - 100 ns Fast, requires AC coupling
Capacitive isolation 5 - 8 kV 10 - 50 ns High CMTI, compact (Si8xxx)
Magnetic (coreless) 3 - 5 kV 10 - 30 ns Fast, robust (ADuM series)

Key Specification: CMTI

Common-Mode Transient Immunity (CMTI) is critical for high-speed switching:

CMTI specifies the maximum dV/dt across the isolation barrier that won't cause false triggering.

Requirement: \( CMTI > \frac{V_{bus}}{t_{rise}} \)

Example: 400V bus with 20ns rise time → CMTI > 20 V/ns

SiC/GaN may switch at 100+ V/ns - requires high CMTI drivers!

5. Protection Features

Desaturation Detection (DESAT)

Monitors VCE (IGBT) or VDS (MOSFET) during conduction. High voltage indicates current overload or short circuit.

Active Miller Clamp

Clamps gate to source during off-state to prevent dV/dt-induced turn-on from Miller capacitance coupling.

Soft Turn-Off

Upon fault detection, slowly reduces gate voltage to limit di/dt and voltage overshoot during emergency shutdown.

Gate Driver Selection Checklist

  • Peak source/sink current for desired switching speed
  • UVLO matches your VCC supply design
  • Propagation delay acceptable for control loop
  • CMTI adequate for switching dV/dt
  • Protection features needed (DESAT, Miller clamp)
  • Package thermal capability for power dissipation
