ESD Protection Design

Protecting electronic systems from electrostatic discharge

Understanding ESD Threats

Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) is a sudden flow of electricity between charged objects. It can destroy sensitive electronics instantly:

Failure Modes:
  • Gate oxide breakdown: MOSFETs with thin gate oxide (< 10nm)
  • Junction damage: PN junctions with low breakdown voltage
  • Metal migration: Thin metal traces vaporized by current
  • Latent damage: Weakened but functional - fails later in field

Via Guideline: Use at least 2-3 vias for ESD device ground connections. Calculate total via inductance: L ≈ 1nH per via. Three 0.3mm vias ≈ 0.3nH total, acceptable for most applications.

System-Level ESD Design

Protection Coordination

Coordination Principle: Each protection layer should:
  1. React faster than the next inner layer
  2. Have lower clamping voltage than the next layer's damage threshold
  3. Handle peak current that may pass through
