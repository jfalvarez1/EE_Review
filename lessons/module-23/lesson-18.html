Hot-Swap & Inrush Current Control

Hot-swap circuits enable safe insertion and removal of circuit boards in live systems without causing voltage transients, connector damage, or system disruption. This capability is essential in servers, telecom equipment, and mission-critical systems requiring high availability.

Industry Applications

  • Server Blades - Compute modules inserted into live backplanes
  • Storage Arrays - Hot-swappable disk drives and controllers
  • Telecom Carriers - -48V line cards in central offices
  • Industrial PLCs - I/O modules replaced during operation
  • USB/PCIe - Consumer hot-plug interfaces

1. The Inrush Current Problem

When a board with bulk capacitors is connected to a power bus, the discharged capacitors appear as a short circuit, causing massive inrush current:

Inrush Current Magnitude

For an ideal capacitor connected through trace/connector resistance:

\[ I_{peak} = \frac{V_{bus}}{R_{trace} + R_{conn}} \]

With typical Rtrace ≈ 10mΩ and Rconn ≈ 50mΩ at 12V: Ipeak ≈ 200A!

Energy Dissipated in Resistance

\[ E = \frac{1}{2} C V^2 \]

For 2000µF at 12V: E = 0.144J per insertion (causes connector wear)

Why Thermistors Are Not Enough

  • NTC thermistors limit cold inrush but have low resistance when warm
  • Repeated insertions while thermistor is warm = no protection
  • Thermistors waste power continuously after startup
  • Not suitable for true hot-swap (live systems)

2. Hot-Swap Controller Architecture

Dedicated hot-swap controllers use a series MOSFET to implement controlled turn-on, current limiting, and fault protection.

Key Controller Functions

Function Purpose Typical Implementation
Soft-Start Ramp gate voltage slowly to limit dV/dt Internal current source (10-50µA) charges gate
Current Limit Prevent overcurrent during startup and faults 50mV sense threshold → Ilim = 50mV/Rsense
Circuit Breaker Latch off after prolonged overcurrent Timer expires → MOSFET off, requires reset
Power Good Signal downstream that power is stable Open-drain output, active after Vout > threshold
UV/OV Protection Protect against out-of-range input voltage Comparators with hysteresis
Reverse Polarity Protect against wrong insertion Body diode blocks, MOSFET stays off

3. MOSFET Safe Operating Area (SOA)

The most critical aspect of hot-swap design is ensuring the series MOSFET survives the turn-on transient. During soft-start, the MOSFET operates in its linear region with both high VDS and high ID, causing significant power dissipation.

SOA Energy Calculation

During soft-start, the MOSFET dissipates energy charging the output capacitor:

\[ E_{MOSFET} = \frac{1}{2} C_{load} \cdot V_{bus}^2 \]

This is independent of soft-start time! Longer soft-start spreads power over time, reducing peak dissipation.

Required Soft-Start Time

For a given current limit and load capacitance:

\[ t_{ss} = \frac{C_{load} \cdot V_{bus}}{I_{limit}} \]

The MOSFET must handle the SOA for this duration.

Peak Power Dissipation

At the midpoint of soft-start (VDS ≈ Vbus/2, ID = Ilimit):

\[ P_{peak} = \frac{V_{bus}}{2} \cdot I_{limit} \]

4. Practical Design Example: 12V/10A Hot-Swap

Specifications

  • Input: 12V ± 10% (10.8V to 13.2V)
  • Load: 10A continuous, 2000µF bulk capacitance
  • Current limit: 15A (50% headroom)
  • Soft-start: < 10ms for SOA compliance

Component Selection

Sense Resistor

For 15A limit with 50mV sense threshold:

\[ R_{sense} = \frac{50mV}{15A} = 3.3m\Omega \]

Power dissipation: \( P = I^2 R = 10^2 \times 0.0033 = 0.33W \)

Select: 3mΩ, 1W, 1% sense resistor (e.g., Vishay WSL2512)

MOSFET Selection

Calculate soft-start time and peak dissipation:

\[ t_{ss} = \frac{2000\mu F \times 13.2V}{15A} = 1.76ms \]

\[ P_{peak} = \frac{13.2V}{2} \times 15A = 99W \]

\[ E_{total} = \frac{1}{2} \times 2000\mu F \times 13.2^2 = 0.174J \]

MOSFET requirements:

  • VDS ≥ 20V (use 30V for margin)
  • ID ≥ 15A at Tj = 125°C
  • RDS(on) < 10mΩ for low conduction loss
  • SOA: Must handle 99W for 1.76ms (check 1ms/10ms curves)

Suggested MOSFET: Si7157DP (30V, 60A, RDS = 3.1mΩ, robust SOA)

Controller Selection

For 12V applications, use a controller with integrated charge pump for N-channel high-side drive:

  • LTC4215 - Feature-rich, I2C monitoring, ±15% OV/UV
  • TPS2480 - Low cost, simple, -48V version available
  • MAX5976B - Fast response, adjustable dV/dt

5. Circuit Breaker and Retry Logic

Hot-swap controllers implement fault handling to protect against sustained overcurrent:

Circuit Breaker Modes

Mode Behavior Application
Latch-Off Fault triggers shutdown, requires external reset (power cycle or pin) High-reliability systems needing operator intervention
Auto-Retry After fault timeout, attempts restart (with cool-down delay) Unattended systems, transient fault recovery
Foldback Reduces current limit progressively during prolonged overload Systems with high inrush but moderate steady-state

Timer Capacitor Calculation

Circuit breaker timeout is set by an external capacitor:

\[ t_{CB} = k \times C_{timer} \]

Where k is controller-specific (check datasheet). For LTC4215, k ≈ 250ms/nF.

For 5ms circuit breaker time: \( C_{timer} = \frac{5ms}{250ms/nF} = 20pF \)

Fault Handling Best Practices

  • Set circuit breaker timeout slightly longer than soft-start time
  • Use latch-off mode for safety-critical applications
  • Add retry delay (cool-down) to prevent thermal runaway
  • Monitor FAULT output for system-level diagnostics
  • Consider I2C/PMBus controllers for telemetry (voltage, current, temperature)

6. High-Side vs Low-Side Configurations

High-Side Switch (N-Channel)

  • MOSFET between Vin and load
  • Requires charge pump to drive gate above Vin
  • Ground plane remains uninterrupted
  • Preferred for most applications

Low-Side Switch (N-Channel)

  • MOSFET between load and GND
  • Simple gate drive (VGS referenced to GND)
  • Load floats during off-state
  • Can cause ground bounce during switching

Negative Voltage Rails (-48V Telecom)

For negative voltages, the polarity is reversed. Use controllers designed for negative rails (e.g., LTC4213 for -48V):

  • MOSFET placed in return path (low-side relative to negative rail)
  • Controller referenced to negative rail
  • Sense resistor in series with MOSFET source

7. Layout Considerations

Critical Layout Rules

  • Kelvin sensing: Route sense resistor connections directly to resistor pads, not through power path
  • MOSFET placement: Close to connector to minimize inductance in hot path
  • Thermal relief: Large copper area under MOSFET for heat spreading
  • Gate drive: Short, direct traces from controller GATE pin to MOSFET gate
  • Bypass caps: Close to controller VCC and GND pins

9. Design Checklist

Component Sizing

  • ☐ Calculate sense resistor for desired current limit
  • ☐ Verify sense resistor power rating (I²R × 2)
  • ☐ Calculate soft-start time from Cload and Ilimit
  • ☐ Verify MOSFET SOA covers soft-start duration and power
  • ☐ Select MOSFET with appropriate VDS, ID, RDS(on)
  • ☐ Size timer capacitor for circuit breaker timeout

Protection Features

  • ☐ UV/OV thresholds set appropriately for bus tolerance
  • ☐ Reverse polarity protection (MOSFET body diode orientation)
  • ☐ Power Good delay allows output to stabilize
  • ☐ Fault reporting connected to system monitor

Thermal Design

  • ☐ MOSFET thermal resistance (RθJA) allows safe Tj during soft-start
  • ☐ Adequate copper area for heat spreading
  • ☐ Consider thermal pad or heatsink for high-power designs

Layout

  • ☐ Kelvin sense connections to Rsense
  • ☐ Short gate drive trace
  • ☐ Bypass capacitors at controller power pins
  • ☐ Wide power traces for rated current
