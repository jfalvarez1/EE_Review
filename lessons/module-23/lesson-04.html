Case Study: Industrial 4-20mA Sensor Interface

Design a robust interface for industrial process sensors using the 4-20mA current loop standard. This scenario covers signal conditioning, isolation, and noise immunity in harsh industrial environments.

Why 4-20mA? Current remains accurate only while the transmitter has sufficient compliance voltage. Cable drop consumes that headroom; grounding, isolation and filtering still determine interference performance. The 4mA live zero can distinguish a minimum reading from an open loop.

The Scenario

Requirement: Interface a pressure transmitter to a PLC analog input module.

  • Sensor: 4-20mA output, 0-100 PSI range
  • Cable run: 200m twisted pair
  • Environment: Factory floor with VFDs, welders, motors
  • ADC: 12-bit, 0-5V input range
  • Accuracy required: ±0.5% of reading

What to run: a DC operating point; then a transient run of 100 ms in steps of 1 ms.

What to watch: V(vsense), V(vfilt), V(rcv_n2).

At DC, the converter model loads the shunt through Rfilter + Radc = 1.01 MΩ. Thus Req = 250 Ω || 1.01 MΩ = 249.938 Ω, V(vsense) = 12 mA × Req = 2.99926 V, and V(vfilt) = V(vsense) × 1 MΩ/1.01 MΩ = 2.96956 V. The nominal ADC reading is already 1.015% low relative to 3.000 V. Calibration or a buffer is required before claiming the stated ±0.5% accuracy.

The low-frequency pole is approximately 1/[2π × ((10 kΩ + 250 Ω) || 1 MΩ) × 160 nF] = 98.0 Hz. Relative to DC this attenuates 50 Hz by only 1.00 dB and 60 Hz by 1.38 dB. It is not a mains notch.

Compliance and protection are separate checks

The upper diode is now an ordinary diode; a 5.1 V zener tied to the 24 V rail would conduct continuously and corrupt the measurement. The lower clamp is explicitly 8.2 V, above the approximately 7.0 V receiver-terminal voltage at 20 mA. This is a nominal clamp model, not a qualified TVS design.

At 20 mA, subtract both cable drops, Rprot and the loaded shunt drop from 24 V: approximately 16.60 V remains across the transmitter. Check this against its actual minimum supply at worst-case cable resistance. An ideal current source will keep forcing current even when that real transmitter would drop out.

Bench acceptance: use a loop calibrator at 4, 12 and 20 mA, record both shunt and ADC voltages, and distinguish offset from gain error. Measure at the ADC pins and include resistor tolerance, leakage, temperature and ADC acquisition settling. The simple Radc/Cadc load is not an isolation barrier or a switched-capacitor ADC.

Reference: TI SLVAF40, sections 2 and 3.2 discusses burden-resistor stress and field miswiring. A nominal 8.2 V clamp cannot by itself protect a 5 V ADC or survive sustained 24 V miswiring; those require a separately rated protection/current-limit path.

Then change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it. A simulator is only worth the time if you are checking an answer you already have — otherwise it is a very slow way of being surprised.
