Case Study: Switching Power Supply Failure

Investigate a failed ATX power supply with no output. This scenario covers systematic SMPS troubleshooting, from input protection through the control loop, identifying common failure modes.

Safety Warning: SMPS contain lethal voltages even when unplugged. The main filter capacitor can hold charge >300V. Always discharge and verify before touching. Use isolation transformer when testing under power.

The Scenario

Equipment: 500W ATX PC power supply

  • Reported: Computer suddenly died, won't turn on
  • No burning smell, no visible damage
  • Fuse OK, bridge rectifier OK, main cap charges to 320V
  • Standby 5V present at motherboard connector
  • PS_ON signal present but no 12V, 5V, 3.3V outputs

Root Cause: Failed Electrolytic Capacitor

Finding: Aux winding filter capacitor (C15, 47µF/25V) has high ESR.

  • ESR measured: 8Ω (expected: <0.5Ω)
  • Ripple prevents PWM controller from reaching UVLO threshold
  • Controller tries to start, drops out, oscillates at ~2Hz
  • Classic capacitor "hiccup" failure mode

Repair: Replace C15 with 47µF/35V 105°C low-ESR capacitor. Also replace nearby electrolytics as preventive maintenance.

Common SMPS Failure Modes

Component Failure Mode Symptom
Electrolytic Caps High ESR, low capacitance Hiccup, ripple, instability
Primary MOSFET Short circuit Blown fuse, smoke
Schottky Rectifiers Short or open No output or shorted rail
PWM Controller IC EOS/Latchup No switching, overheating
Optocoupler CTR degradation Poor regulation, overshoot
