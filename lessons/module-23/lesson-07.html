LED Driver Design

LEDs require constant current drive for consistent brightness and long life. This lesson covers LED driver topologies from simple linear regulators to high-efficiency switch-mode drivers for automotive, display, and general lighting applications.

Industry Reference: Based on TI LED Driver Design Guide, Analog Devices LED Driver Fundamentals, and Diodes Inc. Application Notes.

Section 1: LED Characteristics

LEDs are current-driven devices with an exponential V-I relationship:

LED Type Forward Voltage Typical Current Luminous Efficacy
Red (AlGaInP) 1.8 - 2.2V 20mA - 1A 50-100 lm/W
Green (InGaN) 2.9 - 3.5V 20mA - 1A 100-150 lm/W
Blue (InGaN) 2.8 - 3.4V 20mA - 1A 30-50 lm/W
White (Blue + Phosphor) 2.9 - 3.6V 20mA - 3A 150-200+ lm/W
High-Power (Cree, Lumileds) 2.7 - 3.5V 350mA - 3A 150-200 lm/W
Temperature Coefficient: LED VF decreases ~2-4mV/°C. This means constant voltage drive causes thermal runaway! Always use constant current drive.

Section 3: Dimming Methods

Method Range Color Shift EMI Use Case
PWM Dimming 1000:1 or better None (constant IF) Can cause issues at low frequency Display backlights, general
Analog (DC) Dimming ~10:1 Yes (chromaticity shifts) Low Simple, cost-sensitive
Hybrid 1000:1+ Minimal Medium High-end displays
PWM Frequency Selection:
  • <100Hz: Visible flicker (unacceptable)
  • 100-400Hz: Minimal flicker, some video artifacts
  • 1-2kHz: Good for most applications
  • >20kHz: Above audible range, no flicker concerns
