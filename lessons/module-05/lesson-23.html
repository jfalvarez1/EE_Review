Emitter Follower Oscillation

The emitter follower can oscillate when driving capacitive loads. This paradox - a "unity gain buffer" becoming unstable - trips up many designers. The root cause is the inductive output impedance at high frequencies.

Low Frequency Zout
≈ re + RS/β
High Frequency Zout
Inductive: Z ∝ jω
Resonance with CL
fosc = 1/(2π√LC)
Classic Fix
Base stopper resistor
Warning: An emitter follower with 50Ω source driving 100pF+ can oscillate at 10-100MHz. This is invisible on slow scopes!

Why Output Looks Inductive

At high frequencies, the base-emitter capacitance Cπ creates phase shift. The transistor's response lags the input, making the output impedance appear inductive.

Simplified model:

Zout ≈ re + jωLeff

Leff ≈ re × RS × Cπ

Art of Electronics X Chapters: "The emitter follower's output impedance rises with frequency, eventually looking like an inductance. Combined with a capacitive load, you get an LC tank that can ring or oscillate."

Stabilization Techniques

Technique Implementation Why It Works
Base Stopper Resistor 10-100Ω in series with base Adds damping, reduces loop gain at HF
Output Series Resistor 5-50Ω in series with output Isolates inductive Zout from CL
Ferrite Bead On base or collector lead Absorbs HF energy, lossy at resonance
Snubber RC 10Ω + 100pF on output Damps resonance, dissipates energy
Lower RS Add buffer before EF Reduces effective inductance
Rule of Thumb: Always add a 10-47Ω base stopper when driving capacitive loads or cables > 1 foot.
