Translinear Circuits

Translinear circuits exploit the exponential I-V relationship of BJTs to perform mathematical operations like multiplication, division, and square root in the current domain. They form the basis of analog multipliers, RMS-to-DC converters, and log amplifiers.

Translinear Principle
Product of CW currents = Product of CCW currents
Key Equation
IC = IS eVBE/VT
Loop Rule
Sum of VBE around loop = 0
Applications
Multipliers, dividers, RMS, log amps
Art of Electronics: "The translinear principle is one of the most powerful techniques in analog IC design, enabling precise mathematical operations using only matched transistors."

The circuit

A translinear loop: four base–emitter junctions in a closed loop, two facing each way. Because each VBE is the logarithm of its collector current, and because the voltages around any closed loop sum to zero, the currents obey a product relationship that holds over decades without a single trimmed component.

Going round the loop: \(V_{BE1} + V_{BE2} = V_{BE3} + V_{BE4}\). Substituting \(V_{BE} = V_T\ln(I_C/I_S)\) turns the sum of logarithms into a product, giving \(I_1 I_2 = I_3 I_4\). Note what is not needed: no resistors set the relationship, so it does not depend on resistor tolerance, and \(V_T\) and \(I_S\) cancel provided the four transistors are at the same temperature — which is why translinear circuits are built on one die and essentially never from discretes.

The Translinear Principle

In a closed loop of BJT junctions, the sum of VBE voltages must equal zero. Since VBE = VT ln(IC/IS), this leads to:
