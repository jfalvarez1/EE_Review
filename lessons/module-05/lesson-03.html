BJT High-Frequency AC Model

At high frequencies, parasitic capacitances dominate BJT behavior. The hybrid-pi model with capacitors explains bandwidth limits, the Miller effect, and the transition frequency (fT).

Transition Frequency
fT = gm / (2π(Cπ + Cμ))

Art of Electronics Insight: The Miller effect multiplies the small base-collector capacitance Cμ (typically 2-8pF) by the voltage gain, often making it the dominant bandwidth limitation.

fT vs IC Characteristic

Transition frequency varies with collector current. It peaks at a "sweet spot" and drops at both low and high currents.

Key Observation: fT peaks around 10-50mA for most small-signal BJTs, then drops due to high-level injection effects. At low currents, Cπ dominates; at high currents, transit time limits increase.

Design Strategies for High Bandwidth

Strategy Effect on Bandwidth Trade-off
Reduce RS (source impedance) Higher f-3dB ∝ 1/RS May need buffer stage
Use cascode configuration Eliminates Miller effect Reduced output swing
Emitter degeneration Reduces effective gain, reduces Miller Lower overall gain
Choose high-fT transistor Lower Cπ and Cμ Often more expensive
Operate at optimal IC Maximize fT Power consumption trade-off
