MOSFET Paralleling Techniques

When a single MOSFET cannot handle the required current, paralleling multiple devices is necessary. However, paralleling MOSFETs introduces challenges with current sharing, thermal runaway prevention, and gate drive design that must be carefully addressed.

Why Parallel MOSFETs?

  • Higher current capacity: Distribute current across multiple devices
  • Lower Rds(on): N devices in parallel → Rds(on)/N total
  • Thermal spreading: Distribute heat across larger area
  • Redundancy: System survives single device failure (with derating)

Static Current Sharing

During DC operation, current sharing depends on Rds(on) matching between devices. The MOSFET with lowest Rds(on) carries the most current.

Static Current Sharing Analysis

For two MOSFETs with Rds1 and Rds2:

I₁ = I_total × Rds2 / (Rds1 + Rds2)

I₂ = I_total × Rds1 / (Rds1 + Rds2)

A 20% Rds(on) mismatch causes ~10% current imbalance. Thermal effects can worsen or improve this.

Thermal Coupling and Stability

MOSFETs have a positive temperature coefficient for Rds(on), which provides natural current balancing - the hotter device increases resistance, pushing current to cooler devices.

Dynamic Current Sharing

During switching transitions, current sharing depends on matching of gate charge, threshold voltage, and gate drive impedance. Poor dynamic sharing causes one device to carry all switching losses.

Dynamic Mismatch Causes

  • Vth variation: ±0.5V typical - lower Vth turns on first
  • Gate resistance mismatch: Different Rg = different dV/dt
  • Layout asymmetry: Different trace lengths to gates
  • Qg variation: Device with less Qg switches faster

Kelvin Source Connection

The gate drive return should connect to the MOSFET source at the package, not through the high-current power path. This "Kelvin" or "star" connection prevents di/dt-induced voltage drops from affecting gate drive.

Derating Guidelines

ParameterSingle DeviceParallel DeratingReason
Current per device 100% 70-80% Account for static imbalance
SOA per device 100% 50-60% Dynamic current hogging
Switching loss ÷ N ÷ (N × 0.7) One device may take extra switching stress
Avalanche energy 100% 50% Cannot guarantee sharing during avalanche

Never Rely on Perfect Sharing

Design assuming worst-case: one device carries 50% more than average during switching transitions. For N devices, design each for I_total/(N×0.7) current capability.
