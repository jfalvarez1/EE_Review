BJT Amplifier Distortion

The BJT's exponential I-V relationship makes it inherently nonlinear. Understanding and controlling distortion is critical for audio, RF, and precision applications.

Fundamental Issue
IC = ISeVBE/VT
2nd Harmonic
HD2 ≈ Vin/(4VT)
3rd Harmonic
HD3 ≈ Vin²/(24VT²)
With RE (degeneration)
THD ↓ by factor (1 + gmRE)
Art of Electronics Insight: A bare CE stage driven with 10mV signal has ~10% second harmonic distortion. Adding RE dramatically reduces this at the cost of gain.

Taylor Series Expansion

Expanding the exponential transfer function reveals the distortion mechanism:

iC = IC[1 + vin/VT + (vin/VT)²/2! + (vin/VT)³/3! + ...]
Term Effect Consequence
Linear (vin/VT) Desired amplification gm = IC/VT
Quadratic (v²) 2nd harmonic, DC shift Even-order distortion
Cubic (v³) 3rd harmonic Odd-order distortion

Distortion Reduction Techniques

Technique How It Works Trade-off
Emitter Degeneration (RE) Reduces effective gm, linearizes transfer Reduces gain by (1 + gmRE)
Negative Feedback Error correction, gain desensitization Requires stable loop, reduces gain
Differential Pair Cancels even-order harmonics Needs matched devices, more current
Class A Biasing Never approaches cutoff Lower efficiency, higher power
Increase IC Higher gm, smaller signal swing relative to VT More power consumption

Differential Pair Cancellation

A differential pair naturally cancels even-order distortion when driven differentially:

Single-Ended
HD2 + HD3 + HD4 + ...
Differential
HD3 + HD5 + HD7 + ...
Why it works: The push-pull nature means Q1's second harmonic adds while Q2's subtracts. With perfect matching, HD2 = 0.
