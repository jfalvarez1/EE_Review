Emitter Follower (Common-Collector)

The emitter follower is a unity-gain buffer with high input impedance and low output impedance. It's essential for impedance matching and driving low-impedance loads.

Key Insight: The output "follows" the input with ~0.6V offset. Voltage gain ≈ 1, but current gain = β. Power gain comes from impedance transformation.

Basic Emitter Follower Circuit

Voltage Gain
Av ≈ RE/(RE + re') ≈ 1
Input Z
Zin = β(re' + RE)
Output Z
Zout ≈ re' + Rs/β
Current Gain
Ai ≈ β

Why Use an Emitter Follower?

High Input Impedance

Doesn't load the source. β multiplies the emitter impedance as seen from the base.

Low Output Impedance

Can drive low-Z loads. Source impedance is divided by β at the emitter.

Current Buffering

Small base current controls large emitter current. Ai ≈ β.

No Phase Inversion

Output is in phase with input (unlike CE amplifier).

Impedance Transformation

The emitter follower transforms impedances by a factor of β:

Looking into the base: Zin = β × (re' + ZE)

Looking into the emitter: Zout = re' + ZS/β

where re' = 26mV/IE (dynamic emitter resistance)

Output Impedance vs Frequency

At low frequencies, Zout is dominated by re' + Rs/β. At high frequencies, base-emitter capacitance increases Zout.

Design Tip: For driving cables or low-impedance loads, ensure Zout << ZL to avoid signal loss and maintain bandwidth.

Advantages over Darlington:

  • Lower VCE(sat)

Trade-offs:
  • Requires complementary transistors

Bootstrap Circuit - Maximum Input Impedance

Bootstrapping the bias resistor dramatically increases effective input impedance:

Without bootstrap: Zin = R1||R2||β(re'+RE)

With bootstrap: Zin ≈ β(re'+RE) (bias resistors "disappear")

Bootstrap factor: 1/(1-Av) → very large since Av ≈ 1

How it works: The capacitor couples the output to the junction of R1/R2. Since Vout follows Vin, there's almost no AC voltage across the lower bias resistor, making it appear as infinite impedance.

Applications

Voltage Buffer

Isolates high-impedance sources from low-impedance loads. Essential between op-amp and power stage.

Cable Driver

Low output impedance drives 50Ω or 75Ω coax cables without reflections.

Level Shifter

Shifts DC level by VBE. Useful for biasing subsequent stages.

Current Amplifier

Multiplies source current by β. Drives LEDs, relays, motors.
