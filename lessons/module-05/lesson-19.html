Thermal Management for Power MOSFETs

Power dissipation in MOSFETs creates heat that must be removed to prevent thermal runaway and device failure. Proper thermal design ensures reliable operation across all operating conditions. This lesson covers the thermal analysis tools every power designer needs.

Why Thermal Design Matters

  • Reliability: Every 10°C rise halves device lifetime
  • Performance: Rds(on) increases ~50% from 25°C to 125°C
  • Safety: Thermal runaway can cause fires
  • System design: Thermal limits often set power rating

Thermal Resistance Model

Heat flow is analogous to electrical current flow. Thermal resistance (Rth) relates power dissipation to temperature rise, just as electrical resistance relates current to voltage drop.

Rθja vs Rθjc

Rθja (junction-to-ambient) is for devices without heatsinks, cooling only through package and PCB.

Rθjc (junction-to-case) is used when a heatsink is attached. Total thermal resistance = Rθjc + Rθcs + Rθsa.

PCB Thermal Design

For surface-mount devices without heatsinks, the PCB is the primary thermal path. Copper area and thickness dramatically affect thermal resistance.

PCB Thermal Design Rules

  • Copper area: Larger pour = lower Rθ. Use 2oz copper if possible.
  • Thermal vias: 0.3mm dia, 1mm pitch typical. Filled or tented preferred.
  • Inner layers: Connect to ground planes for heat spreading.
  • Thick copper: 2oz = ~2× better than 1oz for thermal.

Transient Thermal Response

For pulsed operation, thermal mass delays temperature rise. Transient thermal impedance (Zth) is lower than steady-state Rth for short pulses.
