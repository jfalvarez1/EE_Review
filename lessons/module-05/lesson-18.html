Multistage Amplifier Design

Real-world amplifiers often require gains of 1000× or more, which is impractical with a single stage. Multistage designs cascade amplifiers to achieve high gain while managing bandwidth, stability, and DC levels.

Key Principle
Atotal = A1 × A2 × ... × An
Challenge
DC level shifting between stages
Industry Use: Audio preamps, instrumentation amplifiers, RF IF stages, baseband signal chains.

Coupling Methods

Method Description Pros Cons
RC Coupling Capacitor blocks DC Simple, isolates bias Low-freq rolloff, large caps
Direct Coupling No capacitors DC response, compact Complex DC level management
Transformer Magnetic coupling Impedance matching, isolation Bulky, bandwidth limited

Bandwidth Considerations

Each stage reduces overall bandwidth:

N identical stages
BWtotal ≈ BWstage × √(2^(1/N) - 1)
2 stages
BWtotal ≈ 0.64 × BWstage
3 stages
BWtotal ≈ 0.51 × BWstage
Design Trade-off: Higher gain per stage → fewer stages → better bandwidth, BUT more distortion and DC drift. Optimize for your application.

Practical Design Tips

  • Stage 1: Low noise (low IC), moderate gain
  • Middle stages: Higher gain per stage
  • Output stage: Low output impedance (emitter follower), current capability
  • DC feedback: Use overall DC feedback to stabilize operating points
  • Decoupling: Critical between stages to prevent oscillation
