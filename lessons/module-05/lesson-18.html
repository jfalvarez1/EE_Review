Avalanche Energy and MOSFET Ruggedness

When a MOSFET's drain-source voltage exceeds its breakdown rating, it enters avalanche. Unlike older devices that failed catastrophically, modern avalanche-rated MOSFETs can safely absorb limited energy pulses. Understanding avalanche capability is critical for inductive load switching and fault protection.

What is Avalanche?

Avalanche breakdown occurs when VDS exceeds BVdss. High electric field accelerates carriers which ionize silicon atoms, creating an electron-hole avalanche current. The MOSFET clamps voltage while dissipating energy.

  • EAS: Single-pulse Avalanche Energy - max energy for one pulse
  • EAR: Repetitive Avalanche Energy - max energy for repeated pulses
  • IAR: Repetitive Avalanche Current - max current during avalanche

Unclamped Inductive Switching (UIS) Test

The industry-standard test for avalanche ruggedness uses an inductive load with no freewheeling diode, forcing all stored energy through the MOSFET.

Avalanche Energy Calculation

Energy Stored in Inductor

E = ½ × L × I²

When the MOSFET turns off, this energy must go somewhere. Without a clamp diode, the voltage rises until the MOSFET avalanches.

Single-Pulse Avalanche Energy

EAS = ½ × L × I² × (BVdss / (BVdss - VDD))

The correction factor accounts for continued energy delivery from VDD during avalanche.

Avalanche Rating Derating

Datasheet EAS ratings are at specific conditions (usually 25°C junction). Real applications require significant derating.

Design Guidelines for Avalanche Protection

StrategyDescriptionWhen to Use
Voltage Margin Use 80V MOSFET for 48V bus Always - first line of defense
Clamp Diode Add TVS or Zener across drain-source Inductive loads, known energy
Snubber Circuit RC snubber to limit dV/dt High di/dt switching
Active Clamp Zener from drain to gate Controlled avalanche mode
Freewheeling Diode Parallel diode for inductor current Motor drives, solenoids

Repetitive vs Single-Pulse Avalanche

Single-Pulse (EAS)

  • Maximum energy for one isolated event
  • Device starts at room temperature
  • Used for fault condition analysis
  • Typical values: 100mJ to 1J+

Repetitive (EAR)

  • Maximum energy per pulse at switching frequency
  • Accounts for accumulated thermal stress
  • Typically 10-20% of EAS
  • Used for normal operation analysis

Critical Design Rule

Never design to intentionally operate in avalanche during normal operation. Avalanche ratings are for transient fault tolerance, not continuous operation. If regular avalanche is expected, add external clamping.
