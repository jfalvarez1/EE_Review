High-Current BJT Effects

At high collector currents, BJTs exhibit non-ideal behavior: the Kirk effect (base widening), β droop, quasi-saturation, and fT degradation. These effects limit power transistor performance.

Kirk Effect
Base widens into collector
β Droop
β drops above IC,peak
High-Level Injection
n → 2 in I-V curve
fT Falloff
fT ∝ 1/IC at high I
Art of Electronics X Chapters: "The Kirk effect causes the effective base width to increase at high currents, reducing β and fT. This is why power BJTs specify a 'peak fT' collector current."

High-Current Mechanisms

Effect Physical Cause Observable Result
Kirk Effect Collector space charge neutralized by carriers Base widening, reduced β
High-Level Injection Minority carrier density approaches majority n-factor increases from 1 to 2
Current Crowding Lateral base resistance causes non-uniform IE Hot spots, reduced SOA
Quasi-Saturation Collector resistance limits IC even with VBE high Soft saturation knee
Emitter Crowding Current flows at emitter periphery, not center Reduced effective area
