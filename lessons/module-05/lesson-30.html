BJT vs MOSFET Trade-offs

BJTs and MOSFETs each have distinct advantages. BJTs offer higher gm per current and better matching, while MOSFETs provide infinite input impedance and simpler biasing. Understanding when to use each is essential for optimal circuit design.

BJT Strength
gm = IC/VT (40mS/mA)
MOSFET Strength

BJT Advantage
Lower 1/f noise, better matching
MOSFET Advantage
No gate current, simpler switching

Application Selection Guide

Application Preferred Reason
Low-noise amplifier BJT Lower 1/f noise, higher gm
High-impedance sensor MOSFET Infinite input impedance
Power switching MOSFET Lower drive power, no storage time
Precision current mirror BJT Better matching (VBE ~1mV)
RF power amplifier Both BJT for linearity, LDMOS for power
Analog switch MOSFET Bidirectional, low Ron
Voltage reference BJT Predictable VBE temperature behavior

Key Trade-offs Summary

Input Impedance
BJT: rπ = β/gm | MOS: ∞ (DC)
Noise
BJT: Lower 1/f | MOS: Lower shot noise
Matching
BJT: ~1mV ΔVBE | MOS: ~5-20mV ΔVTH
Switching
BJT: Storage time | MOS: No minority carriers
