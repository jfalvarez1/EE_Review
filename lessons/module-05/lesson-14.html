BJT Frequency Response

Understanding how BJT amplifier gain varies with frequency is critical for audio, RF, and high-speed applications. We'll explore the parasitic capacitances that limit bandwidth and the famous Miller effect.

Key Insight: At low frequencies, coupling/bypass capacitors limit response. At high frequencies, internal BJT capacitances (Cπ and Cμ) roll off the gain. The Miller effect can multiply Cμ by the voltage gain!

Internal Capacitances

Two capacitances limit high-frequency response:

Cπ (Base-Emitter)

Dominates at high currents. Two components:

  • Junction capacitance Cje (constant)
  • Diffusion capacitance Cd = gm × τF

Cπ = Cje + gm × τF

Typical: 5-50pF

Cμ (Collector-Base)

Smaller but more troublesome due to Miller effect:

  • Junction capacitance only
  • Decreases with reverse bias (VCB)

Cμ ∝ 1/√(VCB + φ)

Typical: 0.5-5pF

The Miller Effect

A capacitor between input and output of an inverting amplifier appears multiplied at the input:

Miller Effect: Cin(Miller) = Cμ × (1 + |Av|)

For a CE amplifier with Av = -100 and Cμ = 2pF:

Cin(Miller) = 2pF × (1 + 100) = 202pF!

Why it matters: This effective input capacitance forms an RC low-pass filter with the source resistance, severely limiting bandwidth. This is why cascode circuits (which eliminate Miller effect) achieve much higher bandwidth.

Transition Frequency (fT)

fT is the frequency where the short-circuit current gain (h21) drops to 1:

fT = gm / (2π × (Cπ + Cμ))

At frequencies below fT: |β| ≈ fT / f

Typical fT: 100MHz - 10GHz depending on transistor

Low Frequency Limits

Coupling and bypass capacitors create high-pass behavior:

Input Coupling (Cin)

fL1 = 1/(2π × Cin × Rin)

Rin = Rs + Zin(amp)

Output Coupling (Cout)

fL2 = 1/(2π × Cout × Rout)

Rout = RC || RL

Emitter Bypass (CE)

fL3 = 1/(2π × CE × Re)

Re = RE || (re' + Rs/β)

Dominant pole: fL ≈ max(fL1, fL2, fL3)

For audio (20Hz-20kHz), ensure fL < 20Hz

High Frequency Limits

Internal capacitances create low-pass behavior:

Input pole (dominant in CE):

fH ≈ 1 / (2π × Rs' × Cin_total)

where Cin_total = Cπ + Cμ(1 + gm×RC)

Output pole:

fHo ≈ 1 / (2π × RC × Cμ)

What Limits Bandwidth?
  • High source impedance Rs
  • High voltage gain (Miller)
  • Large Cμ (device parameter)
  • Low fT transistor
How to Increase Bandwidth
  • Lower source impedance
  • Reduce gain (emitter degeneration)
  • Use cascode (eliminates Miller)
  • Choose high fT transistor

Effect of Emitter Degeneration

Adding an unbypassed emitter resistor reduces gain but increases bandwidth:

Without RE: Av = gm × RC, BW limited by Miller

With RE: Av = RC / (re' + RE), Miller effect reduced

Net effect: Gain × Bandwidth may actually increase!
