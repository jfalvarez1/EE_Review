Precision BJT Matched Pairs

Matched transistor pairs are essential for precision analog circuits. They share the same silicon die, ensuring matched IS, β, and thermal tracking. Applications include differential pairs, current mirrors, and voltage references where VBE matching to sub-millivolt levels is required.

The circuit

A differential pair is the circuit that cares most about matching, because its whole job is to amplify the difference between two inputs while ignoring what they have in common. Any mismatch between the two halves turns some of the common-mode into differential, and the amplifier cannot tell that apart from real signal.

The input offset voltage is \(V_{OS} = V_T\ln(I_{S2}/I_{S1})\), so it depends on the ratio of the two saturation currents — which is set by emitter area. A 1% area mismatch gives \(26\,\mathrm{mV}\times\ln(1.01) = 259\,\mathrm{\mu V}\) of offset. Two discrete transistors from the same tape typically differ by far more than 1%, which is why matched pairs exist as parts: adjacent devices on one die, at one temperature, sharing one thermal path.
