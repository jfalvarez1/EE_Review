Wide Bandgap Semiconductors: GaN and SiC

Silicon MOSFETs have dominated power electronics for decades, but wide bandgap (WBG) semiconductors are revolutionizing high-efficiency, high-frequency, and high-temperature applications. Understanding when to use GaN or SiC versus silicon is essential for modern power design.

Higher bandgap → higher breakdown voltage for same thickness → thinner, lower resistance devices.

Material Property Comparison

GaN HEMT Structure

GaN power devices use a High Electron Mobility Transistor (HEMT) structure with a 2D electron gas (2DEG) channel, fundamentally different from vertical silicon MOSFETs.

GaN Device Types

  • Depletion-mode (D-mode): Normally ON - 2DEG exists at Vgs=0
  • Enhancement-mode (E-mode): Normally OFF - requires positive Vgs
  • Cascode: D-mode GaN + Si MOSFET for E-mode behavior with standard gate drive

SiC MOSFET Structure

Unlike lateral GaN, SiC power devices use a vertical structure similar to silicon MOSFETs, but with 4H-SiC crystal providing superior breakdown characteristics.

Critical GaN Gate Voltage

GaN HEMTs have a max Vgs of typically 6-7V. Exceeding this causes permanent gate damage. Use dedicated GaN drivers with precise voltage regulation.

Thermal Considerations

SiC Advantages

  • Higher thermal conductivity (3x Si) - better heat spreading
  • Junction temperatures to 175°C standard, 200°C+ available
  • Reduced thermal derating at high temperatures
  • Excellent for high-power, high-ambient applications

GaN Challenges

  • Lateral structure concentrates heat near surface
  • GaN-on-Si has lower thermal conductivity than SiC substrate
  • Smaller die sizes = higher thermal resistance
  • Requires careful thermal design despite lower losses

Cost and Supply Chain

While WBG devices cost more per device, total system cost can be lower due to smaller magnetics, heat sinks, and higher power density.

Practical Considerations

GaN Layout Rules

  • Minimize gate loop inductance (< 1nH target)
  • Use integrated gate drivers when possible
  • 4-layer PCB minimum for proper decoupling
  • Kelvin source connection for gate drive
  • Avoid parallel GaN devices (current sharing issues)

SiC Layout Rules

  • Negative gate bias strongly recommended
  • Gate resistor sizing for dV/dt control
  • Consider body diode reverse recovery
  • Higher gate drive power required
  • Parallel operation possible with derating
