DC Biasing Techniques

Proper DC biasing establishes the BJT's operating point (Q-point), determining gain, linearity, and temperature stability. Poor biasing leads to distortion, thermal runaway, or cutoff.

Goal
Set stable IC and VCE for linear operation
Challenge
β varies 3:1, VBE drifts -2.1mV/°C
Design Philosophy: Good bias design makes the operating point independent of β. This ensures consistent performance across transistor batches and temperature.

Biasing Methods Comparison

Method β Sensitivity Thermal Stability Use Case
Fixed Bias High (IC ∝ β) Poor (thermal runaway risk) Never use in production
Collector Feedback Medium Moderate Simple LED drivers
Voltage Divider Low (if stiff) Good (with RE) Most applications
Emitter Bias (2-supply) Very Low Excellent Precision amplifiers

Fixed Bias (Why It Fails)

Analysis

IB
(VCC - VBE) / RB
IC
β × IB
Problem: IC directly depends on β! If β = 100 → IC = 1mA, but if β = 300 → IC = 3mA. The transistor may saturate.

Additionally, as temperature rises:

  • VBE drops → IB increases
  • IC increases → junction heats more
  • Positive feedback → thermal runaway

Voltage Divider Bias (Recommended)

Design Procedure

  1. Choose VE ≈ 1-2V for thermal stability
  2. Calculate RE = VE / IC
  3. Calculate VB = VE + VBE ≈ VE + 0.7V
  4. Choose divider current ID ≈ 10×IB (stiff)
  5. Calculate R2 = VB / ID
  6. Calculate R1 = (VCC - VB) / ID
  7. Calculate RC from VCE target
Stiffness Rule: The divider is "stiff" when Idivider >> IB. This makes VB independent of β.
Thévenin VB
VTH = VCC × R2/(R1+R2)
Thévenin RTH
RTH = R1 || R2
IC (approximate)
IC ≈ (VTH - VBE) / RE
Stiffness Check
RTH < 0.1 × β × RE

Thermal Stability Factor (S)

The stability factor S = ∂IC/∂ICO measures sensitivity to leakage current (and temperature). Lower S is better.

Fixed Bias
S = β + 1 ≈ β (worst)
Collector Feedback
S = (β+1)/(1 + β×RC/RB)
Voltage Divider
S = (β+1) × (1 + RTH/RE) / (β+1 + RTH/RE)
Emitter Bias
S ≈ 1 (best)
Design Target: S < 10 for good thermal stability. With voltage divider bias and RE >> RTH/β, S approaches 1.
