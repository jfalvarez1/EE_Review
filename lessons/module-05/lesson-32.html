BJT Bootstrapping Techniques

Bootstrapping uses a capacitor to maintain constant current through a resistor as the signal swings, effectively making the resistor appear much larger. This increases input impedance, extends voltage swing, and improves linearity in BJT circuits.

Principle
Capacitor follows signal, keeps VR constant
Effect
R appears multiplied by (1-A) factor
Common Use
Emitter follower input impedance boost
Low Frequency
Limited by bootstrap capacitor size

The circuit

An emitter follower whose bias divider is fed from its own output through a capacitor. Because the emitter follows the base almost exactly, both ends of RB move together — so the signal voltage across it is nearly zero, and a resistor with no voltage across it draws no current.

Without Cboot the input sees R1 ∥ R2, typically a few tens of kilohms. With it, the apparent resistance of RB is multiplied by \(1/(1-A_v)\), and since an emitter follower's \(A_v\) is about 0.99 that is a factor of roughly 100. The bias network stops loading the input without changing the DC bias at all — which is the whole point, because the DC operating point is exactly what the divider was there to set.
