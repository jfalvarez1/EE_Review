MOSFET Gate Drivers

Power MOSFETs require proper gate driving to switch efficiently. The gate driver must charge and discharge the gate capacitance quickly to minimize switching losses. Understanding gate drive requirements is essential for switch-mode power supplies, motor drives, and any high-power switching application.

Key Challenge
Charge Qg through Rg in minimal time
Switching Loss
Psw ∝ trise × fsw

Gate Charge Characteristics

Parameter Description Typical Range
Qg (total gate charge) Total charge to fully enhance 5-500 nC
Qgs Charge to reach threshold ~20% of Qg
Qgd (Miller charge) Charge during Vds transition ~30-50% of Qg
Vgs(th) Gate threshold voltage 1-4V
Vgs(plateau) Miller plateau voltage Vth + Id/gm

Gate Driver Topologies

Topology Voltage Ref Application
Totem-pole Ground-referenced Low-side switches, simple circuits
Bootstrap Floating (VCC relative to SW) Half-bridge, buck, motor drives
Charge pump Above VCC High-side switches, always-on
Isolated Galvanically isolated High voltage, safety-critical

Gate Resistor Selection

Turn-on time: ton ≈ Rg × Qg / (Vdrv - Vplateau)

Peak gate current: Ig,peak = (Vdrv - Vgs) / Rg
Consideration Lower Rg Higher Rg
Switching speed Faster (lower loss) Slower (higher loss)
dV/dt Higher (EMI concern) Lower (less EMI)
Gate ringing More likely Damped
Driver stress Higher peak current Lower peak current
Separate Turn-on/off: Use a diode in parallel with Rg to have different resistances for turn-on (higher) and turn-off (lower) to optimize switching while limiting dV/dt.

Common Gate Driver Issues

Issue Symptom Solution
Gate ringing Oscillation on Vgs, possible false turn-on Ferrite bead, increase Rg, place driver close
Miller turn-on Low-side turns on during high-side switching Active clamp, lower Rg_off, Cgd snubber
Bootstrap UV lockout High-side doesn't turn on fully Increase Cboot, reduce on-time, check diode Vf
Cross-conduction Shoot-through, excessive current spike Dead-time, negative gate bias
