Delta-Sigma (ΣΔ) Conversion

Delta-Sigma converters trade speed for resolution through oversampling and noise shaping. They achieve 16-24 bit resolution with simple analog circuitry, dominating audio and precision measurement.

OSR (Oversampling Ratio)
OSR = fs / (2 × fBW)
Resolution Improvement
+1.5 bits per octave OSR (1st order)
Key Insight: The magic is noise shaping—the modulator pushes quantization noise to high frequencies where it's filtered out. Higher order modulators push more noise out-of-band for more bits.

ΣΔ Modulator Operation

Order Bits Gained per OSR×2 Stability SNR Improvement
1st Order 1.5 bits Always stable 9 dB/octave
2nd Order 2.5 bits Stable 15 dB/octave
3rd Order 3.5 bits Conditional 21 dB/octave
4th+ Order 4.5+ bits Requires care 27+ dB/octave

Digital Decimation Filter

The modulator output is a high-rate 1-bit stream that must be filtered and decimated:

Sinc Filter
Simple moving average, multiple stages
FIR Filter
Better stopband, more complex
Latency: ΣΔ converters have significant latency due to the decimation filter. This matters for closed-loop control—consider SAR ADCs when latency is critical.
