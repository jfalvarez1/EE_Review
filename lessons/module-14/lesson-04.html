Boost Converter Design

Boost converters step up voltage from a lower input to a higher output. Learn the design equations, component selection, and practical considerations for efficient boost converter design.

Topology
Step-up (Vout > Vin)
Duty Cycle
D = 1 - Vin/Vout
Key Limit
High D = low efficiency
Typical Use
Battery → 5V/12V rails
Design Rule: Keep boost ratio under 4:1 for good efficiency. Higher ratios suffer from extreme duty cycles, high switch current, and increased losses. For higher ratios, consider cascaded stages or flyback topology.

Design Equations

Boost Converter Equations (CCM):

Duty Cycle:
    D = 1 - (Vin / Vout)
    D = 1 - (η × Vin / Vout)  ; Including efficiency

Inductance:
    L = (Vin × D) / (fsw × ΔIL)
    ΔIL = ripple_ratio × IL_avg
    IL_avg = Iout / (1 - D)

Peak Inductor Current:
    IL_pk = IL_avg + ΔIL/2
    IL_pk = Iout/(1-D) + (Vin×D)/(2×L×fsw)

Peak Switch Current:
    Isw_pk = IL_pk (switch sees full inductor current)

Output Capacitor:
    Cout = (Iout × D) / (fsw × ΔVout)
    ESR ripple: ΔV_ESR = IL_pk × ESR

Input Power:
    Pin = Vout × Iout / η
    Iin = Pin / Vin = (Vout × Iout) / (η × Vin)

Boost Converter Schematic

Power-stage template: the in, sw, out, and pwm labels match the build table below. Q1 is the physical switch; the table's Xsw is its ideal controlled-switch model. Vpwm represents the gate drive, not a complete regulator. Hardware also needs the controller's specified input bypassing, feedback, protection, and gate-drive circuit. TI SLVA372D, Fig. 1-1 and §6.

Q1 on · store energyCurrent flows from Vin through L1 and Q1 to ground. In normal boost operation D1 blocks, and Cout supplies the load.
Q1 off · deliver energyL1 current flows through D1 into Cout and the load. The input source continues to supply energy during this interval.
What to measureProbe sw and out relative to ground. In CCM, inductor current stays above zero. Diode current is pulsed; Cout smooths the current delivered to the load.

For ideal steady-state CCM, \(V_{out}=V_{in}/(1-D)\) with \(0<D<1\). This is not an “always” condition during startup, shutdown, or faults. While Q1 is off and D1 conducts, Q1's drain is approximately Vout plus the diode drop; allow for switching overshoot when selecting its rating. See TI SLVA061, §2 and SNVA731, §5.
