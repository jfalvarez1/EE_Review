Charge Pumps

Charge pumps generate voltages higher or lower than the supply using switched capacitors—no inductors required. They're essential for LCD bias, flash memory programming, and generating gate drive voltages in low-voltage systems.

Voltage Multiplier
Doubler, tripler, inverter
Switching Frequency
Typically 100kHz - 1MHz
Output Impedance
Rout ≈ 1/(fsw·C)
Efficiency
Typically 80-90%
Key Insight: Charge pump output impedance is inversely proportional to frequency and capacitance. Higher frequency means lower ripple but more switching losses. The sweet spot depends on load current and acceptable ripple.

Design Equations

Output Impedance (doubler): Rout = 1 / (fsw × Cfly)

Voltage Drop: ΔV = Iload × Rout + 2×Vsw

Ripple Voltage: Vripple = Iload / (fsw × Cout)

Efficiency: η = Vout / (N × Vin)

Dickson (N stages): Vout = (N+1) × Vin - N × VF - I × Rout
