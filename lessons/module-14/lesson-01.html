Decoupling & Bypass Strategy

Decoupling supplies local transient current and limits noise coupling through a power distribution network. Nominal capacitance is only one part of the impedance: ESR, mounted inductance, bias, temperature and the route to the load matter.

No universal three-value rule: one repeated value can work well; mixed values can also be appropriate. Different resonances can create an antiresonant peak. Judge the complete network against the rail's requirements while preserving the IC manufacturer's capacitor requirements.

Below its series resonance, a simple capacitor branch is capacitive; above it, inductive. A smaller capacitor moves that resonance upward but does not necessarily improve the parallel bank. Bogatin, Smith and Sandler: The Myth of Three Capacitor Values.

Placement and verification

Minimize the complete power/return loop, not only the distance to a power pin. Same-side placement often helps for accessible pins; a well-designed underside connection can be better for interior BGA balls. Inspect shared necks, via pairs and reference planes. Neither a tee nor routing through a capacitor pad is categorically best.

Use manufacturer effective-capacitance and impedance data at operating conditions. Include regulator, interconnect and load package models; validate at the intended load port with a documented fixture and load-step edge rate. Continue with PMIC/BGA routing and regulator/feedback placement.
