Power Factor Correction (PFC)

AC-DC power supplies that draw non-sinusoidal current create harmonic distortion that pollutes the power grid. Power Factor Correction shapes input current to match the voltage waveform, reducing harmonics and improving efficiency.

What is Power Factor?

PF = Real Power (W) / Apparent Power (VA)

Perfect PF = 1.0: Current is sinusoidal and in phase with voltage.

Poor PF (0.5-0.7): Typical for rectifier + capacitor input.

Why PFC Matters

  • Regulations: IEC 61000-3-2 limits harmonic current
  • Efficiency: Lower RMS current = less I²R loss
  • Utility costs: Industrial users pay for VA, not just W
  • Grid stability: Harmonics cause transformer heating

The Problem: Capacitor Input Filter

Active PFC Solution

A boost converter running in CCM shapes input current to follow the rectified voltage waveform, achieving near-unity power factor.

Component Stress in PFC

Boost Diode

  • Must block Vout (400V typically)
  • Fast recovery essential (< 35ns)
  • SiC Schottky preferred for efficiency
  • IF(avg) = Pout / Vout

Boost MOSFET

  • VDS > Vout + margin (500-650V)
  • Hard-switched - losses significant
  • Superjunction or SiC recommended
  • Irms ≈ Pin / (Vin,min × √2)

Boost Inductor

  • Handles full line frequency + fsw ripple
  • Core losses significant due to AC flux
  • Typical values: 200µH to 1mH
  • Powder core or gapped ferrite

Bulk Capacitor

  • Stores energy for 100/120Hz ripple
  • Sees full Vout (400V electrolytic)
  • Ripple current = Pout/(2×Vout×ΔV) × Vout
  • Life limiting component

Interleaved PFC

For high power (>1kW), interleaving multiple PFC phases reduces ripple and spreads thermal load.
