I2C Physical Layer & Analog Challenges

I2C is a simple two-wire protocol with subtle analog challenges. Understanding the physical layer—pull-ups, capacitance, rise times, and noise—is critical for reliable communication.

Wired-AND for multi-master
Key Insight: I2C's open-drain architecture means the bus is pulled high by resistors and pulled low actively by devices. This allows multiple devices to share the bus, but the RC time constant of pull-ups and bus capacitance limits speed.

I2C Speed Modes

Mode Max Clock Max Rise Time Max Cbus
Standard 100 kHz 1000 ns 400 pF
Fast 400 kHz 300 ns 400 pF
Fast+ 1 MHz 120 ns 550 pF
High-Speed 3.4 MHz 40 ns 100 pF
Rise Time Formula: tr(0.3VCC to 0.7VCC) ≈ 0.8473 × R × C. For Fast mode with 400pF: Rmax = 300ns / (0.8473 × 400pF) = 885Ω. But minimum R depends on sink current capability (typically 3mA → Rmin = VCC/3mA ≈ 1.1kΩ at 3.3V).

Common I2C Problems

Slow Rise Time
Lower pull-up R or reduce Cbus
Bus Lockup
Add clock recovery logic
Noise Glitches
Add input filtering, lower R
Level Mismatch
Use level translator IC
