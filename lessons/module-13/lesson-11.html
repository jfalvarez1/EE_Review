Thermal Design for Power Converters

Power electronics generate significant heat that must be managed to ensure reliability and prevent thermal runaway. Understanding thermal resistance networks and proper heatsink selection is critical for robust designs.

Heat Sources

  • Switching losses: ½ × V × I × (tr + tf) × fsw
  • Conduction losses: I²RDS(on) for MOSFETs
  • Gate drive: Qg × Vgate × fsw
  • Inductor core + copper

Thermal Limits

  • Tj,max: 150°C (Si), 175°C (SiC)
  • Derating: Target Tj < 125°C
  • Capacitors: 85-105°C ambient
  • PCB: Tg limits (130-170°C)

Thermal Interface Materials (TIM)

The case-to-sink interface requires careful attention. Air gaps are excellent thermal insulators - TIMs fill voids and reduce Rθcs.

TIM Type Thermal Conductivity Typical Rθcs Pros/Cons
Thermal grease (silicone) 0.5 - 4 W/m·K 0.2 - 0.5 °C/W + Low cost, fills gaps well. - Pump-out over time
Phase-change material 0.7 - 3 W/m·K 0.1 - 0.3 °C/W + Clean, reliable. - Needs initial heat cycle
Thermal pad (elastomer) 1 - 6 W/m·K 0.3 - 1 °C/W + Easy handling, electrical isolation. - Thicker = higher R
Metal foil (indium, etc.) 80+ W/m·K 0.02 - 0.1 °C/W + Excellent performance. - High cost, conductive
Graphite sheet 5 - 15 W/m·K (in-plane) 0.1 - 0.3 °C/W + Reusable, no pump-out. - Anisotropic

PCB Thermal Management

For surface-mount devices, the PCB itself is the primary heat spreader. Proper copper area and via design are essential.

Technique Thermal Improvement Notes
Heavy copper (2oz → 4oz) ~30% lower Rθ Higher cost, affects trace widths
Thermal via array 50-70% lower Rθ Fill with solder or copper for best results
Inner copper planes 40-60% lower Rθ Heat spreading to larger area
Aluminum or copper core PCB 80-90% lower Rθ Used for high-power LED drivers
Bottom-side heatsink Variable Requires thermal vias to conduct heat

Transient Thermal Analysis

For pulsed loads, thermal capacitance provides short-term energy storage. Junction temperature doesn't respond instantly.

Transient Thermal Impedance: Datasheets provide Zth curves showing how thermal impedance varies with pulse duration. For single pulses:

  • t << τ: Zth ≈ √(t/τ) × Rth (thermal capacitance dominant)
  • t >> τ: Zth ≈ Rth (steady-state)
