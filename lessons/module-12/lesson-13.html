Op-Amp Stability & Compensation

Op-amps can oscillate when phase shift reaches 180° at frequencies where gain > 1. Understanding stability requires analyzing the loop gain.

Key Insight: Capacitive loads add a pole in the feedback loop. A 100pF load on a typical op-amp can reduce phase margin from 60° to 20°, causing ringing or oscillation.

Compensation Techniques

Output isolation resistor

Feedback senses amp_out before Riso. Check the load-side voltage drop and response.

Feedback capacitor with input capacitance

Cf reshapes noise gain; select it from the loop response and verify stability. Physical supplies are omitted in these signal-path templates. See Stability and Compensation for the equations and primary references.

Stability Rules of Thumb

Condition Typical Phase Margin Response
PM > 70° Well damped No overshoot
PM 45-70° Acceptable Some overshoot (5-20%)
PM 30-45° Marginal Significant ringing
PM < 30° Unstable Oscillation likely
Hidden Capacitive Loads:
  • Long PCB traces: ~1pF/cm
  • Coax cables: 30-100pF/m
  • Scope probes: 10-15pF
  • ADC input: 5-20pF
