Synchronous Rectification

Replacing diodes with actively-controlled MOSFETs dramatically reduces power loss in switching converters. This technique is essential for high-efficiency power supplies, especially at low output voltages where diode drops dominate.

The Problem with Diodes

Schottky diode forward voltage: 0.3-0.5V

At 3.3V output, 10A load:

  • Diode loss: 0.4V × 10A × 50% = 2W
  • Power lost: ~12% of output power!

MOSFET Solution

MOSFET Rds(on): 3mΩ typical

At same 3.3V output, 10A load:

  • MOSFET loss: 10² × 0.003 × 50% = 0.15W
  • 13× reduction in rectifier loss!

Shoot-Through and Dead-Time

Shoot-Through Hazard

If both MOSFETs are ON simultaneously, a direct short from VIN to GND occurs:

  • Current limited only by Rds(on) + wiring resistance
  • At 12V, 3mΩ total: I = 4000A (theoretical)
  • Destroys MOSFETs within microseconds

Dead-Time Solutions

  • Fixed dead-time: Simple, but efficiency tradeoff
  • Adaptive dead-time: Controller monitors VSW transition
  • Predictive: Adjusts based on load current

Most integrated controllers use adaptive dead-time for optimal efficiency.

Gate Driver Requirements

Bootstrap Operation

The bootstrap capacitor charges when Q_LS is ON (VSW = 0V). This stored energy drives the high-side MOSFET gate above VIN.

CBOOT sizing: Qg(HS) / ΔV, where ΔV ≤ 0.3V typical

Bootstrap Limitations

  • Cannot sustain 100% duty cycle (needs LS ON to refresh)
  • Max on-time limited by leakage current
  • Requires minimum off-time each cycle

Diode Emulation Mode

At light loads, continuous conduction mode (CCM) wastes energy. Diode emulation lets the low-side MOSFET turn off when current reverses, improving efficiency.
