EMI/EMC in Power Converters

Switching power converters generate electromagnetic interference (EMI) that can disrupt nearby electronics and violate regulatory standards. Understanding EMI sources and mitigation techniques is essential for passing compliance testing.

EMI Emission Types

  • Conducted: Noise on power lines (150 kHz - 30 MHz)
  • Radiated: Electromagnetic waves (30 MHz - 1 GHz)

Key Standards

  • CISPR 32: Multimedia equipment
  • FCC Part 15: US requirements
  • EN 55032: EU harmonized

Sources of EMI in Switching Converters

EMI originates from high dI/dt and dV/dt switching transitions. The "hot loops" with pulsating currents are the primary sources.

EMI Source Frequency Range Primary Path Mitigation
Switching fundamental 100 kHz - 2 MHz Conducted DM Input LC filter
Switch node ringing 10 MHz - 100 MHz Conducted + Radiated Snubber, gate resistor
Body diode reverse recovery 10 MHz - 50 MHz Conducted DM Schottky, soft recovery diode
Parasitic capacitance coupling 1 MHz - 30 MHz Conducted CM Y-capacitors, shielding

PCB Layout for EMI Reduction

Technique EMI Reduction Implementation Notes
Minimize hot loop area 10-20 dB Place input cap adjacent to switches
Kelvin sense connections 5-10 dB Separate power and signal grounds
Shield switch node 5-15 dB Guard ring or ground pour around SW
Snubber on switch node 10-20 dB at ringing freq RC: 100-1000pF, 1-10Ω typical
Reduce gate drive speed 5-15 dB Larger gate resistor (trades efficiency)
Spread spectrum modulation 5-10 dB FSS feature in controller ICs
