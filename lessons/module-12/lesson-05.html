Buck-Boost and SEPIC Converters

When input voltage can be above or below the desired output voltage, traditional buck or boost topologies fall short. Buck-boost and SEPIC converters handle this "overlapping" input range, essential for battery-powered applications where voltage varies widely.

When You Need Buck-Boost

  • Li-ion battery: 4.2V full → 3.0V empty, output = 3.3V
  • Automotive: 6V cold crank → 16V load dump
  • USB-C: 5V-20V input, 12V output

Inverting Buck-Boost

The simplest topology - uses single inductor but produces negative output voltage.

Non-Inverting Buck-Boost

Uses two switches to achieve buck or boost operation as needed. Also called "4-switch buck-boost" or cascaded buck-boost.

SEPIC Converter

Single-Ended Primary-Inductor Converter - non-inverting output, single switch, but needs two inductors and a coupling capacitor.

Coupled Inductor SEPIC

Using a coupled inductor (two windings on one core) instead of two separate inductors reduces size, improves efficiency, and can reduce ripple.

Leakage Inductance

Coupled inductors have leakage inductance that stores energy not transferred between windings. This energy causes voltage spikes during switching. Use a snubber or clamp circuit to protect the MOSFET.
