BJT Operating Regions & Practical Effects

Understanding BJT operating regions, saturation voltage, and non-ideal effects is essential for robust circuit design.

Cutoff
VBE < 0.5V, IC ≈ 0
Active
VBE ≈ 0.7V, IC = β×IB
Saturation
VCE < 0.2V, IC limited
Key Insight: In saturation, VCE(sat) is NOT zero - it ranges from 50mV to 500mV depending on IC/IB ratio. This voltage drop causes power dissipation and affects switching circuits.

Non-Ideal Effects

Effect Description Practical Impact
Early Effect IC increases with VCE due to base width modulation Output impedance ≈ VA/IC (finite, not infinite)
β variation β changes with IC, VCE, temperature Use emitter degeneration for stability
Storage Time Delay exiting saturation (excess base charge) Limits switching speed, use Schottky clamp
Thermal Runaway IC increases with temp → more heat → higher IC Use RE for negative feedback
VBE Tempco VBE drops ~2mV/°C Bias point shifts with temperature
Parasitic Capacitances:
  • CBC (Cµ): Base-collector junction, 1-10pF. Causes Miller effect!
  • CBE (Cπ): Base-emitter, 10-100pF. Limits input bandwidth.
  • CCS: Collector-substrate (in ICs), adds to output capacitance.

What to run: a DC sweep of VIN from 0 V to 5 V.

Predict, measure, diagnose

The 100 kΩ base resistor is deliberately weak drive. At VIN = 5 V, a 0.7 V first estimate gives IB = 43 µA; with the course model’s β = 200 that requests 8.6 mA, below the 12 mA load-line intercept. This sweep does not have to reach saturation. Then hold VIN at 5 V and replace RB with 3.3 kΩ to supply about 1.3 mA of base drive. Measure both VBE and VBC: an NPN in saturation has both junctions forward biased.

Bench acceptance: record VIN, V(base) and V(coll), then calculate IB = (VIN − V(base))/RB and IC = (12 − V(coll))/RC. Compare the measured IC/IB with the active-region gain; do not infer saturation from a universal 0.2 V threshold. Verify the actual package pinout before wiring. The tight checks below apply to the declared course model; transistor spread, Early effect, charge storage and temperature are outside it.

Reference: onsemi 2N2222A/D, electrical characteristics and package drawing. Its 0.3 V maximum VCE(sat) is specified at 150 mA collector current and 15 mA base current; it is not a guaranteed voltage at this lab’s 12 mA.

Then change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it. A simulator is only worth the time if you are checking an answer you already have — otherwise it is a very slow way of being surprised.
