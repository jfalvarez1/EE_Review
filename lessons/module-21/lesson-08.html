Power Amplifiers (PA)

Power Amplifiers are the final amplification stage in transmitters, designed to deliver significant RF power to the antenna while maintaining acceptable efficiency and linearity. PA design requires careful tradeoffs between output power, efficiency, and signal fidelity.

Purpose:Amplify RF signal to required transmit power level
Key Metrics:Pout, Efficiency (PAE), Linearity (ACPR, EVM)
Power Range:100 mW (handsets) to 100+ W (base stations)
Technologies:GaAs, GaN, LDMOS, SiGe, CMOS
Efficiency Matters: In battery-powered devices, PA efficiency directly impacts battery life. A 50% efficient PA wastes half the DC power as heat.

Key PA Parameters

Power Metrics

Drain Efficiency: η_D = P_out / P_DC

Power Added Efficiency (PAE): PAE = (P_out - P_in) / P_DC

1 dB Compression (P1dB): Output power where gain drops 1 dB from linear

Saturated Power (Psat): Maximum output power capability

Linearity Metrics

ACPR:Adjacent Channel Power Ratio - power leakage to adjacent channels
EVM:Error Vector Magnitude - constellation distortion
AM-AM:Amplitude distortion (gain compression)
AM-PM:Phase distortion vs. amplitude

PA Architectures and Linearization

Efficiency Enhancement

  • Doherty PA: Uses auxiliary amplifier for peak power, maintains efficiency at backoff
  • Envelope Tracking: Modulates supply voltage to follow signal envelope
  • Outphasing: Combines two constant-envelope PAs with varying phase

Linearization Techniques

  • Digital Pre-Distortion (DPD): Applies inverse nonlinearity before PA
  • Feedforward: Subtracts error signal using auxiliary path
  • Feedback: Uses closed-loop correction (limited bandwidth)
Doherty Advantage: Maintains high efficiency at 6-10 dB backoff, ideal for high PAPR signals like OFDM.

Thermal Considerations

Power amplifiers generate significant heat that must be managed to ensure reliability and prevent thermal runaway.

Power Dissipation: P_diss = P_DC - P_out = P_out × (1/η - 1)

Junction Temperature: Tj = Ta + P_diss × (θ_jc + θ_cs + θ_sa)

θ_jc:Junction-to-case thermal resistance
θ_cs:Case-to-sink thermal resistance
θ_sa:Sink-to-ambient thermal resistance

Example Calculation

For a 10W output PA at 50% efficiency:

  • P_DC = 10W / 0.5 = 20W
  • P_diss = 20W - 10W = 10W
  • With θ_total = 5°C/W, Ta = 40°C: Tj = 40 + 10×5 = 90°C
