MOSFET Parasitics & Gate Drive

Real MOSFETs have significant parasitic capacitances that affect switching speed and losses. Understanding gate charge is essential for driver design.

Key Insight: The Miller plateau in gate charging occurs because CGD must discharge as VDS falls. This plateau voltage equals VGS at the operating current, and its duration determines switching loss.

Gate Drive Requirements

Parameter Formula Practical Notes
Turn-on time ton ≈ QG / IG Faster with higher gate current
Gate drive power PGD = QG × VGS × fsw Significant at high frequency
Gate resistor RG = (VDRV - Vplateau) / IG Controls dV/dt and EMI
Driver current Ipeak = VDRV / RG Driver must source/sink this
Miller Effect in Practice:
  • During turn-on, gate voltage stalls at Vplateau
  • Insufficient gate drive current extends switching loss
  • During turn-off, VDS rise can couple back through CGD and turn device back on
  • Use low gate resistance and separate turn-on/turn-off paths
