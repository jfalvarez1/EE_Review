Mixers and Frequency Conversion

Mixers are essential nonlinear circuits that translate signals between frequencies. They multiply two signals together to produce sum and difference frequencies, enabling frequency up-conversion (transmitters) and down-conversion (receivers).

Purpose:Translate RF signals to/from intermediate frequency (IF)
Key Ports:RF, LO (Local Oscillator), IF
Operation:f_IF = |f_RF ± f_LO|
Key Metrics:Conversion gain/loss, NF, IIP3, port isolation
Mixing Mathematics: cos(ω₁t) × cos(ω₂t) = ½[cos((ω₁-ω₂)t) + cos((ω₁+ω₂)t)] - this fundamental identity enables frequency translation.

Mixer Fundamentals

Frequency Translation

Down-conversion: f_IF = f_RF - f_LO (high-side injection) or f_IF = f_LO - f_RF (low-side)

Up-conversion: f_RF = f_IF + f_LO

Image Frequency: f_image = f_LO ± f_IF (opposite sideband)

Key Parameters

Conversion Gain:Ratio of IF output to RF input power (active mixers: positive, passive: negative)
Noise Figure:SSB NF typically 6-12 dB for passive, lower for active
IIP3:Third-order intercept point, measures linearity
Port Isolation:LO-RF, LO-IF isolation prevents leakage

Mixer Topologies

1. Single-Balanced Mixer

  • Uses one differential pair driven by LO
  • Provides LO-RF isolation
  • Does not cancel LO-IF feedthrough
  • Even-order distortion not cancelled

2. Double-Balanced Mixer (Gilbert Cell)

  • Two differential pairs in quad configuration
  • Excellent port isolation (LO-RF, LO-IF, RF-IF)
  • Cancels even-order distortion and DC offsets
  • Most common active mixer topology

3. Passive Diode Ring Mixer

  • Four diodes in ring configuration
  • Wide bandwidth, high linearity
  • Conversion loss (typically 6-8 dB)
  • Requires strong LO drive (+7 to +17 dBm)

4. Passive FET Mixer

  • Uses FETs as switches driven by LO
  • Low conversion loss (~2-4 dB)
  • Good linearity, low LO drive required
  • Popular in CMOS implementations

Image Rejection and Filtering

The image frequency problem is fundamental to superheterodyne receivers. Without image rejection, signals at the image frequency will also convert to the same IF, causing interference.

Spurious Response Analysis

Mixers generate unwanted spurious products at frequencies m×f_RF ± n×f_LO. These spurs must be filtered or avoided in frequency planning.

Spur Frequencies: f_spur = |m × f_RF ± n × f_LO|

Order: m + n (odd orders typically stronger)

Common Spurious Products

LO Harmonics:2×LO, 3×LO can mix with RF
Half-IF Spur:f_RF = LO ± IF/2 produces in-band spur
Third-order:2×RF-LO, RF-2×LO products

LO Drive Considerations

  • Soft Switching: Low LO, gradual switching, more linear but less efficient
  • Hard Switching: High LO, abrupt switching, better noise and conversion gain
  • Typical requirement: 0 to +10 dBm for active mixers
