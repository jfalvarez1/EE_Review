Temperature Sensing Interfaces

Temperature is one of the most commonly measured physical quantities. Understanding the analog interface circuits for different sensor types—RTDs, thermistors, and thermocouples—is essential for embedded and industrial design.

RTD (PT100/PT1000) Interface

RTDs use the predictable change in metal resistance with temperature. Platinum RTDs (PT100) have 100Ω at 0°C with ~0.385Ω/°C temperature coefficient (α).

NTC Thermistor Interface

NTC (Negative Temperature Coefficient) thermistors have high sensitivity (~4%/°C) but are highly nonlinear. A 10kΩ NTC might range from 30kΩ at 0°C to 1kΩ at 100°C.

Thermocouple Interface

Thermocouples generate a voltage from the Seebeck effect at the junction of two dissimilar metals. They're self-powered but produce tiny signals (µV) and require cold junction compensation.

Temperature Sensing Design Checklist

Progressive Exercises

Level 1: Fundamentals

Exercise 1: A PT100 RTD reads 119.4Ω. Using the simplified equation R = R₀(1 + αT) with α = 0.00385, what is the temperature?

T = (R/R₀ - 1) / α

Exercise 2: A Type K thermocouple measures 8.2mV. If cold junction is at 25°C, what is the hot junction temperature? (41µV/°C)

ΔT = V_tc / (41µV/°C), then T_hot = ΔT + T_cold

Exercise 3: A 10kΩ NTC thermistor (β=3950) is in a divider with R1=10kΩ and VCC=3.3V. At 25°C, what is the divider output voltage?

At 25°C, NTC = 10kΩ. Vout = VCC × 10k/(10k+10k)

Level 2: Application

Exercise 4: A 2-wire PT100 has 5Ω total lead resistance. What temperature error does this introduce?

Error(Ω) = 2×Rl. Then ΔT = Error / (R₀ × α) = 5 / (100 × 0.00385)

Exercise 5: A PT1000 needs 1mV signal per degree. What excitation current is required?

dR/dT = 1000 × 0.00385 = 3.85Ω/°C. I = dV/dR = 1mV / 3.85Ω
