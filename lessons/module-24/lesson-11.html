Oscilloscope Techniques for Analog Debug

The oscilloscope is your primary tool for analog debugging. Mastering probe compensation, triggering, and measurement techniques is essential.

Probe Ground Lead Effects

Ringing Artifacts: If you see ringing on fast edges that wasn't there before probing, suspect ground lead inductance. Switch to a spring ground or probe directly across component.

What to run: a transient run of 500 ns in steps of 100 ps.

What to watch: V(sig), V(scope), V(gnd_ref).

V(scope) should be a tenth of V(sig) — 9 MΩ against 1 MΩ — with the same shape. It has the same shape only because R C matches on both halves: 9 M × 1.67 pF = 1 M × 15 pF = 15 µs. A 10:1 probe is two dividers, resistive and capacitive, and they only agree at one trimmer setting; the square wave on the front of every oscilloscope exists to let you find it.

Then watch V(gnd_ref), which should be zero and is not. The return current through 100 nH develops a voltage of its own, and the scope subtracts its input from that rather than from your circuit's ground. The ringing you see on a fast edge is frequently the probe's own ground lead resonating with the input capacitance, not anything the circuit is doing — 100 nH with 15 pF rings at 130 MHz, which is exactly the range where people start blaming their layout.

Then change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it. A simulator is only worth the time if you are checking an answer you already have — otherwise it is a very slow way of being surprised.
