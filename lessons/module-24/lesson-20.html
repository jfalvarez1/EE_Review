Thermal Management for Power Electronics

Heat is the enemy of electronics reliability. For every 10°C rise in junction temperature, the failure rate of semiconductors roughly doubles. Proper thermal management is essential for achieving product reliability targets and preventing field failures. This lesson covers the thermal design process from first principles to practical implementation.

Industry Applications

  • Power Supplies - MOSFETs, diodes, and magnetics thermal design
  • Motor Drives - High-current IGBT/MOSFET cooling
  • LED Lighting - LED junction temperature limits lifetime
  • Server/Telecom - High-density compute thermal solutions
  • Automotive Electronics - Wide temperature range operation

1. Thermal Resistance Fundamentals

Heat flow is analogous to electrical current flow. Just as voltage drives current through resistance, temperature difference drives heat through thermal resistance.

Electrical Analogy

  • Voltage (V) → Temperature (T)
  • Current (I) → Heat Power (P)
  • Resistance (R) → Thermal Resistance (Rθ)
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR

Thermal Equivalent

  • Temperature difference (ΔT)
  • Power dissipation (W)
  • Thermal resistance (°C/W)
  • Heat Law: ΔT = P × Rθ

Thermal Resistance Network

Thermal Resistance Definitions

Symbol Name Description Typical Values
RθJC Junction-to-Case Internal to device, set by die attach and package 0.1 - 10 °C/W
RθCS Case-to-Sink Interface material (TIM) between device and heatsink 0.05 - 2 °C/W
RθSA Sink-to-Ambient Heatsink to surrounding air 0.5 - 20 °C/W
RθJA Junction-to-Ambient Total thermal resistance (sum of above) 1 - 150 °C/W
RθJB Junction-to-Board Heat path through leads to PCB 2 - 50 °C/W

RθJA in Datasheets - Use with Caution!

Datasheet RθJA values are measured on a specific test board (JEDEC standards) and assume:

  • Natural convection (no airflow)
  • Specific PCB copper area (often 1 sq. inch)
  • Single component on board

Your actual RθJA will differ! Use RθJC or RθJB and calculate the rest.

2. Junction Temperature Calculation

Junction Temperature Calculator

Thermal Analysis Results

Power Dissipation Sources

MOSFETs

Conduction Loss: \( P_{cond} = I_{RMS}^2 \times R_{DS(on)} \)

Switching Loss: \( P_{sw} = \frac{1}{2} V_{DS} \times I_D \times (t_r + t_f) \times f_{sw} \)

Gate Drive Loss: \( P_{gate} = Q_g \times V_{gs} \times f_{sw} \)

Total: \( P_{total} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_{gate} \)

Diodes

Forward Loss: \( P_F = V_F \times I_{avg} + R_D \times I_{RMS}^2 \)

Reverse Recovery Loss: \( P_{rr} = \frac{1}{2} Q_{rr} \times V_R \times f_{sw} \)

Inductors/Transformers

Copper Loss: \( P_{Cu} = I_{RMS}^2 \times R_{DC} \times F_{AC} \)

Where FAC accounts for skin/proximity effects (1.2-3× at high frequency)

Core Loss: \( P_{core} = k \times f^\alpha \times B^\beta \times V_e \) (Steinmetz equation)

3. Heatsink Selection

Required Heatsink Thermal Resistance

Rearranging the thermal equation to find required RθSA:

\[ R_{\theta SA} = \frac{T_{j,max} - T_a}{P} - R_{\theta JC} - R_{\theta CS} \]

Common Heatsink Types

Type RθSA Range Airflow Application
Stamped aluminum 5 - 30 °C/W Natural convection Low power, cost-sensitive
Extruded aluminum 0.5 - 10 °C/W Natural or forced General purpose power electronics
Bonded fin 0.1 - 2 °C/W Forced air High power density
Skived fin 0.05 - 1 °C/W Forced air High performance servers
Liquid cold plate 0.01 - 0.1 °C/W Liquid cooling Extreme power (kW+)

Effect of Airflow

Heatsink Selection Guidelines

  • Natural convection: Multiply datasheet Rθ by 1.5-2× for real enclosure conditions
  • Forced air: Expect 50-70% reduction in Rθ at 200 LFM (1 m/s)
  • Orientation: Vertical fins perform 10-20% better than horizontal in natural convection
  • Altitude: Add 10% to Rθ per 1000m above sea level (thinner air)

4. Thermal Interface Materials (TIM)

The interface between device and heatsink is critical. Air gaps (even microscopic) dramatically increase thermal resistance.

TIM Type Thermal Conductivity (W/m·K) RθCS (°C/W) typical Application Notes
Thermal grease (silicone) 0.7 - 4 0.05 - 0.5 Best performance, messy, pump-out over time
Thermal pad (silicone) 1 - 6 0.2 - 1.0 Easy to use, gap filling, consistent
Phase change material 3 - 5 0.1 - 0.3 Melts at operating temp, excellent wetting
Graphite sheet 5 - 10 (in-plane) 0.2 - 0.5 Good for spreading, reusable
Insulating pad (silicone + fiberglass) 0.5 - 2 0.5 - 2.0 Electrical isolation required
Bare metal (mica washer) 0.5 1.0 - 3.0 Legacy, electrical isolation

TIM Thermal Resistance

\[ R_{\theta CS} = \frac{t}{k \times A} + R_{contact} \]

Where: t = thickness, k = thermal conductivity, A = contact area, Rcontact = interface resistance

Thinner is better (if gap is filled). But too thin = air voids!

TIM Application Best Practices

  • Clean surfaces with IPA before applying TIM
  • Apply thin, even layer of grease (avoid excess squeeze-out)
  • For pads, choose thickness to match gap (compress 10-25%)
  • Ensure adequate mounting pressure (follow torque specs)
  • Consider thermal aging - some TIMs degrade over years

5. PCB as a Heatsink

For surface-mount devices without heatsinks, the PCB is the primary heat path. Proper layout dramatically affects thermal performance.

Thermal Vias

Thermal vias conduct heat from top copper to inner/bottom layers:

Single Via Thermal Resistance

\[ R_{\theta,via} = \frac{L}{\pi \cdot k_{Cu} \cdot (r_o^2 - r_i^2)} \]

For 0.3mm drill, 25µm plating, 1.6mm PCB: Rθ ≈ 70°C/W per via

Via Array

N vias in parallel: \( R_{\theta,array} = \frac{R_{\theta,via}}{N} \)

Example: 16 vias → 70/16 = 4.4°C/W contribution from vias

Copper Spreading

Spreading Resistance

Heat spreading from small source to larger area adds thermal resistance:

\[ R_{spreading} \approx \frac{1}{2\pi k_{Cu} t} \cdot \ln\left(\frac{A_{spread}}{A_{source}}\right) \]

Larger copper area = lower Rθ, but diminishing returns beyond ~25mm radius

PCB Thermal Design Rules

  • Use 2oz copper on power layers minimum
  • Place thermal vias directly under thermal pad (not offset)
  • Connect bottom copper to large ground plane
  • Keep high-power devices away from board edges (less spreading area)
  • Avoid thermal relief on power pads - use solid connections
  • Consider copper pour on top layer connected to internal plane

6. Derating and Reliability

Temperature Derating Curves

Most components must be derated at high temperatures. Check datasheet for:

  • MOSFETs: RDS(on) increases ~0.4%/°C above 25°C
  • Capacitors: Electrolytic life halves per 10°C above rated temp
  • Current rating: Linear derating from Tcase to Tj,max

Arrhenius Equation for Reliability

Component failure rate acceleration with temperature:

\[ AF = e^{\frac{E_a}{k_B}\left(\frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2}\right)} \]

Where: Ea = activation energy (~0.7eV for semiconductors), kB = Boltzmann constant

Rule of Thumb

Failure rate doubles for every 10°C increase in Tj

Running at 125°C vs 85°C = 16× higher failure rate!

Design for Reliability

  • Target Tj at least 20°C below Tj,max at worst case
  • Include 10°C margin for manufacturing variation
  • Consider ambient temperature range (e.g., 0-50°C or -40 to +85°C)
  • Account for adjacent component heating in dense designs

7. Thermal Measurement

Measurement Methods

Method Accuracy Pros Cons
Thermocouple ±1-2°C Direct contact, wide range Measures surface, not junction
IR Camera ±2-5°C Non-contact, thermal imaging Emissivity calibration needed
Thermochromic paint ±5°C Visual mapping Single threshold, one-time use
VBE / VDS(on) method ±1°C Measures actual Tj Requires calibration curve
On-chip sensor ±3-5°C Real-time monitoring Location may not be hotspot

VDS(on) Temperature Sensing

MOSFET RDS(on) has a known temperature coefficient (~0.4%/°C). By measuring VDS at a known current:

\[ T_j = T_{ref} + \frac{R_{DS(on),meas} - R_{DS(on),ref}}{R_{DS(on),ref} \times \alpha} \]

Where α ≈ 0.004/°C for typical MOSFETs

8. Practical Design Example

Example: 12V/5A Synchronous Buck Converter

Specifications:

  • Input: 12V, Output: 3.3V @ 5A
  • Switching frequency: 500kHz
  • MOSFETs: CSD17579Q5A (high-side), CSD17577Q5A (low-side)
  • Ambient: 50°C max, Tj target: 100°C

Step 1: Calculate Power Losses

High-side MOSFET (HS):
  Duty cycle: D = 3.3/12 = 0.275
  I_HS,RMS = 5A × √0.275 = 2.62A
  RDS(on)@100°C ≈ 10mΩ × 1.3 = 13mΩ
  P_cond,HS = 2.62² × 0.013 = 89mW

  Switching loss (from Qgd):
  P_sw = 0.5 × 12V × 5A × (10ns + 8ns) × 500kHz = 270mW

  Total HS: ~360mW

Low-side MOSFET (LS):
  D_LS = 1 - 0.275 = 0.725
  I_LS,RMS = 5A × √0.725 = 4.26A
  RDS(on)@100°C ≈ 7mΩ × 1.3 = 9.1mΩ
  P_cond,LS = 4.26² × 0.0091 = 165mW

  Body diode loss (dead time):
  P_diode = 0.7V × 5A × 50ns × 500kHz × 2 = 175mW

  Total LS: ~340mW

Step 2: Thermal Analysis

Package: SON 5x6mm, exposed pad
Rθ_JC = 1.1°C/W (from datasheet)
Rθ_JB = 0.6°C/W (from datasheet)

Using PCB as heatsink:
- 16 thermal vias (0.3mm, 25µm plating)
- 2oz copper on bottom, large ground plane
- Estimated Rθ_board = 15°C/W

For high-side MOSFET (360mW):
  Tj = Ta + P × Rθ_JA
  Tj = 50 + 0.36 × (1.1 + 15) = 50 + 5.8 = 55.8°C ✓

For low-side MOSFET (340mW):
  Tj = 50 + 0.34 × (1.1 + 15) = 50 + 5.5 = 55.5°C ✓

Both well under 100°C target!

9. Design Checklist

Power Loss Calculation

  • ☐ Calculate conduction loss for all semiconductors
  • ☐ Calculate switching losses for all switches
  • ☐ Include gate drive losses
  • ☐ Calculate inductor copper and core losses
  • ☐ Use worst-case (high temperature) RDS(on) values

Thermal Design

  • ☐ Identify thermal resistance path (Rθ_JC, Rθ_CS, Rθ_SA)
  • ☐ Calculate required heatsink Rθ_SA
  • ☐ Select heatsink with margin
  • ☐ Select appropriate TIM
  • ☐ Verify Tj < Tj,max - 20°C at worst case ambient

PCB Layout

  • ☐ Thermal vias under exposed pads
  • ☐ Large copper spreading area
  • ☐ Heavy copper (2oz+) on power layers
  • ☐ No thermal relief on power pads

Verification

  • ☐ Thermal simulation (optional but recommended)
  • ☐ Prototype thermal measurement
  • ☐ Verify component derating at operating temperature
