Complete Audio Subsystem Design

Modern laptops and smartphones integrate sophisticated audio subsystems that handle headphone output, speaker amplification, and microphone input. This lesson covers the complete analog signal chain from codec to transducer.

Industry Reference: This design follows practices from Analog Devices CN-0162 and TI TIPD189 reference designs.

Section 1: Headphone Amplifier Design

The headphone amplifier must drive 16-600Ω loads with low distortion and adequate power for loud listening (typically 30-100mW into 32Ω).

Capless vs. Capacitor-Coupled Output

Topology Advantages Disadvantages Use Case
Capless (Ground-Referenced) No large caps, better bass, smaller BOM Needs virtual ground, ground detection circuit Modern smartphones, laptops
AC-Coupled Simple, robust Large caps (100-470µF), roll-off at low freq Legacy designs, simple audio
Ground Sensing Required: Capless outputs use AVDD/2 as virtual ground. If a user connects line-out (true ground), the virtual ground shorts to real ground. AN-1056 describes protection circuits.

Section 2: MEMS Microphone Interface

Modern devices use MEMS (Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems) microphones with either analog or digital (PDM) outputs. PDM has become dominant due to noise immunity.

PDM Clock
1.024-3.072 MHz typical
Audio Bandwidth
fc = CLK / (2 × OSR)
Sensitivity
-26 dBFS typical @ 94dB SPL
SNR
65-70 dB (A-weighted)

PDM Interface Design

Layout Critical: Keep PDM traces short (<2cm) and away from switching power supplies. The high-frequency clock (>1MHz) can radiate EMI. Use 100nF decoupling at each mic, placed within 1mm of VDD pin.

Section 3: Class-D Speaker Amplifier

Class-D amplifiers achieve 85-95% efficiency by using PWM switching, making them essential for battery-powered devices. Modern "filterless" Class-D amps use the speaker inductance as the output filter.

Typical Power
1-5W per channel (mobile)
Efficiency
85-93% at full power
Switching Freq
250kHz - 1.5MHz
THD+N
0.03-0.1% typical

Filterless vs. LC Filter Output

Approach Components EMI Use Case
Filterless None (speaker is filter) Higher - needs shielding Short speaker wires (<10cm)
Ferrite Bead Ferrite + small cap Medium Most portable devices
Full LC Filter 10-22µH + 0.1-1µF per leg Low Long wires, strict EMI
EMI Consideration: Class-D outputs are essentially square waves at the switching frequency. Per Analog Devices guidelines, keep speaker traces short and use twisted pairs. Ferrite beads (600Ω @ 100MHz) on each output reduce radiated emissions significantly.
