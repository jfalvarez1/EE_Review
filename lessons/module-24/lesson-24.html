Power Factor Correction (PFC)

Power Factor Correction ensures that power supplies draw current in phase with the AC line voltage, maximizing power delivery and minimizing harmonic distortion. PFC is mandatory for equipment above 75W in many jurisdictions. This lesson covers PFC fundamentals, boost PFC design, and compliance requirements.

Industry Applications

  • Server Power Supplies - 80+ efficiency standards require PFC
  • LED Lighting - High-power LED drivers
  • Industrial Motor Drives - VFDs with active front-end
  • EV Chargers - High-power AC-DC conversion
  • Telecom Rectifiers - -48V power systems

1. Power Factor Fundamentals

Power Factor Definition

\[ PF = \frac{P_{real}}{S_{apparent}} = \frac{P}{V_{rms} \times I_{rms}} \]

For Sinusoidal Systems

\[ PF = \cos(\phi) \]

Where φ is the phase angle between voltage and current.

For Non-Sinusoidal Current (with harmonics)

\[ PF = \frac{I_1}{I_{rms}} \times \cos(\phi_1) = \text{Distortion Factor} \times \text{Displacement Factor} \]

The Rectifier Problem

A simple capacitor-input rectifier draws current in short pulses at the peaks of the AC waveform:

Parameter Without PFC With Active PFC
Power Factor 0.5 - 0.7 0.95 - 0.99
THD (Current) 80% - 150% < 5%
Peak/RMS Current 2.5 - 3.0 ~1.4 (sinusoidal)
3rd Harmonic 70% - 80% < 3%

2. Boost PFC Topology

The most common active PFC uses a boost converter operating in CCM or CrCM:

PFC Boost Design Equations

Output Voltage: \( V_{out} > V_{in,pk} \) → Typically 380-400V DC for universal input

Duty Cycle: \( D(t) = 1 - \frac{|V_{in}(t)|}{V_{out}} \)

Inductor Current: \( I_L(t) = I_{in,pk} \cdot |\sin(\omega t)| \)

Power: \( P_{out} = \frac{V_{out} \cdot I_{out}}{\eta} \)

Operating Modes

Mode Description Pros Cons
CCM Continuous Conduction Mode - inductor current never reaches zero Lower RMS current, smaller inductor Diode reverse recovery losses
CrCM/BCM Critical Conduction Mode - current reaches zero each cycle ZVS turn-on, no reverse recovery Variable frequency, higher peak current
DCM Discontinuous Mode - dead time each cycle Simple control, inherent current shaping Highest peak current, large EMI filter

3. Control Architecture

PFC requires two control loops:

Voltage Loop (Slow)

  • Regulates output DC bus voltage
  • Bandwidth << 2× line frequency (e.g., 10-20 Hz)
  • Prevents 100/120 Hz ripple from appearing in current reference

Current Loop (Fast)

  • Forces input current to track rectified voltage shape
  • Bandwidth >> line frequency (e.g., 5-20 kHz)
  • Multiplies voltage loop output by |Vin| to form current reference

Average Current Mode Control

\[ I_{ref}(t) = k \cdot V_{EA} \cdot |V_{in}(t)| \]

Where VEA is the voltage error amplifier output

Current loop tracks Iref by adjusting duty cycle

4. Component Selection

Boost Inductor

For CCM: \( L > \frac{V_{in,min}^2}{2 \cdot P_{out} \cdot f_{sw}} \cdot D_{max}(1-D_{max}) \)

For CrCM: \( L = \frac{V_{in,pk}^2}{4 \cdot P_{out} \cdot f_{min}} \)

Core selection: Powder cores (Kool Mu, MPP) handle DC bias well

Boost Diode

  • CCM: Ultrafast or SiC Schottky (reverse recovery critical)
  • CrCM: Standard ultrafast OK (ZCS at turn-off)
  • Voltage rating: ≥ 1.2 × Vout (≥ 500V for 400V bus)

MOSFET

  • Voltage rating: ≥ 500V (600-650V typical)
  • Current rating: Based on RMS current at low line, high load
  • Consider superjunction MOSFETs for efficiency

Bulk Capacitor

\[ C_{bulk} = \frac{P_{out}}{2 \cdot \pi \cdot f_{line} \cdot V_{out} \cdot \Delta V} \]

Holdup time: \( C = \frac{2 \cdot P \cdot t_{hold}}{V_{out}^2 - V_{min}^2} \)

5. Regulatory Requirements

IEC 61000-3-2 Harmonic Limits

Class Equipment Type Power Threshold Notes
A Balanced 3-phase, household appliances > 75W (most equipment) Strictest limits
B Portable tools - Relaxed limits
C Lighting equipment > 25W THD-based or % fundamental
D PC and monitors 75W - 600W mA/W limits

Compliance Tips

  • Most equipment > 75W needs active PFC
  • Passive PFC may work for 75-200W with careful design
  • Test at worst-case line voltage (typically 230V for EU)
  • Include margin for production variation

6. Design Checklist

  • ☐ Output voltage > peak input voltage
  • ☐ Inductor sized for chosen operating mode
  • ☐ Diode rated for voltage and handles reverse recovery
  • ☐ MOSFET thermal design adequate
  • ☐ Bulk capacitor provides holdup and ripple
  • ☐ Control loop stable across line/load
  • ☐ Inrush limiting for initial capacitor charge
  • ☐ EMI filter for conducted emissions
  • ☐ IEC 61000-3-2 compliance verified
