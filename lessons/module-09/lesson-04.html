555 Timer Circuits

The 555 timer is one of the most versatile ICs ever designed. It can operate as a monostable (one-shot) or astable (free-running) oscillator with timing set by external RC components.

Astable Frequency
f = 1.44 / ((R₁ + 2R₂) × C)
Monostable Pulse
t = 1.1 × R × C
Key Insight: The 555 uses a voltage divider (3 resistors) to set thresholds at ⅓VCC and ⅔VCC. An internal comparator and flip-flop create precise timing based on capacitor voltage crossing these thresholds.

Design Tips

Duty Cycle > 50%
Standard astable: Duty = (R1+R2)/(R1+2R2)
Duty Cycle = 50%
Add diode across R2
CMOS 555 (7555)
Lower current, higher impedance
Decoupling Required: Place 0.1µF bypass cap close to VCC pin. The 555 draws current spikes during output transitions that can cause glitches.

What to run: a transient run of 10 ms in steps of 2 µs. The old 100 µs step gave a hundred samples across five cycles, which draws the exponential as a triangle.

What to watch: V(out), V(thr), V(ct), V(cb), V(q).

There is no 555 part in Circuit Toy, so the table builds one out of its own insides: two comparators watching the capacitor against the ⅔ and ⅓ taps of a three-resistor divider, an SR latch remembering which one fired last, an NMOS to dump the charge, and an inverter on the output. That is the whole chip. Seeing the thresholds as real nodes you can probe is worth more than a black box would have been.
