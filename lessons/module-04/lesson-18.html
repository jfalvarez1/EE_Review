BJT in Bandgap References

The bandgap voltage reference is one of the most important analog building blocks. It combines BJT physics to produce a stable voltage (~1.2V) nearly independent of temperature - the foundation of every precision ADC, DAC, and voltage regulator.

Output Voltage
VREF ≈ 1.2V (Si bandgap)
Tempco
< 50 ppm/°C (typical)
Ubiquitous Use: Every ADC, DAC, LDO, battery charger, PMIC, and precision analog IC contains a bandgap reference.

The Bandgap Principle

Combine two signals with opposite temperature coefficients:

Signal Tempco Type
VBE -2.1 mV/°C CTAT (Complementary to Absolute Temp)
ΔVBE +0.085 mV/°C (typically) PTAT (Proportional to Absolute Temp)
VREF ≈ 0 (designed) Temperature-stable
Formula
VREF = VBE + K × ΔVBE
Scale Factor K
≈ 2.1/0.085 ≈ 25 (to cancel tempcos)

PTAT Voltage: ΔVBE

For two transistors at different current densities:

ΔVBE
= VT × ln(J1/J2) = (kT/q) × ln(N)

where N is the current density ratio. This is PTAT because:

  • VT = kT/q increases with temperature
  • At 300K: VT ≈ 26mV, tempco = +0.086 mV/°C
  • With N = 8: ΔVBE = 26mV × ln(8) ≈ 54mV at 25°C

Classic Bandgap Circuit

How It Works

  1. Op-amp forces VR1 equal on both branches
  2. Equal VR1 → equal currents I1 = I2
  3. Q2 has N× area → different current density
  4. ΔVBE = VT ln(N) appears across R2
  5. I = ΔVBE/R2 is PTAT current
  6. VREF = VBE + I×Rgain
Result
VREF ≈ Vg0 ≈ 1.2V

Design Equations

PTAT Current
IPTAT = (VT/R2) × ln(N)
Output Voltage
VREF = VBE + IPTAT × Rgain

For zero tempco:

Required Gain
Rgain/R2 = |∂VBE/∂T| / (ln(N) × ∂VT/∂T)
Typical Values
Rgain/R2 ≈ 10-15 (for N=8)

Curvature Correction

First-order bandgaps have a residual "bow" in VREF vs T curve:

Uncorrected
50-100 ppm/°C typical
With correction
< 10 ppm/°C possible

Correction techniques:

  • Add higher-order PTAT terms
  • Use VBE² term (nonlinear)
  • Digital calibration with temperature sensor
