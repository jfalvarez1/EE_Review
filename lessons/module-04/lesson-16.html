Temperature Compensation Techniques

BJT parameters vary significantly with temperature. Understanding and compensating for these effects is essential for designing robust circuits that work from -40°C to +85°C (or beyond).

VBE Tempco
-2.1 mV/°C (typical)
β Variation
+0.5 to +1.0 %/°C
Industry Relevance: Automotive (-40°C to +125°C), industrial, aerospace, and outdoor equipment all require temperature-stable designs.

VBE Temperature Dependence

The VBE vs temperature relationship:

Formula
VBE(T) ≈ Vg0 - (Vg0 - VBE0)·T/T0
Vg0
≈ 1.2V (Si bandgap)
Impact: In a fixed-bias circuit, a 60°C temp rise reduces VBE by ~125mV, increasing IB and potentially causing thermal runaway.

Compensation Technique 1: Emitter Degeneration

Adding RE provides negative feedback that stabilizes IC against VBE changes:

How It Works

  1. Temperature ↑ → VBE ↓
  2. IC tries to increase
  3. VE = IC·RE increases
  4. VBE = VB - VE decreases
  5. This counteracts the original IC increase
Stability Factor
S = ΔIC/ΔICO ≈ 1 + RE/re

Compensation Technique 2: Diode Compensation

Use a diode (or diode-connected transistor) to track VBE changes:

How It Works

The diode has the same -2.1mV/°C tempco as the transistor's VBE. As temperature changes:

  • Both VD and VBE change equally
  • VB = VCC - IBRB - VD
  • Net effect: constant IC
Best Practice: Use a transistor with collector tied to base (same part as Q1) for best tracking.

Compensation Technique 3: Current Source Bias

Replace bias resistors with a current source that maintains constant IB:

Advantage
IC = β × IB with constant IB
Limitation
β still varies with temperature

Better approach: Use an emitter-degenerated current source that forces IE (≈ IC) constant.

Thermal Runaway

A dangerous positive feedback condition:

  1. IC increases → more power dissipation
  2. Junction temperature rises → VBE decreases
  3. Lower VBE → higher IB → higher IC
  4. Repeat until destruction
Prevention
Adequate RE, thermal design, current limiting
Stability Criterion
∂Pdiss/∂Tj < 1/RθJA
