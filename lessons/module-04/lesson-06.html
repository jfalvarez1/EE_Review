Signal Integrity Basics

Signal integrity (SI) determines whether digital and analog signals arrive at their destinations uncorrupted. Poor SI causes timing errors, false triggering, EMI emissions, and mysterious failures. Understanding transmission lines, reflections, and crosstalk is essential for any high-speed design.

Critical Speed
When trace length > λ/10 (rise time dependent)
Key Metric
Characteristic impedance Z₀

When SI Matters

A trace becomes a transmission line when its length is significant compared to the signal wavelength:

Critical Length: Lcritical = tr × vp / 10

Where: tr = rise time, vp ≈ 6 in/ns (FR4)
Rise Time Critical Length Examples
10 ns 6 inches I²C at 100kHz
1 ns 0.6 inches SPI at 10MHz, UART high speed
100 ps 0.06 inches DDR memory, PCIe, USB 3.0
Rule of Thumb: If the trace is longer than 1/10th of the rise time distance, you need controlled impedance and termination.

Transmission Line Fundamentals

Reflections and Termination

When impedance changes, part of the signal reflects back:

Reflection Coefficient: ρ = (ZL - Z₀) / (ZL + Z₀)
Termination Type Location Pros Cons
Series (source) Near driver Low power, simple Half amplitude until reflection returns
Parallel (load) Near receiver Full amplitude immediately DC power consumption
AC (RC) Near receiver No DC power Requires tuning for frequency
Thevenin Near receiver Sets DC bias level Two resistors, power consumption

Crosstalk

Coupling between adjacent traces creates unwanted signals:

Capacitive (FEXT)
Electric field coupling, far-end crosstalk
Inductive (NEXT)
Magnetic field coupling, near-end crosstalk
3W Rule: Space traces at 3× trace width for <2% crosstalk
High-Speed Routing: Never run clock or high-speed signals parallel to sensitive analog traces. Cross at 90° if crossing is unavoidable.
