Thermal Design and Heat Management

Every circuit dissipates power as heat. Managing thermal loads ensures reliability, prevents failures, and extends product life. Understanding thermal resistance networks, derating curves, and cooling strategies is essential for production hardware.

Key Relationship
ΔT = P × θ (thermal Ohm's law)
Reliability Rule
Every 10°C reduces lifetime by ~50%

Thermal Resistance Network

Heat flows from junction to ambient through series thermal resistances:

Parameter Symbol Typical Values Controlled By
Junction-to-Case θJC 0.5-5 °C/W IC manufacturer (package)
Case-to-Sink θCS 0.1-2 °C/W Thermal interface material
Sink-to-Ambient θSA 1-20 °C/W Heatsink size, airflow
Junction-to-Ambient θJA 20-150 °C/W Package + PCB + environment

Thermal Interface Materials (TIM)

Material Thermal Conductivity θCS Application
Bare contact (air gap) 0.026 W/m·K 1-5 °C/W Never acceptable
Thermal grease 0.5-5 W/m·K 0.1-0.5 °C/W Standard heatsink mount
Thermal pad 1-6 W/m·K 0.3-1 °C/W Production, gap filling
Phase-change material 3-5 W/m·K 0.1-0.3 °C/W High-performance
Solder (direct die attach) ~50 W/m·K <0.1 °C/W LED, power modules
Mounting Pressure: Thermal pads and grease require proper pressure for optimal contact. Follow torque specifications for heatsink mounting screws.

Heatsink Selection

Required Heatsink: θSA = (TJ,max - TA) / P - θJC - θCS

PCB Thermal Design

Technique Benefit Implementation
Thermal vias Conduct heat to inner layers/bottom Array under thermal pad, 0.3mm diameter typical
Copper pours Spread heat laterally 2oz copper on power layers
Exposed paddle Direct thermal path to PCB QFN/QFP packages with ground pad
Aluminum substrate Excellent thermal conduction LED boards, high-power applications
Via Fill: For production reliability, thermal vias under components should be filled (epoxy or copper-plated) to prevent solder wicking during reflow.
