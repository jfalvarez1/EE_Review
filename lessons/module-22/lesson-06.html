Radiated Emissions

Radiated emissions are electromagnetic energy unintentionally emitted from electronic equipment that propagates through space. These emissions can interfere with nearby equipment and must be controlled to meet regulatory requirements like FCC Part 15 and CISPR 32. Understanding antenna mechanisms and mitigation strategies is essential for EMC compliance.

Frequency Range:30 MHz - 1 GHz+
Test Distance:3m or 10m
Limit Class B @3m:40 dBμV/m (30-230 MHz)
Antenna Types:Loops, slots, cables
Key Insight: At frequencies where cable lengths approach λ/4 (quarter wavelength), cables become efficient antennas. A 1-meter cable resonates at 75 MHz, making cable management critical for radiated emissions compliance above 30 MHz.

Common Radiation Mechanisms

Mechanism Type Frequency Mitigation
PCB Current Loops Magnetic dipole ∝ f²A (below λ) Minimize loop area
I/O Cables Electric monopole Peak at λ/4 resonance Ferrites, CM filtering
Heatsink Slots Slot antenna Peak at λ/2 Add fins, conductive coating
Enclosure Seams Slot antenna λ/2 of seam length EMI gaskets, bonding

Radiated Emissions Design Rules

Loop Rule: E ∝ f² × A × I
Dipole Rule: E ∝ f × L × I
λ/20 Rule: Apertures < λ/20 for 20dB SE
3dB per Octave: Field increases 6dB per frequency doubling
