Shielding Effectiveness

Electromagnetic shielding is a fundamental EMC technique that contains or excludes electromagnetic fields using conductive or magnetic materials. Understanding shielding mechanisms enables proper enclosure design for both emissions containment and immunity protection.

Purpose:Attenuate EM fields crossing a boundary
Mechanisms:Reflection, Absorption, Multiple Reflections
Materials:Copper, Aluminum, Steel, Mu-metal, Conductive plastics
Typical SE:40-100 dB for solid metal enclosures
Key Insight: A shield is only as good as its weakest point. Apertures (holes, seams, gaps) typically limit practical shielding effectiveness far more than the shield material itself.

Shielding Mechanisms

Total Shielding Effectiveness

SE_total = SE_reflection + SE_absorption + SE_multiple_reflection [dB]

1. Reflection Loss

Caused by impedance mismatch at shield boundary. Dominant for low-frequency electric fields.

R = 20 log₁₀[|η₀ + η_s|² / (4|η₀||η_s|)]

For good conductors: R ≈ 168 - 10 log₁₀(μᵣf/σᵣ) [dB]

2. Absorption Loss

Exponential attenuation as wave travels through material. Dominant at high frequencies.

A = 8.686 × t/δ [dB]

where δ = skin depth = √(2/(ωμσ))

3. Multiple Reflection Loss

Internal reflections within thin shields. Can be positive or negative.

M ≈ 20 log₁₀|1 - e^(-2t/δ)| (often neglected when A > 10 dB)

Aperture Effects

Apertures (holes, slots, seams) are the primary limiters of practical shielding effectiveness. Even small openings can significantly degrade shielding at high frequencies.

Single Aperture

SE_aperture ≈ 20 log₁₀(λ / 2L) [dB]

where L = longest aperture dimension, λ = wavelength

Multiple Apertures

SE_n_holes = SE_single - 10 log₁₀(n)

n identical holes reduce SE by 10×log(n) dB

Slot vs. Round Hole

Slot Antenna:Length is critical, width has minor effect
Round Hole:Diameter determines cutoff
Rule of Thumb:Keep apertures < λ/20 for 20 dB SE

Material Selection Guidelines

  • Electric field (high Z): Any good conductor works well
  • Magnetic field (low Z): High permeability materials needed
  • Plane wave: Thickness matters for absorption
  • Low frequency: Permeability more important
  • High frequency: Conductivity more important

Practical Shielding Considerations

Seam and Joint Treatment

  • Continuous welding: Best for permanent enclosures
  • EMI gaskets: Conductive foam, beryllium copper fingers, elastomers
  • Conductive adhesives: For non-removable joints
  • Overlap joints: Increase contact surface area

Gasket Selection

Conductive Foam:Low cost, moderate SE, limited compression life
BeCu Fingers:High SE, long life, expensive
Wire Mesh:Good for ventilation openings
Conductive Elastomer:Environmental sealing + shielding

Cable Penetrations

  • Filter all signals entering/leaving enclosure
  • Use shielded connectors with 360° bonding
  • Avoid pigtail grounds on cable shields
  • Consider waveguide-below-cutoff for ventilation
