Common-Mode Chokes

Common-mode chokes are critical EMI filtering components that attenuate common-mode noise while allowing differential signals to pass unimpeded. They are essential for USB, Ethernet, HDMI, power lines, and many other interfaces.

Purpose:Block common-mode (CM) noise, pass differential (DM) signals
Operation:Coupled inductors with opposing DM flux cancellation
Applications:Data lines, power entry, motor drives
Key Specs:CM impedance, insertion loss, DCR, rated current
Key Principle: In a CM choke, differential currents create opposing magnetic flux that cancels, presenting low impedance. Common-mode currents create additive flux, presenting high impedance.

Common-Mode vs Differential-Mode

Definitions

Common-Mode Current: I_CM = (I₁ + I₂) / 2

Differential-Mode Current: I_DM = (I₁ - I₂) / 2

where I₁ and I₂ are the currents in each conductor

Noise Sources

TypeSourcePathSolution
Common-ModeGround noise, capacitive couplingBoth wires to groundCM choke

Emissions:CM currents radiate more efficiently (cable as antenna)
Immunity:External fields couple as CM noise

Winding Configurations

  • Bifilar wound: Two wires wound together - best coupling, balanced
  • Separate sections: Each winding in different section - higher voltage isolation
  • Toroid: Lowest EMI, no air gap needed
  • U/E core: Easy winding, air gap possible for DC bias

Selection Criteria

Key Parameters

CM Impedance:Higher = better CM rejection (typically 100-2000Ω)
Rated Current:Must handle max DC + peak signal current
DCR:Lower = less voltage drop, power loss
DM Inductance:Should be low for minimal signal impact
Insertion Loss:Signal attenuation - lower is better for data

Design Considerations

Placement Guidelines

  • Place CM choke close to connector (EMI entry/exit point)
  • Keep traces short between choke and connector
  • Maintain signal pair symmetry through choke
  • Ground any shield connection at choke location

Common Pitfalls

  • Saturation: DC or low-frequency currents can saturate core
  • Resonance: Parasitic capacitance creates unwanted resonances
  • Mode conversion: Asymmetric layout converts DM to CM
  • Insufficient impedance: CM Z too low at problem frequency

Complementary Filtering

CM chokes work best combined with:

  • ESD protection diodes (TVS) for transient protection
  • Series resistors for impedance matching
  • Shunt capacitors to ground for additional HF filtering
