BLDC Motor Driver Design

Brushless DC (BLDC) motors are found in everything from drones and electric vehicles to hard drives and industrial robots. Understanding the three-phase inverter bridge and gate driver design is essential for power electronics work.

What You'll Learn:
  • Three-phase bridge topology and commutation
  • Bootstrap gate driver circuit operation
  • Dead-time control to prevent shoot-through
  • Current sensing for motor control
  • Practical component selection

Three-Phase Inverter Bridge

A BLDC motor is driven by a six-switch inverter that creates a rotating magnetic field by energizing the stator coils in sequence. Each phase (U, V, W) has a high-side and low-side MOSFET.

Commutation Sequence (Six-Step)

BLDC motors use trapezoidal commutation where only two phases conduct at a time. The controller sequences through 6 states, each 60 electrical degrees apart:

Step High-Side ON Low-Side ON Current Flow Hall Sensors
1Q1 (U)Q4 (V)U → V101
2Q1 (U)Q6 (W)U → W001
3Q3 (V)Q6 (W)V → W011
4Q3 (V)Q2 (U)V → U010
5Q5 (W)Q2 (U)W → U110
6Q5 (W)Q4 (V)W → V100

Bootstrap Gate Driver Circuit

The high-side MOSFETs need a gate voltage 10-12V above their source, which floats with the motor phase. The bootstrap circuit charges a capacitor when the low-side is ON, then uses that charge to drive the high-side gate.

Bootstrap Limitations:
  • Max duty cycle ~95%: Low-side must turn ON periodically to refresh C_boot
  • Startup: Need initial LS pulse to charge bootstrap before HS can turn on
  • Leakage: At very low frequencies, C_boot may discharge too much

Dead-Time Control

Shoot-through occurs when both high-side and low-side MOSFETs conduct simultaneously, creating a short from V+ to GND. This causes massive current spikes that can destroy the MOSFETs instantly.

Dead-Time Calculation

Dead-time must be longer than the MOSFET turn-off time but short enough to minimize distortion and body diode conduction losses.

Dead-Time Trade-offs:
  • Too short: Risk of shoot-through → MOSFET destruction
  • Too long: Body diode conduction → losses, voltage spikes, EMI
  • Typical values: 100-500ns for 48V systems, 50-200ns for 12V

Motor Current Sensing

Current sensing is essential for torque control, overcurrent protection, and sensorless commutation (back-EMF zero-crossing detection).

Sensing Methods

Method Location Pros Cons
DC Bus Shunt Between V- and GND Single resistor, simple, low-side sensing Only sees total DC current, not phase currents
Low-Side Shunts Each LS source to GND Low-side sensing (easy), phase currents visible Only valid when respective LS is ON, 3 resistors
Inline Phase Shunts In series with each motor phase Always valid, true phase currents High-side sensing needed, 3 resistors, complex
Hall Effect Sensor Around phase wire Isolated, no insertion loss Cost, bandwidth, offset drift

What to run: a transient run of 200 µs in steps of 100 ns.

What to watch: V(vs), V(vb,vs), I(Lmotor) — and V(pwm_h) against V(pwm_l) on the same axes, to see the dead time.

The switch node V(vs) is a square wave between 0 and 48 V with a duty of 11/24 = 45.8%, so its average is 22 V. The winding sees 22 V against 18 V of back-EMF across 500 mΩ, which is 8 A, with 2.86 A of ripple. Without the back-EMF source the same bridge would show 44 A, because a resistor is not a motor — and confusing the two is how a drive gets designed around a current that never happens.

Then change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it. A simulator is only worth the time if you are checking an answer you already have — otherwise it is a very slow way of being surprised.

BLDC Driver Design Checklist

Progressive Exercises

Level 1: Fundamentals

Exercise 1: A BLDC motor runs at 24V DC with 30A max phase current. If using MOSFETs with RDS(on) = 10mΩ, what is the conduction loss in each MOSFET (one HS + one LS conducting)?

P = I²R for each MOSFET, then sum for both conducting FETs

Exercise 2: Calculate the bootstrap capacitor for: Qg = 40nC, Iqbs = 150µA, ton_max = 20ms, allowed droop = 0.3V.

Q_total = Qg + Iqbs × ton, then C = Q/V

Exercise 3: A gate driver has 120ns propagation delay and 10% matching spec. MOSFETs have td(off) = 45ns and tf = 25ns. What is the minimum dead-time?

DT > td(off) + tf + prop_mismatch. Mismatch = 120ns × 10%

Level 2: Application

Exercise 4: A shunt resistor of 5mΩ measures motor current. At 25A, the shunt voltage is amplified by a CSA with gain 50. What is the output voltage?

Vshunt = I × R, Vout = Vshunt × Gain

Exercise 5: A BLDC driver switches at 20kHz with 95% max duty cycle. What is the minimum low-side ON time per cycle to refresh bootstrap?

Period = 1/f, LS ON time = (1 - D) × Period

Level 3: Design Analysis

Exercise 6: A 48V/50A BLDC controller uses MOSFETs with Qg = 80nC. At 20kHz switching, what is the total gate drive power for all 6 MOSFETs (P = Qg × Vgs × f)?

P = 6 × Qg × Vgs × f, assume Vgs = 12V

Exercise 7: Motor current sampling uses a 12-bit ADC with 3.3V reference. CSA gain is 20, shunt is 10mΩ. What is the current resolution (A per LSB)?

1 LSB = Vref/4096. Then I_lsb = V_lsb / (Gain × Rshunt)
