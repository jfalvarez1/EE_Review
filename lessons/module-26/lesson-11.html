ERCOT — An Islanded Grid and Its Market

ERCOT is the only one of the three North American interconnections that is also a single balancing authority and a single ISO. Everything unusual about it — the regulatory status, the energy-only market, the inertia problem, the speed of its interconnection queue — follows from one physical fact: it is not synchronously connected to anything else.

The DC ties — and why they matter legally

ERCOT touches its neighbours only through DC links, which transfer power without synchronising frequency:

Total import capability is on the order of 1,200 MW — about 1.3% of peak demand. In an emergency, ERCOT is essentially on its own.

The jurisdictional consequence: under Federal Power Act §201(b)(1), FERC's authority attaches to transmission and wholesale sales in interstate commerce. Because ERCOT's power does not cross state lines synchronously, its wholesale market and intrastate transmission largely fall outside FERC ratemaking jurisdiction — they are governed by ERCOT protocols under Public Utility Commission of Texas oversight instead. FERC retains reliability-standard authority under §215.

Nameplate is not capacity. ERCOT's resource-adequacy accounting uses Effective Load Carrying Capability: about 8,186 MW from 41 GW of wind, 9,932 MW from 41 GW of solar, and only 5,029 MW from 21 GW of batteries (a 31% contribution factor at the peak-load hour). If you take one number away from this lesson, take that gap — it is the difference between a headline and a planning study.

Battery storage has grown roughly 24× in under five years: 889 MW at the start of 2022, ~5 GW in early 2023, 8.5 GW in April 2025, and 21,177 MW by August 2026.

The nodal market

ERCOT converted from four zonal prices to full nodal pricing on 1 December 2010. Locational marginal price at every bus decomposes as

LMP = energy + congestion + loss
  • SCED (Security-Constrained Economic Dispatch) runs every 5 minutes, issuing new base points and prices. Settlement intervals average three consecutive SCED prices into 15 minutes.
  • Day-ahead market clears once, around 10 a.m., co-optimising all 24 hours. It is financial; the real-time market is physical.
  • Settlement points: resource nodes at each generator; load zones (LZ_HOUSTON, LZ_NORTH, LZ_SOUTH, LZ_WEST); and trading hubs (HB_HOUSTON, HB_NORTH, HB_SOUTH, HB_WEST, HB_BUSAVG, and HB_PAN for the Panhandle, added as wind built out).
  • CRRs (Congestion Revenue Rights) are the financial hedge against node-to-node congestion cost.
  • RTC+B (real-time co-optimisation plus batteries) went live 5 December 2025 — energy and ancillary services now clear together in every 5-minute SCED run.

Energy-only, and how scarcity is priced

ERCOT has no capacity market. Generators earn only from energy and ancillary services, so the market must produce prices high enough, often enough, to justify building plant. The mechanism is the Operating Reserve Demand Curve, in place since 2014:

ORDC adder = LOLP × (VOLL − system lambda)

As reserves tighten, the loss-of-load probability rises and the adder pushes price toward the value of lost load. Post-Uri reforms lowered VOLL from $9,000 to $5,000/MWh, and the system-wide offer cap with it, effective 1 January 2022. A low cap (LCAP) of $2,000/MWh takes over once cumulative scarcity revenue passes a threshold.

Interconnection: connect and manage

Governed by ERCOT Planning Guide Section 5. The sequence is a screening study, then a Full Interconnection Study containing steady-state, stability, short-circuit and facility studies, then the Standard Generation Interconnection Agreement.

The Planning Guide states outright that the Full Interconnection Study “is not intended to determine the deliverability of power… or to ensure that the proposed Generation Resource does not experience any congestion-related curtailment.” That single clause is ERCOT's connect-and-manage philosophy: interconnection is fast, and congestion risk sits with the generator.

Because ERCOT is not FERC-jurisdictional, it was never required to adopt Order 2023's mandatory cluster studies, and still runs a serial, first-come-first-served queue. The result is a queue of 2,008 active requests totalling 453,562 MW (February 2026) — storage 177,642 MW, solar 162,927 MW, gas 60,715 MW, wind 47,793 MW — and among the highest renewable curtailment in the country (over 8 TWh of wind and solar in 2024, with West Texas export constraints alone accounting for roughly 22%).

Large loads are the newer pressure: roughly 410 GW of interconnection requests as of March 2026, about 87% data centres, against an all-time peak of 91 GW. Senate Bill 6 (2025) makes large loads interconnected after 31 December 2025 curtailable during firm load shed unless designated critical — putting them in the emergency response pool rather than behind it.

The interconnection, drawn

ERCOT's entire external connection: five DC links totalling roughly 1,200 MW against a 91 GW peak. Every one is a converter station, not a synchronous tie — power crosses, frequency does not.

Why this single drawing explains so much: a synchronously connected grid leans on its neighbours for inertia, reserves and emergency imports. ERCOT cannot. Its frequency is its own, its reserves must be carried internally, and in an emergency there is roughly 1.3% of peak demand available from outside. That isolation is simultaneously why it escapes FERC ratemaking jurisdiction and why Winter Storm Uri came within minutes of a full interconnection blackout.

Check yourself

Why is ERCOT largely outside FERC's wholesale ratemaking jurisdiction?

FERC's authority under FPA §201(b)(1) attaches to transmission and wholesale sales in interstate commerce. ERCOT is asynchronously islanded — its only external links are DC ties totalling roughly 1,200 MW — so its wholesale transactions are not interstate commerce in the relevant sense. PUCT regulates instead. FERC does keep reliability-standard authority under §215.

21 GW of batteries are installed, but only about 5 GW counts toward resource adequacy. Why?

ELCC measures how much firm load a resource can actually support at the hours that matter. A battery's contribution is limited by its duration — most ERCOT batteries are 1–2 hour systems, and a peak-load event can outlast them. The 31% contribution factor reflects the probability that the fleet is charged and available across the full risk window, not its instantaneous power rating.

What does an energy-only market have to do instead of a capacity market?

It must let prices rise high enough during scarcity to recover fixed costs — the “missing money” problem. ORDC does this explicitly by adding LOLP × (VOLL − lambda) to the energy price as reserves thin. The trade is volatility: revenue arrives in a few hundred hours a year rather than as a steady capacity payment, which shifts risk onto developers and financiers.

Why does ERCOT have unusually high renewable curtailment?

Connect-and-manage. The interconnection study deliberately does not test deliverability, so generation can be built faster than the transmission to export it — especially in West Texas and the Panhandle. Congestion is then managed after the fact by redispatch and curtailment, and the economic risk of that sits with the generator, not the ISO.
