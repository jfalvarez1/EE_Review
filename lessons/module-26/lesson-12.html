Frequency control

NERC BAL-001-2 sets CPS1 ≥ 100% on a rolling 12 months, with a frequency-bound constant ε1 of 0.030 Hz for ERCOT — the loosest of the four interconnections, reflecting a smaller, islanded, lower-inertia system. The Balancing Authority ACE Limit gives the operating band 60 ± 3ε1 = 59.91–60.09 Hz.

ERCOT's own regional standard BAL-001-TRE-2 governs governor response: deadband ≤±0.034 Hz for mechanical governors and ≤±0.017 Hz for electronic ones, with 4% droop on steam and 5% on hydro, combustion turbines, combined cycle, diesel, DC ties and variable renewables.

Energy Emergency Alert levels

Physical Responsive Capability — the reserve that can actually respond to frequency — is the trigger variable. Current thresholds from the ERCOT Nodal Operating Guides §4.5.3:

LevelTriggerActions
Advisory PRC < 3,000 MW Emergency Response Service becomes deployable
EEA1 PRC < 2,500 MW, no recovery within 30 min Recall reserves, import on the DC ties, hold units online
EEA2 PRC < 2,000 MW, or frequency < 59.91 Hz for 15 min Distribution voltage reduction, public conservation appeal, deploy ECRS and RRS from load resources
EEA3 PRC cannot be held above 1,500 MW, or frequency < 59.91 Hz for 20–25 min, or steady-state frequency < 59.8 Hz Firm load shed, ordered in 100 MW blocks; storage ordered to stop charging

At least 25% of ERCOT load must be UFLS-armed at all times. Separately, NERC PRC-024 governs when generators may trip: no automatic trip above 59.4 Hz, and at least a 9-minute delay in the 58.4–59.4 Hz band. Hold that number.

The number everyone quotes, corrected. Frequency stayed below 59.4 Hz for 4 minutes 23 seconds, not 4:37. The widely repeated “4 minutes 37 seconds” is the remaining margin to the 9-minute generator trip threshold: 9:00 − 4:23 = 4:37. Had frequency stayed low for the full nine minutes, roughly 17,000 MW of additional generation would have tripped on under-frequency relays, which the report says could have blacked out the entire interconnection. The low point was 59.304 Hz, not the frequently misquoted 59.302 Hz.

Root causes

Unplanned generation outages in the event area reached 65,622 MW — more than four times the comparable 2011 cold event. By fuel of the failed units: 58% gas, 27% wind, 6% coal, 2% solar, under 1% nuclear.

By cause, 75% of outages were freezing (44.2%) or fuel supply (31.4%), with natural gas fuel-supply problems alone accounting for 27.3% of everything.

The feedback loop is the real lesson. Gas production fell over 30% by 14 February and 71% by the 17th. Of that decline, 52.2% was freezing or preventive shut-in — and 18.1% was loss of electric power to wellheads, gathering and processing. ERCOT's own load shedding cut power to the gas infrastructure that its gas-fired generation depended on. Electricity and gas are one system, and the 2021 event is the canonical demonstration.

At least 210 deaths per the FERC/NERC report; the Texas Department of State Health Services final count was 246 across 77 counties, roughly 65% hypothermia. Economic damage estimates range from $80–130B (Dallas Fed) to $195–295B (Perryman Group) depending on what is counted.

What changed

  • Mandatory weatherization — 16 TAC §25.55 and Senate Bill 3 (2021) made weatherization compulsory for all ERCOT generation and T&D entities, with penalties from $5,000 to $1,000,000 per violation per day. The Railroad Commission designates “critical” gas facilities that must weatherize and receive disconnection protection.
  • Offer cap and VOLL cut — from $9,000 to $5,000/MWh effective 1 January 2022, with an emergency $2,000/MWh cap if the system-wide price holds at the cap for 12 hours in a rolling 24.
  • ECRS — a new 10-minute, 2-hour ancillary service launched June 2023, ERCOT's first new daily-procured product in over 20 years.
  • Texas Energy Fund — SB 2627 (2023) and Proposition 7, initially $5B, offering 3.0% 20-year loans for new dispatchable generation ≥100 MW. Demand was roughly 11× oversubscribed: 125 notices of intent for $38.9B and 55,908 MW.
  • RTC+B — real-time co-optimisation went live 5 December 2025.
  • Performance Credit Mechanism shelved — PUCT concluded in December 2024 that the proposed ~$1B/yr mechanism would not deliver proportionate reliability benefit. ERCOT remains energy-only.

Check yourself

What are the current EEA1/2/3 PRC thresholds?

EEA1 below 2,500 MW; EEA2 below 2,000 MW or frequency under 59.91 Hz for 15 minutes; EEA3 unable to hold above 1,500 MW, or frequency under 59.91 Hz for 20–25 minutes, or steady-state frequency below 59.8 Hz. Note these were raised in a November 2023 update — during Uri itself the operative EEA3 trigger was about 1,430 MW.

Why did ERCOT shed load in 100 MW blocks rather than all at once?

Load shed is the tool for restoring frequency and rebuilding PRC, and it is irreversible for the customers involved. Incremental blocks let the operator apply the minimum needed, observe the frequency response, and stop — the same reason a control loop uses proportional action rather than bang-bang.

Fast Frequency Response must act within 15 cycles. Why so fast, when ECRS gets 10 minutes?

They solve different problems. FFR exists to arrest the initial frequency decline before it reaches the 59.3 Hz UFLS stage — on a low-inertia system that can take barely a second. ECRS replaces the deployed reserve and covers net-load ramps over the following minutes. The whole reserve stack is a set of nested time constants: FFR (0.25 s), RRS (seconds), ECRS (10 min), Non-Spin (30 min).

Why did losing electric power make the gas shortage worse during Uri?

Wellheads, gathering compressors and processing plants run on electricity. When ERCOT shed load, some of that infrastructure lost power, cutting gas production — which starved the gas-fired generation ERCOT needed to restore frequency, forcing more load shed. 18.1% of the 14 February production decline was attributed to loss of power. SB 3's critical-facility designation exists specifically to break that loop.
