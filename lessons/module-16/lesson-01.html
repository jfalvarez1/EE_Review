Audio Amplifier Fundamentals

Audio amplifiers must faithfully reproduce signals from 20Hz to 20kHz with minimal distortion. Understanding frequency response, gain staging, and distortion mechanisms is essential.

Key Insight: Human hearing is logarithmic—we perceive equal ratios as equal intervals. That's why we use dB for gain and why octave-based frequency spans matter.

Gain Staging

Audio systems typically have multiple gain stages:

Stage Typical Gain Purpose
Microphone Preamp +20 to +60 dB Boost mic-level to line-level
Line Stage 0 to +20 dB Buffer, EQ, mixing
Volume Control −∞ to 0 dB User level adjustment
Power Amp +20 to +30 dB Drive speakers
Headroom: Each stage needs headroom above nominal level for peaks. Aim for +10dB minimum. Clipping sounds terrible in audio!

Distortion Types

THD (Total Harmonic)
Harmonics of input frequency (2nd, 3rd, etc.)
IMD (Intermodulation)
Sum/difference frequencies from multiple inputs
Crossover
Distortion at zero-crossing in Class AB/B
Slew-Induced
High frequency distortion from limited slew rate
