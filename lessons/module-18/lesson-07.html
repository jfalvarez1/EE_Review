Strain Gauge Bridges

Strain gauges are resistive sensors that measure mechanical deformation. When bonded to a structure, they change resistance proportionally to strain. Wheatstone bridge configurations convert this tiny resistance change into a measurable voltage while providing temperature compensation and improved sensitivity.

Gauge Factor (GF)~2.0 for metal foil gauges
Typical Strain100-5000 µε (microstrain)
ΔR/R~0.02% to 1% (very small!)
Output~2 mV/V at full scale
Key Concept: The Gauge Factor (GF) relates resistance change to strain: ΔR/R = GF × ε. For a 350Ω gauge with GF=2, 1000µε produces only a 0.7Ω change. High-gain, low-noise signal conditioning is essential.

Wheatstone Bridge Configurations

Vout = V+ − V− = Vexc[R3/(R1+R3) − R4/(R2+R4)]. At balance R1/R3 = R2/R4. Quarter-, half- and full-bridge sensitivities depend on which arms are active and whether their strains have opposite signs; use the interactive arm configuration below. Load Cell Applications

Example full bridge with two tension and two compression gauges placed in alternating arms. For the illustrated changes, Vout = Vexc·ΔR/R; with ΔR/R = GF·ε this is Vexc·GF·ε. The gauges must actually experience those strains for this equation to apply. Choose excitation from sensor limits and self-heating, and verify the load-cell calibration and overload specification. What to run: a DC operating point; then set R3 by hand to 350 Ω (zero strain) and 353.5 Ω (5 mε), which is the sweep.

What to watch: V(vplus,vminus), V(out).

Note what the bridge is doing: it takes 5 V and returns 12 mV, discarding 2.5 V of common mode on each side to do it. That ratio — a differential signal 400 times smaller than the common-mode voltage it rides on — is why a bridge needs an instrumentation amplifier and not just a gain stage, and why the CMRR number on that amplifier's datasheet is the first thing to read.
