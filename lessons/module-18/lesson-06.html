RTD and Thermocouple Interfaces

Precision temperature measurement requires understanding the unique characteristics of RTDs (Resistance Temperature Detectors) and thermocouples. RTDs offer high accuracy and linearity, while thermocouples provide wide temperature range and fast response. This lesson covers the analog front-end design for both sensor types.

RTD TypesPt100, Pt1000, Ni, Cu

Key Difference: RTDs are passive resistors requiring excitation current. Thermocouples are active voltage generators requiring cold junction compensation. Both require careful attention to lead resistance and noise.

RTD Characteristics

RTDs change resistance with temperature following the Callendar-Van Dusen equation:

Pt100 Resistance vs Temperature:
R(T) = R₀(1 + αT + βT² + γT³) for T < 0°C
R(T) = R₀(1 + αT + βT²) for T ≥ 0°C

Where α = 3.9083×10⁻³, β = -5.775×10⁻⁷, γ = -4.183×10⁻¹²
