Logic Level Fundamentals

Digital interfaces require precise voltage thresholds to distinguish logic states. Understanding logic levels is fundamental when interfacing devices with different voltage standards—a common challenge in modern mixed-voltage systems.

Level Shifting Requirements

When interfacing different voltage domains, verify compatibility:

  • High-to-Low compatibility: 5V → 3.3V often works if input has 5V tolerant I/O
  • Low-to-High challenge: 3.3V → 5V TTL may work (2.4V > 2.0V threshold), but 3.3V → 5V CMOS often fails (2.4V < 3.5V threshold)
  • Always check: Absolute maximum ratings for input overvoltage

Key Takeaways

  • Modern systems often mix 1.8V, 2.5V, 3.3V, and 5V logic domains
  • TTL thresholds (~30% of VCC) differ from CMOS (~50% of VCC)
  • Noise margin determines immunity to interference—larger is better
  • 5V-tolerant inputs are common on 3.3V devices; check the datasheet
  • Level shifters are required when voltage domains are incompatible
