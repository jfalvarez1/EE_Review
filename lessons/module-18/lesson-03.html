Scenario: Precision RTD Temperature Measurement

Design a PT100 RTD interface with 0.1°C accuracy over -50°C to +200°C range. This requires careful attention to excitation current, lead resistance, and signal conditioning.

Sensor
PT100 (100Ω @ 0°C)

Key Challenge: 0.1°C accuracy requires measuring 0.0385Ω change (38.5mΩ). With 1mA excitation, this is 38.5µV - easily swamped by noise, offset, and lead resistance errors.

Wiring Configurations

3-Wire Assumption: 3-wire compensation assumes Rlead1 = Rlead2. Use matched cables and connectors. For critical applications, use 4-wire.

Error Sources

Error Source Typical Magnitude Mitigation
Lead resistance 1-10Ω per wire 3-wire or 4-wire compensation
Self-heating 0.1-1°C with 1mA Reduce excitation, pulsed measurement
Reference current 0.1-1% drift Ratiometric measurement
Amplifier offset 10-100µV Chopper amp or calibration
ADC noise 1-10 LSB Oversampling and averaging

What to run: a DC sweep of IEX from 500 µ to 1.50 m.

What to watch: V(amp_out), V(adc_in), V(rtd_lo) — the last one is the drop across RREF, and it is the measurement's own reference.

The two sense leads carry no current, because EAMP's inputs take none. That is the entire point of a Kelvin connection: RLEAD1 and RLEAD2 are in the circuit and drop nothing, so their 1 Ω — almost 1% of a Pt100 at 0 °C, which would be two and a half degrees of error — disappears from the answer. Sweep them from 1 Ω to 100 Ω and watch V(amp_out) not move.

Then change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it. A simulator is only worth the time if you are checking an answer you already have — otherwise it is a very slow way of being surprised.
