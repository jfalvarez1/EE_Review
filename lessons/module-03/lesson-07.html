Gyrator Circuits (Simulated Inductors)

Gyrators use op-amps to simulate inductance without bulky magnetic components. They're essential for active filter design, audio equalizers, and any application requiring large inductance values (henries) in IC-compatible form.

Basic Principle
Capacitor impedance inverted to look like inductor
Simulated L
Leq = R2 × C
Why Simulate Inductors? Real inductors are large, expensive, have parasitic resistance, and are difficult to integrate on-chip. A gyrator can simulate 10H using just a capacitor and resistors.

Impedance at Port A: Zin = jω × R1 × R2 × C = jωLeq
Key Insight: The positive feedback creates a "negative impedance converter" that transforms capacitive reactance into inductive reactance.

Limitations and Practical Considerations

Limitation Cause Mitigation
One terminal grounded Gyrator topology Use floating gyrator or different topology
Limited current capability Op-amp output current Use power op-amp or buffer
Bandwidth limited Op-amp GBW product Use high-speed op-amp
DC resistance R1 appears in series Accept or compensate in design
Non-ideal at high Q Op-amp phase shift Limit Q or use multiple sections
Frequency Limit: The gyrator works well only up to fmax ≈ GBW / (10 × Q). Beyond this, op-amp phase shift causes the simulated inductor to become resistive or capacitive.
