Logarithmic and Exponential Amplifiers

Logarithmic (log) and exponential (antilog) amplifiers exploit the exponential I-V relationship of semiconductor junctions. They're essential for audio companders, RF power measurement, analog computation, and data compression over wide dynamic ranges.

Log Amplifier
Vout = −k × ln(Vin/Vref)
Antilog Amplifier
Vout = Vref × 10(Vin/k)
Foundation: The diode/transistor equation I = IS × e(V/VT) provides the logarithmic transfer function, where VT = kT/q ≈ 26mV at room temperature.

Exponential (Antilog) Amplifier

The antilog amplifier reverses the log function, providing exponential expansion:

Complementary Relationship: Cascading a log amp with an antilog amp recreates the original signal (with sign inversion). This is the basis for analog multipliers and dividers.

Applications

Application Circuit Type Purpose
Audio Compandor Log + Antilog pair Compress dynamic range for transmission, expand on receive
RF Power Meter Log detector Convert dB directly (60-80dB range)
Analog Multiplier Log + Summer + Antilog log(A) + log(B) → log(A×B) → antilog → A×B
Analog Divider Log + Subtractor + Antilog log(A) − log(B) → log(A/B) → antilog → A/B
True RMS Converter Log + Square + Antilog Compute √(mean(V²))
Analog Multiplication: Vout = k × V1 × V2 using log(V1) + log(V2) = log(V1×V2)
