Voltage-to-Current Converters (V-to-I)

Voltage-to-current converters produce an output current proportional to an input voltage. They're essential for 4-20mA current loop transmitters, LED drivers, electrochemical sensors, and any application where current-mode signaling provides noise immunity over long cables.

Basic Relationship
Iout = Vin / Rset
Transconductance
gm = 1/Rset [A/V or S]
Current-Mode Advantage: Current signals are immune to voltage drops in long cables and IR drops in ground paths. The 4-20mA standard also provides a "live zero" (4mA = 0% signal) to detect wire breaks.

4-20mA Current Loop Standards

Current (mA) Meaning Notes
4 0% of range (live zero) Powers the transmitter; detects wire break (0mA)
12 50% of range Midpoint reference
20 100% of range Maximum signal
< 3.8 or > 21 Fault condition Under-range or over-range alarm
Scaling Equation: Iout = 4mA + (16mA × Signal%/100)
Noise Immunity: Current loops are immune to voltage drops in long cables (up to 1km+). The receiver measures current through a precision resistor, converting back to voltage locally.
