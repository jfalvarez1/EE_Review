Analog Oscillators

Analog oscillators generate periodic waveforms (sine, square, triangle) without external clock inputs. They're fundamental to signal generators, function generators, VCOs in synthesizers, and clock sources. Understanding the Barkhausen criterion is key to oscillator design.

Barkhausen Criterion
|Aβ| = 1 and ∠Aβ = 0° (or 360°)
Startup Condition
|Aβ| > 1 (slightly, then AGC limits)

Amplitude Stabilization (AGC)

Without AGC, oscillators either die out (|Aβ| < 1) or saturate (|Aβ| > 1). Common techniques:

Method Implementation Distortion
Lamp (incandescent) Positive temp coefficient limits amplitude Very low (< 0.01%)
JFET as variable R VGS controlled by peak detector Low (< 0.1%)
Diode limiting Back-to-back diodes clip output Moderate (1-3%)
Zener limiting Zeners set clipping level Higher (depends on zener)
Multiplying DAC Digital control of feedback Very low, precise amplitude

Relaxation Oscillators

For square and triangle waves, use comparators with hysteresis:

Astable Frequency: f = 1 / (2RC × ln((1+k)/(1-k))) where k = R2/(R1+R2)

For 50% duty cycle with symmetric hysteresis: f ≈ 1/(2.2RC)
