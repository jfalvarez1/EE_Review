Op-Amp Stability and Compensation

An op-amp circuit can oscillate if the feedback loop has too much phase shift at frequencies where the loop gain is still above unity. Understanding stability analysis and compensation techniques is essential for reliable analog design.

The Barkhausen Criterion

A feedback system will oscillate when:

|A × β| ≥ 1 AND ∠(A × β) = 360° (or 0°)

In an inverting amplifier, the feedback already introduces 180°. The op-amp must not add another 180° at frequencies where |Aβ| > 1.

Phase Margin and Gain Margin

Definitions

  • Phase Margin (PM): How much additional phase lag would cause oscillation at the unity-gain crossover frequency. PM = 180° + phase at |Aβ| = 1.
  • Gain Margin (GM): How much additional gain would cause oscillation when phase = -180°. GM = 1/|Aβ| when phase = -180°.

Rule of thumb: PM ≥ 45° and GM ≥ 6dB for stable operation

Noise Gain vs Signal Gain

Stability is determined by noise gain, not signal gain:

Unity Gain is the Worst Case

A unity-gain buffer (G = 1) has noise gain = 1, meaning the full GBW is available. This is actually the hardest configuration to stabilize because the crossover frequency is at maximum. Higher gain configurations are inherently more stable.

Decompensated Op-Amps

Some op-amps are "decompensated" to achieve higher slew rate and bandwidth. They are stable only above a minimum gain.

Op-Amp Min Stable Gain GBW Slew Rate
OP27 (compensated) 1 8 MHz 2.8 V/µs
OP37 (decompensated) 5 63 MHz 17 V/µs
AD8099 (decompensated) 2 3.8 GHz 1350 V/µs
LT1028 (compensated) 1 75 MHz 15 V/µs

Warning

Using a decompensated op-amp at a gain below its minimum stable gain will cause oscillation. Always check the datasheet!
