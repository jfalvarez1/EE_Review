BJT as a Voltage-Controlled Current Source

The bipolar junction transistor (BJT) is fundamentally a voltage-controlled current source. The collector current IC is controlled by the base-emitter voltage VBE through an exponential relationship described by the Ebers-Moll model.

Core Equation
IC = IS × e(VBE/VT)
Thermal Voltage
VT = kT/q ≈ 26mV @ 25°C
Key Insight: A 60mV change in VBE changes IC by a factor of 10. This extreme sensitivity means small VBE errors cause large current errors.

BJT Symbol and Terminal Structure

The NPN transistor has three terminals: Base (B), Collector (C), and Emitter (E). The arrow on the emitter indicates conventional current direction.

Terminal Functions

Transconductance (gm)

Transconductance is the ratio of change in collector current to change in base-emitter voltage:

Definition
gm = dIC/dVBE = IC/VT
Rule of Thumb
gm ≈ 40 × IC(mA) in mS

The intrinsic emitter resistance is the reciprocal of transconductance:

Intrinsic re
re = 1/gm = VT/IC
Rule of Thumb
re ≈ 25/IC(mA) in Ω

Temperature Effects

Temperature has profound effects on BJT operation:

Parameter Tempco Effect
VBE −2.1 mV/°C VBE decreases as temperature rises
IC (constant VBE) +9%/°C IC doubles every ~8°C
β (hFE) +0.5%/°C typical β increases with temperature
Critical Warning: Never design circuits that depend on a specific β value! β varies with temperature, IC, VCE, and unit-to-unit (50-500 for same part number).

Early Effect (Base Width Modulation)

In a real transistor, collector current IC is not perfectly independent of VCE. The Early effect causes IC to increase slightly as VCE increases, due to base width modulation.

Modified Ebers-Moll
IC = IS × e(VBE/VT) × (1 + VCE/VA)
Early Voltage
VA ≈ 50-200V (typical)

Physical Mechanism

As VCE increases, the collector-base depletion region widens, effectively narrowing the base width. A thinner base means:

  • Less recombination in the base
  • Higher collector efficiency
  • Slightly higher IC for the same VBE
Output Resistance
ro = VA / IC
Intrinsic Gain
Av,max = gm × ro = VA/VT
Design Impact: For a CE amplifier, the output resistance ro limits the maximum voltage gain. At IC=1mA with VA=100V: ro=100kΩ.

Design Guidelines

  • Use for IC < 100mA
  • Best β at IC ≈ 1-10mA
  • Pair with 2N3906 (PNP)
  • fT drops at high IC
  • TO-92 package
VEBO = 6V Maximum! The base-emitter junction can be destroyed by reverse voltages as low as 6-7V. Always protect against reverse bias in switching circuits.
