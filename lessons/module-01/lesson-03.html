Integrator and Differentiator

By replacing resistors with capacitors in op-amp feedback networks, we can create circuits that perform mathematical operations on signals. These are fundamental to control systems, signal processing, and analog computation.

Integrator
Vout = −(1/RC) ∫Vin dt
Differentiator
Vout = −RC × dVin/dt
Key Insight: The integrator has gain that increases with decreasing frequency (-20dB/decade slope). The differentiator has gain that increases with frequency (+20dB/decade). Both have stability issues!

Stability Issue: Differentiators have gain that rises with frequency. High-frequency noise is amplified enormously, and the circuit can oscillate. Always add Rs in series with C and Cf across R to limit HF gain.

Practical Modifications

Integrator DC drift to rail Add Rf in parallel with C Limits DC gain to Rf/Rin

Application: Triangle Wave Generator

A classic application combines an integrator with a comparator (Schmitt trigger) to create a triangle/square wave oscillator:

±12V

—

Formula
f = R2/(4 × R1 × R × C)
How it works: The comparator output (±Vsat) is integrated into a ramp. When the ramp crosses the comparator's threshold, the output flips, reversing the ramp direction. This creates continuous triangle and square wave outputs.
