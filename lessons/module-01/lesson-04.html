Bandwidth and Slew Rate

Op-amp performance is limited by two distinct mechanisms: bandwidth (small-signal) and slew rate (large-signal). Understanding when each dominates is crucial for high-frequency design.

Gain-Bandwidth Product
GBW = ACL × BW-3dB
Closed-Loop Bandwidth
BW = GBW / ACL
Key Insight: The GBW product is constant (for most op-amps). Double the gain → halve the bandwidth. This trade-off is fundamental to op-amp design.

Slew Rate Limiting

Slew rate is the maximum rate of change of the output voltage. When the required dV/dt exceeds the slew rate, the output becomes a triangle wave instead of following the input.

Dominant Pole Compensation: Why Op-Amps Are Stable

Internally, most op-amps use dominant pole compensation to ensure stability at any closed-loop gain. Understanding this is key to high-frequency design.

Uncompensated Op-Amp
Multiple poles → phase shift → oscillation risk
Compensation Capacitor
Miller cap (Cc) creates dominant low-frequency pole
How Miller Compensation Works:
A small capacitor (typically 30pF) across the high-gain second stage creates a pole at very low frequency. This "dominant pole" causes the gain to roll off at 20dB/decade, reaching unity gain (0dB) before other poles can add phase shift.

Slew Rate Origin

Slew rate is limited by the current available to charge the compensation capacitor:

Slew Rate Equation
SR = Itail / Cc

For a 741: Itail ≈ 20µA, Cc ≈ 30pF → SR = 20µA/30pF = 0.67 V/µs (close to spec of 0.5 V/µs)

Trade-off: Larger Cc improves stability but reduces slew rate. High-speed op-amps use smaller compensation caps and higher bias currents.

Phase Margin and Stability

Phase margin is the key stability metric. It's the "safety margin" before oscillation.

Phase Margin Definition
PM = 180° + ∠(Loop Gain) at f where |Loop Gain| = 1
Typical Target
PM > 45° (60° preferred)
Phase Margin Step Response Stability
< 0° Oscillation Unstable
0° - 45° Heavy ringing Marginally stable
45° - 60° Some overshoot (5-15%) Good
60° - 90° Minimal overshoot Excellent
Design Rule: When adding capacitive loads or feedback networks, always check phase margin. Capacitive loads reduce phase margin and may require compensation (Riso or feedback cap).

Op-Amp Comparison: Speed vs Power

Op-Amp SR GBW IQ Input Type Best For
LM741 (vintage) 0.5 V/µs 1 MHz 1.7 mA BJT Learning, low-freq
LM358 0.5 V/µs 1 MHz 0.5 mA BJT Single-supply, low cost
TL072 (JFET) 13 V/µs 3 MHz 1.4 mA JFET Audio, high-Z sources
OPA2134 20 V/µs 8 MHz 4 mA JFET High-quality audio
OPA1612 27 V/µs 40 MHz 3.6 mA BJT Low-noise audio
AD8065 180 V/µs 145 MHz 6 mA JFET DAC buffers, video
OPA657 700 V/µs 1.6 GHz 11 mA JFET TIA, photodiode
Selection Guide:
  • Low power: LM358 (0.5mA) for battery applications
  • Audio: TL072 or OPA2134 for good SR and low noise
  • High-Z input: JFET input (TL07x, OPA series) for piezo, pH sensors
  • High speed: AD8065 or OPA657 for video, TIA applications
