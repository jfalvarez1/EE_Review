BJT Current Limiters and Foldback Protection

Current limiting protects both the power supply and the load from damage during fault conditions. Understanding these circuits is essential for power supply design and any application where overcurrent protection is needed.

Simple Limit
Constant current above threshold
Foldback
Current decreases as voltage drops
Industry Use: LDO regulators, DC-DC converters, USB power delivery, motor drivers, LED drivers.

Simple Current Limiter

How It Works

  1. Load current flows through Rs
  2. Voltage across Rs = ILOAD × Rs
  3. When VRs ≥ VBE(Q2) ≈ 0.65V...
  4. Q2 turns ON, stealing base drive from Q1
  5. Q1 limits output current
Limit Current
ILIM = VBE/Rs ≈ 0.65V/Rs

For 1A limit: Rs = 0.65V / 1A = 0.65Ω

Problem with Simple Limiting

Power Dissipation Issue: During short circuit, Q1 must dissipate P = Vin × ILIM. For 12V input and 1A limit, that's 12W in the pass transistor!

Foldback Current Limiting

Solution: Reduce current as output voltage drops, minimizing power dissipation during fault:

Foldback Operation

R1 and R2 form a voltage divider that senses output voltage. As Vout drops:

  • Q2's base voltage (from divider) changes
  • Less voltage needed across Rs to trigger Q2
  • Current limit decreases as Vout drops
At short circuit
ISC = VBE × R2/(Rs×(R1+R2))
ISC can be designed to be 10-20% of ILIM, dramatically reducing power dissipation during faults.

Foldback Considerations

Aspect Advantage Disadvantage
Power Dissipation Much lower at short circuit —
Recovery — May latch in low-current state
Inrush Current — May not allow capacitor charging
Motor Loads — Startup current may trigger limit
Design Tip: Ensure ISC is sufficient to start the load (charge output capacitors, motor inrush), or use a "hiccup" mode that periodically retries.
