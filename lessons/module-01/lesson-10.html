Power BJTs and Thermal Design

Power transistors dissipate significant heat that must be managed to prevent failure. Understanding thermal resistance, Safe Operating Area (SOA), and heatsink design is essential for reliable power circuits.

Critical Insight: BJT junction temperature must stay below Tj(max), typically 150°C. Exceeding this causes immediate failure. β decreases with temperature, which can lead to thermal runaway in linear operation.

Thermal Model

Heat flows from junction to ambient through a series of thermal resistances:

θjc
Junction to case
θcs
Case to heatsink
θsa
Heatsink to ambient
θja
Junction to ambient (total)

Total thermal resistance: θja = θjc + θcs + θsa

Junction temperature: Tj = Ta + P × θja

Maximum power: P(max) = (Tj(max) - Ta) / θja

SOA Limits Explained

1. Maximum Current (IC(max))

Bond wire and metallization limits. Horizontal line on SOA.

2. Maximum Voltage (VCE(max))

Breakdown voltage limit. Vertical line on right side of SOA.

3. Power Limit (Thermal)

P = IC × VCE = constant. Diagonal line (slope -1 on log-log).

4. Second Breakdown

Current crowding failure at high VCE. Steeper diagonal below power limit.

Second Breakdown

A destructive failure mode unique to BJTs where current concentrates in hot spots:

Warning: Second breakdown can occur even within the thermal power limit! It's caused by:
  • Positive temperature coefficient of VBE (hotter areas conduct more)
  • Current crowding creates hot spots
  • Hot spots get hotter, drawing more current (thermal runaway)
  • Localized melting destroys the transistor

Most dangerous: High VCE + moderate IC

Safest: Low VCE (saturation mode)

MOSFETs don't suffer from second breakdown (positive temp coeff of RDS(on))

Thermal Runaway in Linear Operation

In linear (Class A/AB) amplifiers, BJTs can experience thermal runaway:

Mechanism: T↑ → VBE↓ → IC↑ → P↑ → T↑ (positive feedback!)

Prevention: Emitter degeneration resistor RE creates negative feedback

Stability criterion: ∂P/∂Tj < 1/θja

Thermal Interface Materials

Thermal Grease

θcs ≈ 0.1-0.3°C/W

Best thermal performance. Can be messy.

Thermal Pad (Silicone)

θcs ≈ 0.3-0.8°C/W

Clean, pre-cut. Good for production.

Mica + Grease

θcs ≈ 0.4-0.9°C/W

Electrical isolation. Classic solution.

Insulating Pad (Sil-Pad)

θcs ≈ 0.5-1.5°C/W

Electrical isolation + easy install.

Important: For TO-220, the metal tab is often connected to the collector. Use isolation if the heatsink is grounded or shared with other transistors at different potentials.

Paralleling Power Transistors

For higher current, parallel transistors with emitter ballast resistors:

Why ballast resistors?

VBE decreases ~2mV/°C. Hotter transistor conducts more → gets hotter (runaway!)

RE creates negative feedback: More IC → More VE → Less VBE → Less IC

Rule of thumb: RE × IC ≈ 100-200mV for good current sharing
