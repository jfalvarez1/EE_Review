BJT Oscillators

Oscillators generate periodic signals without an external input. Understanding BJT oscillators provides insight into feedback, stability, and signal generation - foundational concepts for RF, audio, and clock circuits.

Barkhausen Criterion
Loop Gain ≥ 1, Phase = 0° or 360°
Common Types
Colpitts, Hartley, Crystal
Industry Use: Local oscillators in receivers, clock generation, VCOs in PLLs, RF signal sources.

Barkhausen Oscillation Criteria

For sustained oscillation at frequency f:

Magnitude
|A × β| ≥ 1
Phase
∠(A × β) = 0° (or 360°, 720°...)

Where A is amplifier gain and β is feedback factor. At startup, gain must exceed 1; limiting mechanisms (saturation, AGC) then stabilize amplitude.

Colpitts Oscillator

How It Works

  1. L and C1-C2 form resonant tank at fosc
  2. C1-C2 capacitive divider provides feedback
  3. Feedback voltage applied to base via Cc
  4. BJT provides gain to sustain oscillation
Frequency
f = 1/(2π√(L×Ceq))
Ceq
C1×C2/(C1+C2)
Gain Requirement
gm > C2/C1 × (losses)

Hartley Oscillator

Characteristics

  • Uses tapped inductor for feedback
  • L1-L2 may be mutual-coupled (autotransformer)
  • Complementary to Colpitts (inductive divider vs capacitive)
Frequency
f = 1/(2π√((L1+L2)×C))
Feedback Ratio
β = L2/(L1+L2)

Design Considerations

Aspect Consideration
Startup Gain > 1 needed; noise initiates oscillation
Amplitude Control AGC or transistor limiting prevents saturation
Phase Noise Higher Q → lower phase noise
Frequency Stability Temperature compensation, buffer stage
Harmonics Class C operation improves efficiency but adds harmonics
