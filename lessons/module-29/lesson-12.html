PC, Server and Aerospace Power Implementation

The physics of a capacitor does not change between a laptop and a satellite. Load behavior, cooling, fault consequences, assembly constraints and qualification do. Use the comparisons below to construct requirements; they are application patterns, not a universal bill of materials for each industry.

Coupled inductors, TLVR and additional phases

Begin with the coupled-inductor regulator lesson, including winding dots and the inductance matrix. A coupled-inductor multiphase buck and a TLVR are related magnetic approaches but not interchangeable circuit names. In TLVR, coupled secondary windings form a series compensation loop with Lc.

TI's TLVR seminar includes circuit equations and a comparative design example reporting more than 40% capacitor reduction under that example's requirements. This is a topology/design result for the stated conditions, not a promise to remove 40% of any board's capacitors. TI SLUP413: Introduction to TLVR.

  • Implement the magnetic network. Verify dot polarity, leakage/coupling, saturation, loss and thermal data. For TLVR, route the secondary/Lc loop as an intentional circuit. Include its stray inductance in the design; do not accidentally treat the specified Lc as unwanted wiring inductance.
  • Give every phase a local input path. Interleaving can reduce aggregate ripple while each power stage still switches current locally. Share the output rail through adequate copper and preserve phase-current sensing integrity.
  • Test transitions between phase counts. Include phase shedding, fast phase addition, minimum/maximum load and any fault state the controller supports. Equal average current does not prove equal transient stress.
  • Keep load decoupling in the analysis. Faster aggregate inductor response does not eliminate the package/board inductance between the regulator and die.

Concrete modern controller: TI's TPS536C5 supports two outputs with N + M ≤ 12 phases, native TLVR, dynamic phase shedding, fast phase addition, SVI3 and PMBus. Its layout, power-stage compatibility and configuration must come from the device design documentation. These features illustrate PC/server-class control requirements rather than aerospace approval. Modules and LDOs: integration changes the boundary

ADI's LTM4681 integrates four channels, specified as quad 31.25 A or a combined 125 A configuration, with digital power management. Its datasheet documents current sharing, phase assignment and external decoupling. A module reduces some internal layout work; external input loops, output copper, cooling, sense pickup and startup configuration remain board responsibilities. Follow its supported parallel arrangement rather than tying unrelated regulator outputs together. LTM4681 datasheet: decoupling, PolyPhase and layout.

An LDO is useful where its noise, PSRR, voltage headroom and thermal dissipation fit the rail. It can follow a switcher to supply a sensitive clock/RF/analog domain. Check the actual switching-frequency rejection and transient behavior with the chosen capacitors; “LDO” does not mean zero noise or unconditional stability. Power-domain load switches

A load switch controls the connection to a domain; it does not regulate that domain's voltage. Distinguish enable sequencing, controlled slew, current limiting, output discharge and reverse-current blocking. A device may implement some of these and omit others.

Inrush calculation: Iinrush ≈ Cdomain dV/dt + Iload during startup. Charging 100 µF to 3.3 V in 1 ms requires 0.33 A of capacitor current for a linear ramp, before load current. Slowing the ramp can reduce upstream droop, but must meet the load's allowed ramp time and reset sequencing. Include the effective capacitance of all downstream devices, not just the capacitor nearest the switch.

For the on state, ΔV ≈ I Ron and P ≈ I²Ron; use the resistance at operating temperature and input voltage. For shutdown, verify reverse conduction, discharge paths and I/O backpower. A retained output or external interface can energize a supposedly disabled domain.

As a concrete example, TPS22912C provides controlled turn-on and reverse-current protection with conditions detailed in its datasheet. Do not infer that every load switch blocks reverse current in every enabled/disabled/unpowered state. TI's SLVA730 compares these cases. TPS22912C family datasheet; SLVA730: reverse current in load switches.

Place the switch's required input bypass locally and distribute the switched domain through a low-resistance path. Keep required load decoupling downstream. Do not turn an upstream regulator's remote-sense node into an uncontrolled floating node when the switch opens; the complete off-state circuit must be analyzed.

Choose a documented controller/module and a documented load. Make three requirement sheets: a compact PC, a server board and an airborne or space unit (choose which). Keep device capability claims tied to its datasheet; do not declare a catalog device qualified for the third application.

  1. Specify input range, load-step size/edge rate, voltage budget, operating modes, ambient/cooling, height and reliability objectives.
  2. Propose phase count/topology, capacitor candidates, local/remote sense pickup, domain switches and a manufacturable stackup. Document what is a calculation, vendor result or assumption.
  3. Simulate the complex PDN and controller response with part/bias/temperature corners. Record startup, load release, phase transitions and domain enable/disable behavior. Compare equal performance requirements rather than equal capacitor counts.
  4. Validate load-port voltage, phase currents, temperature, impedance and EMI with documented fixtures and bandwidth. For the aerospace case, add the project's required qualification and parts/process evidence.
  5. Deliver a source-linked design review with unresolved risks. A clean nominal waveform does not resolve absent radiation, mechanical or process qualification data.
What should change between the three designs?

Only what the stated requirements and evidence justify. A server may need more phases and telemetry; a laptop may emphasize height and sleep leakage; a space system may require different parts and qualification. These are hypotheses to test against a requirement sheet, not automatic rules based on the product label.
