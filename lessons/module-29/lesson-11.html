Stackups, Vias and Assembly Constraints

A layout is only useful if the stackup carries the intended return current and the factory can repeatedly build it. Read the stackup fundamentals first. Here the question is how to connect a dense PMIC/BGA and capacitor bank through real layers without losing the intended electrical behavior.

Each fast signal needs a nearby, continuous reference conductor. Ground is often the simplest reference to maintain between layers. A solid power plane can also carry AC return current; a change of reference requires a suitable high-frequency transfer path. Do not treat the words “power” or “ground” as substitutes for examining the electromagnetic geometry.

Rick Hartley's Altium discussion emphasizes reference adjacency and planning return paths in the stackup. It is expert design guidance, not measured approval of every board with the same layer names. Altium OnTrack: PCB stackup practices with Rick Hartley.

Loop inductance is not a via-count lookup: for two coupled outgoing/return paths, Lloop = Lout + Lreturn − 2M with consistent current directions. Spacing, depth and mutual coupling matter. Parallel vias share common spreading and pad inductance, so dividing “1 nH per via” by the via count is not a reliable extraction.

Plane capacitance check: an ideal 50 mm × 50 mm overlap, εr = 4 and 0.10 mm dielectric gives C = ε0 εr A/h ≈ 0.885 nF. At 0.01 mm, the same ideal calculation gives 8.85 nF. These assumed geometries illustrate scaling; they do not select a manufacturable dielectric or model a distributed plane resonance. Plane capacitance can be useful at high frequency but does not replace tens of µF of local energy storage.

0201 DFM and corners

For each candidate, give the assembler the actual manufacturer land pattern, body dimensions, pad spacing, mask and paste apertures, copper thickness, via treatment and surrounding heights. Ask it to review solder-volume balance, wetting timing, tombstoning, inspection and rework access. A package smaller on the BOM can need a larger practical courtyard than an idealized sketch suggests.

Unequal copper connected to the two pads changes thermal behavior during reflow; the shortest electrical route and the best assembly process must be reconciled. Avoid placing brittle MLCCs in high board-strain regions without mechanical review. Board edges, slots, mounting screws, connectors and depanelization features deserve attention. A universal corner rotation or fixed edge distance cannot be derived without the board support and strain conditions.

Published simulation: Murata's downsizing study reports relative principal stress of 100, 132 and 183 for its 0603, 0402 and 0201 inch examples. Conditions include 1 mm wiring width, 35 µm copper, a 1.6 mm FR-4 board and the 2 mm imposed displacement described on page 6. These are FEM results, not measured failure probabilities or a universal package ranking. Murata: Points of Downsizing MLCCs, pages 6–7.

Lab — release a stackup and via specification

Take the PMIC floorplan from the previous lessons and produce a layer-by-layer review. For every power/return and signal transition, identify the exact planes reached. Mark solderable via-in-pad locations separately from ordinary stitching vias.

  1. Obtain a fabricator's proposed materials, finished dielectric thicknesses, copper weights and qualified via structures. Do not substitute generic FR-4 numbers for its impedance calculation.
  2. Check the fastest interface's reference continuity, then the rail's resistance, loop inductance and current-density bottlenecks. Ground fills must be connected; inspect the final refill.
  3. Review buildup symmetry and copper balance for warpage, and stack/stagger choices for process reliability. Select the relevant qualification coupons with the supplier.
  4. Review the capacitor assembly panel with the contract manufacturer: stencil, paste, placement, reflow, inspection and depanelization. Record any constrained part orientations.
  5. Attach the accepted stackup, via table, impedance requirements, manufacturing notes and electrical validation plan to the release. “DRC clean” alone does not establish any of these.

Evidence to retain: supplier stackup revision, fabrication acceptance results, assembly yield, cross-section/qualification data where required, load-port impedance and thermal/load-step results. A process capability claim without the qualified construction is incomplete.
