PMIC and BGA Power Layout

A capacitor is connected to a power/return port, not just to a power net. Place it by the current path it serves. The nearest visible package edge may be farther electrically than an underside capacitor with a short power/ground via pair.

Floorplan a large package before routing

  1. Map each rail and return. Mark VIN/PGND pairs, switch nodes, analog supplies, reference/feedback pins, sense pairs and thermal land. Do not merge distinct supply rails because adjacent balls look convenient.
  2. Reserve the fast loops. For each buck phase, reserve the local input capacitor and switching power stage loop. Keep switch-node copper sufficient for current and heat but avoid unnecessary exposed area near sensitive circuitry.
  3. Reserve escape and assembly space. Lay out the actual pad/via geometry, mask clearance, capacitor courtyard and component heights. A drawn rectangle labeled “0201 bank” does not prove that it can be assembled.
  4. Allocate output distribution. Inductor outputs join the regulated rail. Place required regulator output capacitors at its specified sense/output region and local load decoupling at the load's power/return access points.
  5. Route quiet sensing separately. Use the controller's specified ground reference and sense pickup. Avoid routing underneath a switching inductor or beside a noisy gate/switch trace.

Do not force radial symmetry. At a package corner, two edges may offer more escape options, but soldermask, power balls, signal escape and mounting courtyards decide what fits. A board corner or depanelization edge introduces mechanical constraints; it is a different problem from a package corner. Review both with the fabricator and assembler.

Straight, T, Y, V or plane fill?

These are geometric descriptions, not impedance classes. At DC compare copper resistance and current density. At fast edges compare the complete loop, shared inductance, reference continuity and coupling. A short wide branch with a nearby return can outperform a visually straight long trace.

ConnectionUseful propertyTradeoff to verify
Short direct connection to a ball/pinFew segments; practical for accessible edge pinsIts return may be remote; inner balls may require vias and escape routing. Do not make a high-current rail a long narrow trace just to keep it straight.
T branch from a common feedSimple fanout; capacitor can join near the loadThe common neck carries combined current. Its impedance couples loads, and a long capacitor stub adds loop inductance.
Y or V splitCan distribute available copper into separate branchesAngles alone do not remove the common stem or return path. Branch imbalance, pad entry and manufacturable widths determine performance.
Wide plane/polygon fillMany parallel current paths, low DC resistance, natural BGA rail distributionVia antipads, slots and rail islands can create bottlenecks. Check the actual filled copper and its adjacent return plane, not the outline before refill.
Via-in-pad / underside decouplingCan access interior balls with little lateral travelRequires a qualified filled/capped via process where solder would otherwise wick; layer depth and return-via geometry still contribute inductance.

For ordinary two-terminal shunt capacitors, both tee and feed-through pad routing can work when the loop is short; neither is categorically forbidden. A true three-terminal feedthrough capacitor has a specified input/output/ground topology that must be followed. Do not apply a two-terminal routing rule to it.

Quantify the shared neck: ΔV = L × ΔI/Δt. A 4 A change in 100 ns through 0.625 nH produces 25 mV. The inductance is a budget for the relevant loop, not a universal “maximum trace length.” Use the shared-inductance control to see the penalty. It does not assign invented nH values to T, Y or V layouts.

How close is close enough?

A capacitor does not become useless at a universal distance, nor does its dielectric suddenly stop working. At the load port, its branch includes the mounting and distribution impedances. At increasing frequency that path may become too inductive or introduce phase shift to suppress the disturbance. It can still supply lower-frequency charge.

AMD's UltraScale PCB guide explains the roles of lateral plane inductance and the phase relationship between the noise source and mounted capacitor. Its device recommendations are more specific than “put everything within 1 mm.” Use an IC's stated placement limits when supplied, and verify performance at the actual load port. AMD UG583: Capacitor Placement Background.

Small loop area matters on both sides of the capacitor. Moving the power pad closer while sending the ground pad around a keepout can worsen the loop. Adding several vias helps only if the connected planes and common copper do not dominate. More vias also remove plane copper through their antipads.

It isolates placement distance; it does not assign that result to T, Y or V routing. The LTspice experiment below isolates an assumed distribution inductance instead.

Controlled circuit experiment: a 1 V source with 5 mΩ source resistance feeds a 100 µF bulk branch, then a 5 mΩ distribution resistance and Lpath of 1, 10 or 30 nH. The load has 10 µF local capacitance. Each capacitor branch has 10 mΩ ESR and 1 nH ESL. Load current rises from 0 to 4 A at 2 µs and falls at 12 µs, with 100 ns raised-cosine edges. This is a passive distribution model; there is no active regulator loop.

The minimum load voltages were 0.956, 0.896 and 0.805 V for 1, 10 and 30 nH respectively. These deliberately visible differences demonstrate sensitivity to assumed L. They are not extracted values for straight, T, Y or V routes.   2. Produce three CAD candidates: direct edge placement, an underside bank with paired vias, and a plane-fed bank. Hold stackup, part numbers and load ports constant. Include pad, via, plane and package connections.
  3. Extract DC resistance and a multiport impedance model with the same port/reference definitions for all candidates. Record mesh convergence and which package model was included. Without extraction, report a geometry comparison, not simulated milliohms.
  4. Compare load-port Z and transfer impedance between rails/loads. Inspect current density at necks and via fields. A low capacitor-terminal impedance can coexist with a poor load-port impedance.
  5. Build a coupon or board variant, calibrate the fixture to a stated plane, and compare the extracted result with impedance and load-step measurements. Keep the same probe connection and bandwidth for A/B tests.
Review prompt: can every topology be achieved?

No. Ball pitch, internal rail assignment, layer count, via aspect ratio, assembly clearances, thermal path and neighboring signals constrain the solution. If a candidate needs an unqualified via process or blocks escape routing, it is not an available design option. Record the constraint instead of pretending every board can use the same ideal drawing.
