Regulator Capacitors and Feedback

The capacitors beside a switching IC, beside its inductor and beside the powered processor perform different jobs. Start by identifying where current is discontinuous, where energy is stored and where voltage is sensed. This lesson connects multiphase and coupled-inductor regulators to the PCB.

A first-pass buck calculation

Illustrative assumptions: ideal 12 V to 1 V buck, 8 A output, 1 MHz switching, continuous conduction, negligible inductor ripple for the input estimate. D = Vout/Vin = 1/12. These are analytical sizing estimates, not a recommended converter design.

  • ICin,rms ≈ Iout√[D(1−D)] = 2.21 A RMS. Check the bank's actual current sharing and thermal data.
  • ΔVin ≈ Iout D(1−D)/(fs Cin). For a 100 mV capacitive ripple allowance, Cin ≥ 6.11 µF effective. ESR/ESL spikes and source dynamics need their own budget.
  • With triangular inductor ripple ΔIL = 2 A peak-to-peak and Cout = 22 µF effective, ΔVC ≈ ΔIL/(8 fs Cout) = 11.36 mV. A 5 mΩ ESR contributes about 10 mV peak-to-peak; adding these gives a conservative first estimate of 21.36 mV. ESL and loop dynamics are excluded.

The output ripple derivation is charge integration over a triangular capacitor current, not a load-step calculation. During an assumed 4 A deficit lasting 1 µs, a separate 40 mV charge budget needs C ≥ ΔIΔt/ΔV = 100 µF effective. A controller that restores current sooner changes the answer. Adding this capacitance requires rechecking stability and startup.

These buck sizing relationships and their assumptions are developed in TI SLVA477, Basic Calculation of a Buck Converter's Power Stage. Multiphase ripple cancellation depends on duty ratio, phase timing and sharing; do not simply divide every capacitor requirement by the number of phases.

Where do feedback resistors and capacitors go?

Sense the intended regulated node. In the buck template that is after the output inductor, at the specified output-capacitor/load sense pickup. The node before the inductor is SW: a switching waveform, not the ordinary feedback voltage. Do not add a feedback tap there unless implementing an explicitly documented current/ripple sensing topology.

Keep the high-impedance FB node short by placing the divider near the feedback pin. Route its VOUT connection as a quiet Kelvin sense trace from the specified pickup, away from switching fields, and route its bottom connection to the prescribed quiet reference. If the controller supports differential remote sensing, take both sense leads to the designated load point. Local sensing and remote sensing require different loop and fault analyses.

A feedforward capacitor Cff goes across Rtop in the template. For ideal components the divider transfer is (Rbot/(Rtop+Rbot)) × (1+s Rtop Cff)/(1+s (Rtop∥Rbot) Cff): a zero at 1/(2π Rtop Cff), followed by a pole at 1/(2π(Rtop∥Rbot)Cff). It changes loop gain and phase. A capacitor from FB to ground does something different; an arbitrary extra capacitor can harm stability. TI SLVA289, feedforward-capacitor design and transient comparisons.

Compensation capacitors on COMP, LDO noise-reduction capacitors, bootstrap capacitors and switch-node snubbers are different networks. Their names do not make them interchangeable with Cout or Cff. Follow the chosen device's circuit and layout. In particular, a shunt capacitor on SW is not an extra output capacitor.

LDOs, RF rails and EMC filtering

AN-1099 shows measured ADP151-3.3 load steps from 1 mA to 200 mA at 500 mA/µs, with 5 V input, comparing 1, 10 and 20 µF output capacitance. This is useful evidence that capacitance changes transient behavior, not permission to fit any value to any LDO. ADI AN-1099, Figures 1–3 and Table 1.

For a modern low-noise example, study the TPS7A94's input/output and noise-reduction networks, PSRR curves versus frequency and headroom, and thermal conditions. A quoted low-frequency PSRR is not its rejection at a switching harmonic. An LDO also dissipates approximately (Vin−Vout)Iout. TI TPS7A94 datasheet.

Use the template above to annotate a selected converter's evaluation-board schematic. Identify input hot loop, output energy storage, local load decoupling and Kelvin sense pickup. Overlay the board layout and trace both outgoing and return paths. Record the controller's permitted Cout/ESR range before proposing a change.

Measure a load step at the regulator output and the load simultaneously with short probe connections. Report probe bandwidth, load edge rate and operating mode. Separate immediate inductive/ESR error from charge deficit and recovery. Then change only load-local capacitance or distribution geometry and repeat. If adding Cff, use the vendor's method and verify stability across input, load and capacitor corners.

Should both the inductor output and load have capacitors?

Often yes: the regulator needs its local validated output network, while the distant load needs local transient current. Their interconnect creates a coupled network with additional resonances. Total µF alone does not determine the response, and removing either bank requires device-specific analysis and measurement.
