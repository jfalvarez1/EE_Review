Soldering and Rework Techniques

Proper soldering is fundamental for prototyping and production. This lesson covers hand soldering techniques, SMD rework, common defects, and industry standards (IPC). Mastering these skills directly impacts circuit reliability and debug efficiency.

Good Solder Joint
Shiny, concave fillet, complete wetting
IPC Standard
IPC-A-610 (workmanship), J-STD-001 (process)

Soldering Iron Selection

Type Power Best For Examples
Temperature-Controlled Station 60-80W General PCB work, SMD Hakko FX-888D, Weller WE1010
High-Power Station 100-150W Ground planes, large connectors JBC CD-2B, Metcal MX-5000

Tip Selection: Use the largest tip that fits the joint. Larger tips have more thermal mass and recover temperature faster after contact.

SMD Soldering Techniques

Package Technique Tips
0603, 0402 Passives Tin one pad, place with tweezers, solder other pad Use fine tip (conical or chisel 1mm)
SOIC, TSSOP Tack corner pins, then drag solder with flux Use flux generously; wick bridges
QFP (fine pitch) Align carefully, tack corners, drag/flood Microscope recommended; flux essential
QFN (no-lead) Stencil paste + hot air or reflow oven Ground pad needs paste; check for shorts
BGA Reflow only (hot air or oven) Proper profile critical; X-ray inspection
ESD Precautions: Always use a grounded wrist strap and ESD-safe mat when handling CMOS, MOSFETs, and sensitive ICs. Even brief static discharge can cause latent damage.

Rework Procedures

Component Removal
Hot air for SMD; solder sucker/wick for THT
Pad Cleaning
Solder wick + flux; IPA for residue

Hot Air Profile for Lead-Free:

  1. Preheat board to 150°C (slow ramp, ~2°C/s)
  2. Increase to 217°C (Sn/Ag/Cu liquidus)
  3. Peak at 245-260°C for 10-30 seconds
  4. Cool slowly to prevent thermal shock

Solder Types

Alloy Melting Point Use Notes
Sn63/Pb37 183°C Aerospace, medical (exempt) Eutectic, easy to work
SAC305 (Sn/Ag/Cu) 217-220°C RoHS-compliant production Higher temp, duller appearance
Sn/Bi (low-temp) 138°C Temperature-sensitive parts, rework Brittle; avoid mixing with lead
Flux Types: No-clean flux is standard for production. Water-soluble requires cleaning. Rosin requires IPA cleaning for high-reliability applications.

Common Defects and Causes

Defect Appearance Cause Fix
Cold Joint Dull, grainy, cracked Insufficient heat, movement during cooling Reheat with fresh solder and flux
Bridge Solder connects adjacent pins Too much solder, insufficient flux Add flux, wick away excess
Tombstone Passive stands on end Uneven heating during reflow Improve thermal symmetry in layout
Solder Ball Loose sphere near joint Outgassing, excessive paste Clean and reduce paste volume
Lifted Pad Copper separated from FR4 Excessive heat, mechanical stress Epoxy repair or scrap board
Void Hollow inside joint (X-ray) Outgassing, trapped flux Improve profile, reduce paste
