Flyback and Isolated Topologies

When galvanic isolation is required between input and output, transformer-coupled topologies are used. The flyback is the simplest isolated topology, essentially a buck-boost with a coupled inductor.

Flyback Output
Vout = Vin × (Ns/Np) × D/(1−D)
Forward Output
Vout = Vin × (Ns/Np) × D
Key Insight: The flyback stores energy in the transformer's magnetizing inductance during switch-on and transfers it to the secondary during switch-off. It's a buck-boost with isolation and multiple outputs possible.

Flyback Converter Topology

The flyback uses a coupled inductor (transformer) to store energy on the primary during switch-on and transfer it to the secondary during switch-off:

Switch ON
Primary magnetizes, secondary diode blocked
Switch OFF
Energy "flies back" to secondary
Isolation
Galvanic isolation via transformer

Flyback Design Considerations

CCM vs DCM
DCM simpler control, CCM lower peak currents
Leakage Inductance
Causes voltage spike—need snubber or clamp
Voltage Stress: The primary switch sees Vin + Vout×(Np/Ns) + leakage spike. For offline (120/240VAC) designs, use 600V or 800V MOSFETs with appropriate derating.
