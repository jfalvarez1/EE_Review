Current Sensing Techniques with MOSFETs

Accurate current measurement is essential for protection, regulation, and monitoring in power circuits. Multiple techniques exist, each with tradeoffs between accuracy, cost, power loss, and bandwidth. Understanding these methods is critical for power supply and motor drive design.

Why Measure Current?

  • Overcurrent protection: Prevent device destruction
  • Current-mode control: Improve transient response
  • Power metering: Efficiency monitoring
  • Motor control: Torque and commutation
  • Battery management: State of charge estimation

Sense Resistor (Shunt) Method

The most common and accurate method: insert a precision resistor in series with the current path and measure the voltage drop.

Rds(on) Sensing

Use the MOSFET itself as a sense resistor - eliminates the power loss of an external shunt but requires temperature compensation.

Rds(on) Temperature Compensation

Rds(on) Temperature Model

Rds(Tj) = Rds(25°C) × [1 + TC × (Tj - 25°C)]

Typical temperature coefficient (TC) is 0.4-0.5%/°C for standard silicon MOSFETs.

At 125°C: Rds ≈ 1.5 × Rds(25°C)

Current Sensing in Switch-Mode Supplies

Leading Edge Blanking

When the high-side MOSFET turns on, large current spikes occur due to capacitor charging and reverse recovery. Blanking ignores the sense signal during this time.
