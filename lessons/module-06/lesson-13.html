Battery Charging Systems

Battery charging requires careful control of current and voltage to maximize battery life while ensuring safe operation. Different chemistries have different charging profiles and safety requirements.

Li-Ion/LiPo Characteristics

  • Nominal: 3.6-3.7V per cell
  • Charged: 4.2V ± 50mV per cell
  • Discharged: 2.8-3.0V minimum
  • Typical C-rate: 0.5C to 1C charge

Safety Hazards

  • Overcharge: Thermal runaway, fire
  • Overdischarge: Copper dendrites, capacity loss
  • Overcurrent: Internal heating, venting
  • Temperature: Charge only 0-45°C

Li-Ion CC-CV Charging Profile

Li-Ion batteries use a Constant Current / Constant Voltage (CC-CV) charging algorithm. Current is held constant until voltage reaches 4.2V, then voltage is held constant while current tapers.

Phase Control Mode Duration Charge Delivered
Pre-conditioning Low current (C/10) if V < 2.8V ~30 min max ~5%
Constant Current (CC) Fixed current (0.5C-1C typical) ~1 hour @ 1C ~75%
Constant Voltage (CV) Fixed voltage (4.2V/cell) 1-2 hours ~20%
Termination Current drops below C/10 End of charge 100%

Linear Charger Circuit

Simple linear chargers are cost-effective for low-power applications. They dissipate excess power as heat.

Switching Charger for Higher Power

For higher power or when efficiency matters, switching chargers use a buck converter topology.

Feature Linear Charger Switching Charger
Efficiency 50-80% (drops as VIN increases) 85-95%
Max Current ~1A (thermal limited) 3-6A typical, higher with cooling
Cost $ (few external parts) $$ (inductor, MOSFETs)
Size Small Medium (inductor dominates)
Noise None (DC) Switching ripple (filter at fsw)
Best for Single cell, <500mA, cost sensitive Multi-cell, high current, efficiency critical
