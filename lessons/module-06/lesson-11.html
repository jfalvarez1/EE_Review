MOSFET as a Switch

MOSFETs are the dominant power switch technology due to their high input impedance, fast switching, and lack of second breakdown. Understanding gate drive, RDS(on), and switching losses is essential for efficient power conversion.

Key Advantage: MOSFETs are voltage-controlled (no DC gate current), have very low RDS(on), and don't suffer from BJT-like second breakdown. Gate charge determines switching speed.

Key MOSFET Parameters

Vth (Threshold Voltage)

VGS where drain current begins to flow.

Typical: 1-4V (standard), 0.5-1.5V (logic-level)

RDS(on) (On Resistance)

Drain-source resistance when fully enhanced.

Increases with temperature (~0.4%/°C)

Qg (Total Gate Charge)

Charge needed to switch gate fully ON.

Determines switching speed and drive current

Ciss, Coss, Crss

Input, output, and reverse transfer capacitances.

Ciss = Cgs + Cgd, affects switching

Miller Plateau: During the plateau, VGS stays constant while Cgd discharges. This is when VDS is falling. The plateau length determines how long the MOSFET spends in the linear region (where losses are highest).

Total Power Loss

MOSFET losses have two components that scale differently with frequency:

Conduction loss: Pcond = ID² × RDS(on) × D (independent of frequency)

Switching loss: Psw ≈ ½ × VDS × ID × (tr + tf) × f (proportional to frequency)

Gate drive loss: Pg = Qg × VGS × f (proportional to frequency)

Bootstrap Gate Drive

For high-side N-channel MOSFETs, a bootstrap circuit provides VGS above VCC:

Operation:

1. Low-side ON: Cboot charges through Dboot to VCC

2. High-side ON: Cboot provides gate voltage (source rises to VCC)

3. VGS = Vcboot ≈ VCC - Vf ≈ 12V

Limitation: Bootstrap only works with switching. For 100% duty cycle, use a charge pump or isolated supply.

RDS(on) Temperature Coefficient

Unlike BJTs, MOSFET RDS(on) increases with temperature - this provides natural current sharing:

Typical: RDS(on) at 125°C ≈ 1.5-2× RDS(on) at 25°C

Benefit: Hot spots conduct less current (negative feedback)

Design: Always use RDS(on) at operating Tj, not 25°C spec!

Logic-Level vs Standard MOSFETs

Logic-Level (3.3V/5V compatible)
  • Vth ≈ 0.5-1.5V
  • Full RDS(on) at VGS = 4.5V
  • Direct MCU drive (3.3V/5V)
  • Slightly higher RDS(on)
  • Lower max VGS (±8V to ±12V)
Standard (10V gate drive)
  • Vth ≈ 2-4V
  • Full RDS(on) at VGS = 10V
  • Needs level shifter from MCU
  • Lower RDS(on) available
  • Higher max VGS (±20V)
Selection Guide: Use logic-level for simple designs. Use standard when you need lowest RDS(on) or have a dedicated gate driver.
