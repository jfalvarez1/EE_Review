Body Diode and Synchronous Rectification

Every power MOSFET contains an intrinsic body diode formed by the P-N junction between body and drain. Understanding this diode's behavior is critical for synchronous rectification, where MOSFETs replace Schottky diodes for higher efficiency in power converters.

Body Diode Characteristics

Parameter Typical Value Implication
Vf (forward voltage) 0.7-1.2V Higher than Schottky (~0.3-0.5V)
trr (reverse recovery) 50-500ns Slow, causes losses at turn-off
Qrr (recovery charge) 50-5000nC Energy lost during commutation
di/dt (recovery) 100-1000 A/μs Can cause voltage spikes
Reverse Recovery: The body diode's slow reverse recovery is a major source of switching losses in hard-switched converters. It causes current spikes when the complementary switch turns on.

Synchronous Rectification

Efficiency improvement:
Diode: Vf × I = 0.4V × 10A = 4W
Sync FET: I² × Rdson = 100A² × 10mΩ = 1W
→ 75% reduction in rectifier loss

Dead-Time and Body Diode Conduction

Optimal Dead-Time: Too short → shoot-through risk. Too long → body diode conduction loss. Typical values: 20-100ns. Some controllers use adaptive dead-time.
