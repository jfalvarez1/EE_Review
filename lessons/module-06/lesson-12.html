Power Sequencing and Hot-Swap

Complex systems with multiple supply rails require controlled power-up sequences to prevent latch-up, ESD damage, and functional failures. Hot-swap circuits enable safe insertion of boards into live backplanes.

Why Sequence Power?

  • Latch-up prevention: I/O before core can trigger SCR
  • FPGA/CPU requirements: Core → auxiliary → I/O
  • Analog stability: Reference before amplifier
  • Inrush current: Staged startup reduces peak

Hot-Swap Requirements

  • Inrush limiting: Slow capacitor charging
  • Overcurrent protection: Fault detection
  • Undervoltage lockout: Wait for valid supply
  • Connector make-before-break: Ground first

Power Sequencing Strategies

Different systems require different sequencing approaches based on the type of ICs being powered.

Sequencer IC Architecture

Dedicated power sequencer ICs monitor rail voltages and control enable pins with programmable delays.

Hot-Swap Controller

Hot-swap controllers manage inrush current and protect the system during live board insertion. They use a series MOSFET to slowly charge bulk capacitance.

Power Good (PGOOD) Supervision

PGOOD signals indicate when power rails have stabilized within tolerance. They're essential for reliable system startup.
