MOSFET Switching

MOSFETs excel as switches due to their low on-resistance (RDS(on)) and voltage-controlled gate. Understanding gate charge and switching dynamics is critical for efficient power conversion.

On-Resistance
RDS(on) decreases with higher VGS
Gate Charge
Qg = Ciss × ΔVGS
Key Insight: Switching losses occur during transitions when both voltage and current are non-zero. Faster switching reduces losses but increases EMI. Gate drive strength controls this trade-off.

Gate Charge Curve

The gate charge (Qg) curve shows three distinct regions:

Region What Happens Voltage Behavior
Qgs Gate-source cap charges VGS rises to threshold
Qgd (Miller plateau) Gate-drain cap discharges VGS flat, VDS falls
Qg - Qgd - Qgs Final VGS reached VGS rises to drive voltage
Miller Effect: During the Miller plateau, the gate voltage is flat while VDS transitions. A weak gate driver extends this time, increasing switching losses dramatically.

Switching Loss Calculation

Switching Loss
Psw = ½ × VDS × ID × (tr + tf) × fsw
Conduction Loss
Pcond = ID² × RDS(on)

At high frequency, switching losses dominate. At high current, conduction losses dominate. Optimal efficiency requires balancing both.
