Body Effect & Back Gate (The 4th MOSFET Terminal)

A MOSFET is a four-terminal device: Gate, Drain, Source, and Body (Bulk/Substrate). In many IC circuits, the body is tied to a fixed potential (substrate or well), so when the source moves the source-to-body voltage VSB changes and the threshold voltage shifts. This is the body effect.

Threshold shift
Vth = Vth0 + γ(√(2φF + VSB) - √(2φF))
Body transconductance
gmb ≈ gm · γ / (2√(2φF + VSB))
Rule of thumb
gmb ≈ 0.1–0.3·gm
Practical impact
Source followers lose headroom as VS rises
Why it matters: Any time a MOSFET source is not fixed (source follower, pass device, switches), body effect changes bias points and gain. In analog ICs it also creates an extra small-signal path via gmb.

Example Circuit: NMOS Source Follower (Body at Ground)

As the source rises, VSB increases and Vth rises, reducing VGS - Vth. This is one reason source followers need headroom.
