MOSFET Selection for Power Applications

Selecting the right MOSFET for a power application requires balancing voltage rating, Rdson, gate charge, thermal performance, and cost. This lesson provides a systematic approach to MOSFET selection for switching regulators, motor drives, and other power circuits.

Primary Trade-off
Rdson vs Qg (lower one increases the other)
Figure of Merit
FOM = Rdson × Qg (lower is better)

Key Selection Parameters

Parameter Symbol Selection Criterion
Drain-Source Voltage Vds,max ≥ 1.5× max voltage in circuit (80% derating)
Drain Current Id,max ≥ 2× peak current (margin for thermals)
On-Resistance Rdson Determines conduction loss (I²R)
Gate Charge Qg Determines switching loss and driver requirements
Thermal Resistance θJC, θJA Must dissipate total losses without overheating
Package TO-220, D2PAK, QFN... Space, thermal, assembly constraints

Rdson vs Qg Trade-off

Figure of Merit: FOM = Rdson × Qg

Trade-off: Making Rdson lower (more parallel cells) increases Qg

Loss Analysis

sw Conduction Sw Medium fsw Balanced High fsw Switching Conduction (∝ Rdson) Switching (∝ Qg × fsw)
Conduction Loss: Pcond = I²rms × Rdson

Switching Loss: Psw ≈ ½ × Vds × Id × (trise + tfall) × fsw

Gate Drive Loss: Pgate = Qg × Vgs × fsw

Selection by Application

Application Priority MOSFET Choice
Low-side switch (DC) Low Rdson Large die, standard gate charge OK
Buck converter (high fsw) Low Qg, low Qgd Smaller die with good FOM
Motor drive (PWM) Balanced Rdson/Qg Consider body diode Qrr
Hot-swap SOA, linear mode Low Rdson, good spiral immunity
High voltage (>200V) Low Coss, Qgd Superjunction or GaN
