Linear Regulators

Linear regulators (LDOs - Low Dropout Regulators) provide clean, low-noise regulated voltage by dissipating excess power as heat. They're simple but inefficient at large voltage differentials.

Output Voltage
Vout = Vref × (1 + R1/R2)
Dropout Voltage
Vdropout = Vin(min) − Vout
Key Insight: Linear regulators dissipate (Vin - Vout) × Iload as heat. At 12V in and 3.3V out with 500mA load, that's 4.35W of wasted power! Use switching regulators for high efficiency.

LDO Internal Architecture

Inside an LDO, a pass transistor (PMOS for low dropout) is controlled by an error amplifier comparing a reference to a feedback voltage:

Pass Element
PMOS (LDO) or NPN (standard)
Error Amplifier
High gain for tight regulation
Reference
Bandgap (~1.2V, temp stable)

PSRR vs Frequency

Power Supply Rejection Ratio (PSRR) indicates how well the LDO attenuates input ripple. It decreases at higher frequencies:

Low Frequency
PSRR ≈ 60-80 dB (excellent rejection)
At Switcher Freq (100kHz+)
PSRR ≈ 20-40 dB (reduced rejection)
Post-Regulator Application: When using an LDO after a switching regulator to clean up ripple, check the PSRR at the switcher's frequency. A 40dB PSRR at 500kHz turns 50mVpp ripple into 0.5mVpp.

Key Specifications

Parameter Typical Range Why It Matters
Dropout Voltage 100mV - 1.5V Determines minimum Vin for regulation
PSRR (Power Supply Rejection) 40 - 80 dB @ 1kHz How much input ripple is attenuated
Output Noise 10 - 100 µVrms Critical for sensitive analog circuits
Quiescent Current 1µA - 5mA Power consumed with no load

Output Capacitor Selection

The output capacitor affects stability and transient response:

Minimum ESR
Some LDOs need ESR for stability (check datasheet)
Typical Value
10-47µF ceramic (X5R/X7R)
Ceramic Capacitor Warning: Ceramic caps have very low ESR. Some older LDOs require ESR in a specific range (0.1-1Ω) for stability. Modern LDOs are usually ceramic-stable.

When to Use LDO vs Switching

Use LDO When Use Switching When
Small Vin-Vout difference (<1V) Large Vin-Vout difference
Low noise required (analog, RF) High efficiency required
Low current (<100mA) High current
Simple design, low cost Battery life critical
