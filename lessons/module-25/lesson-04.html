Current Sensing Applications

Current sensing is essential for power management, motor control, battery monitoring, and protection circuits. This lesson covers shunt-based sensing techniques used in real products.

Industry Reference: Based on Analog Devices High-Side Current Sensing, Low-Side vs High-Side Sensing, and Microchip Current Sensing Guide.

High-Side vs Low-Side Sensing

Low-side shunt

Both examples show separate Kelvin leads, local supply bypass and REF grounded for positive unidirectional current. Vout = G × I × Rsense within the input and output ranges. Low-side sensing raises the load return; a ground fault can bypass that shunt. TI INA181 datasheet, Figure 7-4 and §8.

Section 2: Current Sense Amplifier Design

Current sense amplifiers (CSAs) are specialized amplifiers designed to measure small differential voltages in the presence of large common-mode voltages.

Section 3: Bidirectional Current Sensing

For applications like battery charging/discharging or motor control, current can flow in both directions. Bidirectional sensing requires special consideration.

Bidirectional CSA Configurations

Analog bidirectional measurement using INA181: Vout = Vref + G(VIN+ − VIN−). Drive REF from a low-impedance reference and choose gain, shunt and reference voltage so both current polarities stay inside the output swing limits. The battery represents a source or sink; the charging controller is omitted. INA181 datasheet, bidirectional operation. INA226 instead reports current digitally over I²C and has no analog REF input.

Reference Voltage: For bidirectional sensing, set Vref = VDD/2 to allow equal positive and negative current range. Some CSAs (INA226) use I2C and internal ADC, eliminating this concern.

Section 4: Power Measurement

For accurate power measurement, you need both voltage and current. Power monitor ICs multiply V × I internally.

INA226, INA3221, PAC1932
Typical Accuracy
±0.5-1% power, ±0.1% voltage

Practical Application: Motor Current Sensing

Three-phase inverter power-stage template with one low-side shunt per leg. Take Kelvin sense traces from each shunt and sample only in switching states that expose the required phase current. Gate drivers, bootstrap networks, dead time, current-sense amplifiers and intrinsic FET diodes are omitted here. For the full driver and power-stage connections see TI DRV8301 typical application; that IC includes two current-shunt amplifiers, so a third measurement requires another channel.
