EMI/EMC Filter Design

Electromagnetic Interference (EMI) compliance is a make-or-break requirement for any commercial product. Failed EMC testing can delay product launches by months and cost significant redesign effort. This lesson covers practical filter design techniques that help products pass conducted and radiated emissions testing on the first attempt.

Industry Applications

  • Consumer Electronics - FCC Part 15 / CE marking required
  • Industrial Equipment - CISPR 11 / EN 55011 Class A/B
  • Medical Devices - IEC 60601-1-2 (stringent EMC requirements)
  • Automotive - CISPR 25 (vehicle EMC)
  • Telecom - EN 300 386 / FCC Part 68

1. EMI Fundamentals

Emissions vs Immunity

Aspect Emissions Immunity
Definition Noise your product generates Ability to withstand external noise
Conducted 150kHz - 30MHz on power/signal cables ESD, EFT/Burst, Surge
Radiated 30MHz - 1GHz (6GHz for some standards) RF immunity, magnetic field immunity
Test Equipment LISN + spectrum analyzer / EMI receiver ESD gun, surge generator, RF amplifier

Conducted Emissions: Differential Mode vs Common Mode

Measuring DM and CM Noise

LISN measures noise on Line and Neutral. Convert to DM/CM:

\[ V_{DM} = \frac{V_L - V_N}{2} \]

\[ V_{CM} = \frac{V_L + V_N}{2} \]

In practice, both modes exist simultaneously. Identify the dominant mode by comparing Line and Neutral spectra - if they're similar magnitude and in-phase, it's mostly CM.

2. Regulatory Standards Overview

Standard Region Conducted Range Class A Limit Class B Limit

CISPR 32 / EN 55032 EU / International 150kHz - 30MHz 79-73 dBµV (QP) 66-60 dBµV (QP)
CISPR 11 / EN 55011 EU (Industrial) 150kHz - 30MHz 79-73 dBµV 66-60 dBµV
IEC 60601-1-2 Medical Devices 150kHz - 30MHz Varies by device classification

Class A vs Class B

  • Class A: Industrial/commercial environment. Less stringent limits (~10dB relaxed).
  • Class B: Residential environment. Stricter limits for consumer products.
  • Most consumer electronics must meet Class B. Failing Class B but passing Class A means you can only sell to commercial customers.
  • Design margin: Aim for 6dB below the limit to account for production variation.

3. EMI Filter Topology

A typical EMI input filter combines components to attenuate both DM and CM noise:

Component Functions

Component Purpose Typical Values Safety Class
X-Capacitor (Cx) Shunt DM noise between L and N 100nF - 1µF X1 (4kV), X2 (2.5kV)
Y-Capacitor (Cy) Shunt CM noise to earth 1nF - 4.7nF Y1 (8kV), Y2 (5kV)
Common-Mode Choke High impedance to CM current 1mH - 30mH Reinforced insulation
DM Inductor Series inductance for DM filtering 100µH - 1mH Current rating critical
MOV/TVS Transient voltage clamping 275V - 320V (for 230Vac) UL listed

Y-Capacitor Leakage Current Limits

Y-caps connect line/neutral to earth, creating leakage current:

\[ I_{leak} = 2\pi f \cdot C_Y \cdot V_{line} \]

For 2 × 2.2nF at 230V/50Hz: I = 2 × 2π × 50 × 2.2nF × 230 = 0.32mA

  • IEC 60950 (IT): 3.5mA max for Class I equipment
  • IEC 60601 (Medical): 0.5mA max for patient-connected devices
  • Double-insulated (Class II): No Y-caps to earth allowed!

4. Filter Design Calculations

Required Attenuation

Measure or estimate noise without filter, then calculate needed attenuation:

\[ A_{required} = V_{noise,measured} - V_{limit} + Margin \]

Example: 85dBµV measured, 66dBµV limit, 6dB margin → Need 25dB attenuation

LC Filter Attenuation

Single-Stage LC (2nd order)

Corner frequency: \( f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \)

Attenuation above fc: 40dB/decade (12dB/octave)

Two-Stage LC (4th order)

Attenuation above fc: 80dB/decade (24dB/octave)

More effective for steep rolloff, but beware of resonance peaks!

Common-Mode Choke Selection

The CMC provides high impedance to common-mode current while allowing differential-mode current to pass:

CM Impedance

\[ Z_{CM} = j\omega L_{CM} \]

At 150kHz with 10mH: |Z| = 2π × 150k × 10m = 9.4kΩ

Attenuation with Y-caps

\[ A_{CM} = 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{Z_{CM}}{Z_{Cy}}\right) \]

With Cy = 2.2nF at 150kHz: Zcy = 1/(2π × 150k × 2.2n) = 482Ω

A = 20 × log10(9400/482) = 26dB

CMC Selection Guidelines

  • Inductance: 1-30mH depending on attenuation needed
  • Current rating: Must handle full load current without saturation
  • Leakage inductance: Provides some DM filtering (bonus)
  • Self-resonant frequency: Should be above noise frequency of interest
  • Differential saturation current: Must exceed inrush and fault currents

5. Ferrite Beads

Ferrite beads are lossy inductors that convert high-frequency noise to heat. They're effective for frequencies above 10MHz where LC filters become less practical.

Ferrite Bead Impedance Characteristics

Ferrite Bead Model

At low frequencies: Primarily inductive (\(Z \approx j\omega L\))

At peak frequency: Maximum resistance (losses dominate)

At high frequencies: Capacitive parasitics reduce effectiveness

Selecting by Impedance at 100MHz

Datasheets specify |Z| at 100MHz. Match to noise frequency:

  • 30-100Ω: Light filtering, minimal signal impact
  • 100-600Ω: General purpose power supply filtering
  • 600-2000Ω: High attenuation for noisy digital supplies

Ferrite Bead Saturation

Ferrite beads lose inductance with DC bias current. Always check:

  • Rated DC current for acceptable impedance
  • AC+DC combined current for saturation margin
  • Use beads rated for at least 1.5× expected current

6. PCB Layout for EMI

Critical Layout Rules

Power Supply Layout

  • ☐ Minimize high-di/dt loop area (switch node to input cap)
  • ☐ Place input capacitors directly at MOSFET drain and source
  • ☐ Use wide, short traces for power paths
  • ☐ Ground plane under switching node minimizes radiation
  • ☐ Keep gate drive traces short and away from sensitive signals

EMI Filter Placement

  • ☐ Filter at input connector, before any branching
  • ☐ Y-capacitors close to chassis ground connection
  • ☐ Physical separation between input and output of filter
  • ☐ Ground plane under filter provides shielding

Signal Integrity

  • ☐ Route sensitive analog signals away from switching nodes
  • ☐ Use ground plane as shield between layers
  • ☐ Avoid routing signals over split planes
  • ☐ Add series resistors on fast digital outputs to slow edges

7. Shielding Basics

When Layout Isn't Enough

For radiated emissions above 30MHz, shielding may be necessary:

Shielding Method Effectiveness Application
Board-level shield can 20-60dB depending on openings Wireless modules, oscillators
Conductive enclosure 40-100dB for solid metal Full product shielding
Conductive gaskets Maintains shielding at seams Enclosure openings, doors
Ferrite sleeves on cables 5-20dB above 1MHz Cable CM filtering

Shielding Effectiveness

\[ SE = 20 \log_{10}\left(\frac{E_{without}}{E_{with}}\right) \text{ dB} \]

For a solid conductor, SE has three components:

\[ SE = A + R + B \]

A = Absorption loss, R = Reflection loss, B = Multiple reflection correction

Aperture Rule of Thumb

Openings in shields act as slot antennas. Maximum frequency for effective shielding:

\[ f_{max} = \frac{c}{2 \times L_{slot}} \]

For 1cm slot: fmax = 3×10⁸ / (2 × 0.01) = 15GHz

For 10cm slot: fmax = 1.5GHz

Keep ventilation holes small or use honeycomb shields!

8. Pre-Compliance Testing

LISN (Line Impedance Stabilization Network)

A LISN provides a defined 50Ω impedance to the DUT and couples conducted noise to a spectrum analyzer:

Near-Field Probes

Use magnetic (H-field) and electric (E-field) probes to locate noise sources on the PCB before formal testing:

  • H-field probe: Detects high di/dt loops (switch nodes, inductors)
  • E-field probe: Detects high dv/dt nodes (switch node voltage)
  • Probe the board while monitoring a spectrum analyzer to find "hot spots"

DIY Pre-Compliance

  • Use an SDR (software-defined radio) as a cheap spectrum analyzer
  • Build simple H-field probes from coax and small loops
  • Compare scans before/after layout changes or filter additions
  • Absolute accuracy isn't critical - relative changes guide optimization

9. Design Checklist

Conducted Emissions

  • ☐ Determine DM vs CM noise dominance
  • ☐ Calculate required attenuation from pre-scan
  • ☐ Size X-capacitors for DM filtering
  • ☐ Size Y-capacitors within leakage limits
  • ☐ Select CMC with adequate inductance and current rating
  • ☐ Consider DM inductors for additional attenuation

Radiated Emissions

  • ☐ Minimize high-di/dt loop areas
  • ☐ Use ground plane under switching circuits
  • ☐ Add series resistors to slow fast digital edges
  • ☐ Consider shielding for high-frequency sources
  • ☐ Use ferrite beads on cables leaving the enclosure

Safety Compliance

  • ☐ X and Y capacitors are safety rated
  • ☐ Total Y-cap leakage current within limits
  • ☐ CMC rated for reinforced insulation if bridging isolation
  • ☐ MOV/TVS rated for worst-case surge
  • ☐ Fuse rating coordinates with expected fault currents
