Every smartphone, laptop, and portable device requires a sophisticated battery management system. This lesson covers the complete BMS design including charging, protection, fuel gauging, and thermal management.

Section 1: CC-CV Charging Profile

Li-ion batteries require Constant-Current / Constant-Voltage (CC-CV) charging with precise voltage regulation to maximize capacity while preventing damage.

Charging Phases

Phase Condition Action Typical Duration
1. Pre-charge (Trickle) Vcell < 3.0V I = 0.1C (10% of full) 0-30 min
2. Fast Charge (CC) 3.0V < Vcell < 4.2V I = 0.5-1C constant 1-2 hours
3. Saturation (CV) Vcell = 4.2V V constant, I tapers 30-60 min
4. Termination I < 0.02-0.1C Charging stops —
Critical Voltage Accuracy: The 4.2V regulation must be within ±1% (±42mV). Under-voltage means reduced capacity; over-voltage causes accelerated degradation, gas generation, and fire risk.

Section 2: Battery Protection Circuit

Protection circuits prevent dangerous conditions that could damage the battery or cause fire/explosion. The protection IC monitors cell voltages and current, controlling FETs to disconnect the battery.

Protection Parameters

Protection Threshold Action Recovery
Overcharge (OVP) 4.25-4.35V/cell Open charge FET V < 4.1V or charger removed
Over-discharge (UVP) 2.5-3.0V/cell Open discharge FET Charger connected
Overcurrent (OCP) 3-10A (design-dependent) Open discharge FET Load removed or timeout
Short Circuit (SCP) 20-50A (fast, µs response) Immediate FET off Load removed + delay
Over-temperature 45-60°C (charge), 60-80°C (discharge) Reduce or stop charging Temp returns to safe range

Protection Circuit Schematic

Multi-Cell Protection with Balancing

For 2S+ packs, cell balancing is required to prevent one cell from reaching limits before others:

Passive Balancing
Bleed resistors dissipate energy from high cells (simple, lossy)
Active Balancing
Switch-mode transfer between cells (efficient, complex)
Balancing Current: Typical passive balancing uses 50-200mA bleed current. For a 3000mAh pack with 5% cell mismatch (150mAh), balancing time = 150mAh / 100mA = 1.5 hours.

Section 3: Fuel Gauge and SOC Estimation

Accurate state-of-charge (SOC) estimation is challenging because battery capacity varies with temperature, age, and discharge rate. Modern fuel gauges combine multiple techniques.

SOC Estimation Methods

Method Principle Accuracy Limitations
Coulomb Counting Integrate current over time: Q = ∫I dt ±2-5% Drift over time, needs calibration
OCV Lookup Map open-circuit voltage to SOC table ±5-10% Needs rest period (no load)
Kalman Filter Combine models with measurements ±1-3% Complex, tuning required
Impedance Tracking Correlate internal R with SOC/SOH ±3-5% Temp and age dependent
