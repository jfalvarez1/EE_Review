BLDC Motor Driver Design

Brushless DC (BLDC) motors power everything from drone propellers to electric vehicles. This lesson covers the complete motor driver design including gate drive, current sensing, and control algorithms.

Industry Reference: Based on TI DRV8301 Application Guide, Analog Devices BLDC Drive Power Stage, and Infineon Motor Control Solutions.

Three-Phase Inverter Architecture

Three-phase inverter power-stage template with one low-side shunt per leg. Take Kelvin sense traces from each shunt and sample only in switching states that expose the required phase current. Gate drivers, bootstrap networks, dead time, current-sense amplifiers and intrinsic FET diodes are omitted here. For the full driver and power-stage connections see TI DRV8301 typical application; that IC includes two current-shunt amplifiers, so a third measurement requires another channel.

Section 2: Gate Drive Design

The gate driver must provide sufficient current to switch the MOSFETs quickly while managing dead time to prevent shoot-through:

Bootstrap vs. Isolated Gate Drive

Topology Advantages Disadvantages Use Case
Bootstrap Simple, low cost, integrated 100% duty cycle limit, refresh needed Motor drives, general inverters
Charge Pump 100% duty cycle capable Limited current, more complex DC-DC, always-on switches
Isolated (transformer) Full isolation, 100% duty Expensive, larger, more parts High-voltage, safety critical
Dead Time Requirement: A dead time of 50-500ns between high-side turn-off and low-side turn-on (and vice versa) prevents shoot-through current. Too much dead time increases distortion; too little risks destruction.

Bootstrap Circuit Design

Bootstrap capacitor and driver supply template. Cboot is between VB and VS; it is not in series with the charging diode. Gate outputs must be referenced to the appropriate source node. TI bootstrap design guidance.

Section 3: Current Sensing

Accurate current sensing is essential for torque control and overcurrent protection:

Current Sensing Methods

Method Location Accuracy Cost Notes
DC Bus Shunt Single shunt, bus return Medium Low Requires reconstruction
Three Shunts Each low-side leg High Medium Most accurate for FOC
In-Line Shunts Phase outputs Highest High Best for high-performance
FET RDS(on) Low-side FETs Low Free Temperature dependent

Section 4: Control Algorithms

Six-Step Commutation (Trapezoidal)

Simple control using Hall sensors or back-EMF sensing. One phase is always off, one sourcing, one sinking.

Step Phase U Phase V Phase W Hall State (CBA)
1+-Z101
2+Z-100
3Z+-110
4-+Z010
5-Z+011
6Z-+001

+ = PWM high, - = PWM low (or low-side on), Z = both off (floating)

Field-Oriented Control (FOC)

Advanced control treating the motor as a DC machine in a rotating reference frame. Provides smooth torque and efficiency at all speeds.

The d- and q-axis PI controllers act on reference minus measured current. Inverse Park transforms their two voltage commands; SVPWM generates the inverter commands. Both Park transforms use electrical rotor angle. This view omits the outer speed loop, voltage limiting and decoupling terms. For the complete implementation and staged bring-up see TI Sensored Field Oriented Control of 3-Phase PMSM.
