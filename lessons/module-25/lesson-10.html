Wireless Charging (Qi) Design

Wireless power transfer enables convenient charging without cables. This lesson covers inductive coupling principles, Qi protocol, and circuit design for transmitters (TX) and receivers (RX).

Industry Reference: Based on TI Wireless Charging Design Guide, Analog Devices Wireless Charging Solutions, and Wireless Power Consortium (WPC) Qi specifications.

Wireless Power Transfer Principle

Power-flow overview. The transmitter drives a resonant coil; the receiver rectifies induced AC and regulates its output. The return and control connections are shown in the detailed examples below. TI bq500212A reference schematic and BQ51013B datasheet.

Section 1: TX (Transmitter) Design

Full-Bridge Inverter

Full-bridge resonant power-stage template: Q1 + Q4 apply one polarity to Cseries–Ltx; Q2 + Q3 apply the opposite polarity. Never turn on both devices in one leg together; the gate-driver implementation must provide suitable dead time and source-referenced high-side drive. The coupled receiver and intrinsic FET diodes are omitted from this stage view. Component values, drivers, current sensing and tuning must follow the selected coil/controller design; see TI SLUUAP8B transmitter schematic.

Section 2: RX (Receiver) Design

Receiver power-flow blocks, with the resonant input feeding both AC terminals of the rectifier. BQ51013B integrates a synchronous rectifier and LDO; its implementation also needs the specified resonant, bootstrap, communication and output capacitors. Build from BQ51013B datasheet, Typical Application, which shows those connections in full.

Key RX Design Considerations

Parameter Typical Value Design Impact
RX Coil Inductance 6-12 µH Must resonate with TX frequency range

Output Voltage 5V or 9V for fast charge Direct to battery charger IC
Overvoltage Protection Clamp at 20-25V Protect against open-load conditions
Thermal Limit 45°C surface Reduce power if overheating

Section 3: Foreign Object Detection (FOD)

FOD prevents heating of metallic objects (coins, keys) on the charging surface:

FOD Methods

Method Implementation Accuracy
Power Loss Compare TX input power vs RX reported power Good (standard Qi)
Q-Factor Monitoring Measure coil Q change during ping High
Capacitive Sensing Detect object capacitance on surface Medium
Thermal Sensors Monitor surface temperature Backup safety
Safety Critical: FOD is essential for safety. Metallic objects can heat rapidly (10-50W absorbed) and cause burns or fire. Qi certification requires robust FOD with <500mW threshold.
