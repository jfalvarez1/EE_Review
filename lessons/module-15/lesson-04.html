Systematic Debug Methodology

Efficient debugging requires a systematic approach. Divide and conquer, verify assumptions, and document findings.

Step 1
Understand expected behavior
Step 2
Isolate the problem
Step 3
Form and test hypotheses
Key Insight: Never assume the circuit is built correctly. Verify power, ground, and critical signals before complex debugging. 80% of "circuit doesn't work" issues are simple: missing power, swapped pins, or solder bridges.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake Better Approach
Changing multiple things at once One change at a time, verify each
Assuming schematic matches board Verify critical nets with meter
Trusting component values Measure resistors, caps in circuit
Ignoring probe loading Consider 10pF adds to high-Z nodes
Not reading the datasheet Absolute max, timing, application notes
Skipping power verification Always check all power rails first
Documentation: Keep a debug log. Write down what you measured, what you changed, and what happened. This prevents going in circles and helps others understand your findings.
