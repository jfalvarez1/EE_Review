Class D Amplifiers

Class D amplifiers achieve >90% efficiency by switching the output stage fully on or off, then filtering the PWM signal. They dominate portable and high-power audio applications.

Efficiency
85-95% (vs 25% Class A, 60% Class AB)
Switching Freq
200kHz - 2MHz typical
Key Insight: Class D is essentially a 1-bit DAC + LC filter. The audio signal modulates the duty cycle of a high-frequency carrier. The LC filter averages the PWM to recover the audio.

LC Output Filter Design

Filter Order
2nd order LC (resonant)
Cutoff
fc = 1/(2π√LC)
Typical fc

Damping
Speaker provides damping R
Load Dependency: The LC filter's response depends on speaker impedance. Open-circuit output can ring or oscillate. Many Class D amps include a minimum load resistor.

EMI Considerations

High-frequency switching creates EMI that must be managed:

Spread Spectrum
Modulate switching freq to spread energy
Ferrite Beads

Shielding
Keep filter close to IC, short traces
