Active Crossovers for Speakers

Active crossovers split the audio signal before amplification, allowing each driver (woofer, midrange, tweeter) to have its own dedicated amplifier. This provides better control, lower distortion, and eliminates passive component losses.

Active vs Passive Crossovers

  • Active: Before power amp, low-level signals
  • Passive: After power amp, high-power inductors/caps
  • Bi-amping: 2-way active + 2 amplifiers
  • Tri-amping: 3-way active + 3 amplifiers

Typical Crossover Points

  • Subwoofer: 80-120 Hz
  • Woofer/Mid: 500-800 Hz
  • Mid/Tweeter: 2-4 kHz
  • Slope: 12-24 dB/octave

Linkwitz-Riley Crossover

The Linkwitz-Riley (LR) alignment provides flat summed response at the crossover frequency. LR4 (24 dB/octave) is popular for its steep slope and in-phase outputs.

Sallen-Key Filter Stage

Each 2nd-order stage uses a Sallen-Key topology. For Linkwitz-Riley, Q = 0.707 (Butterworth squared).
