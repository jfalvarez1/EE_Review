# 04 — Red-Team Review: Five Lessons

Adversarial review of five lesson files, concentrating on the `SimCheckWidget` `expect`/`why`
fields and `experiments` arrays, the "Build it in Circuit Toy" tables and the surrounding prose.
Interactive JS models were read where they make quantitative claims. CSS, SVG and canvas
*drawing* code was ignored except where drawing code encodes a physical claim.

Files reviewed:

- `lessons/module-08/lesson-03.html` — chopper-stabilised amplifier
- `lessons/module-09/lesson-04.html` — 555 astable from comparators + SR latch
- `lessons/module-25/lesson-03.html` — USB-C CC pull-up current advertisement
- `lessons/module-18/lesson-09.html` — ratiometric Hall sensor
- `lessons/module-08/lesson-09.html` — charge pump

**Note on M08 L09:** the brief assumed a SimCheck block in the charge pump lesson. There is
none (`grep -c SimCheckWidget` → 0). Its strongest numbers — 6.600 V, 6.591 V, 0.90 Ω — are
asserted in bare prose with no probe list and no tolerances at all. That is worse, not better.

**One verification succeeded outright.** The USB Type-C thresholds were checked against the
USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification, Release 1.3 (14 July 2017), text extracted from
the PDF. Tables 4-10, 4-20, 4-21, 4-22, 4-25, 4-29, 4-30 and 4-32 are quoted below by number.
Where the lesson matches the spec I say so and move on; where it does not, the spec text is
the evidence.

| **M08 L03** — SimCheck probe `sig`: `expect: '1.000'` V for `V(output)`, and prose "it arrives at the filter as DC and passes straight through: **1.000 V** at the output." | **The netlist cannot produce this.** `SW_DEMOD` is specified with pins `amp_out, ground, demod, demod_n` — it alternates `demod` between `amp_out` and *ground*. `amp_out` is a single-ended ±1 V square wave. So `V(demod)` is `+1.01 V, 0, +1.01 V, 0…` — a half-wave chop, mean **0.505 V**, not a demodulated 1.000 V. Recovering 1.000 V requires taking `demod − demod_n` differentially, but `R_LPF` runs from `demod` to `output` and `C_LPF` from `output` to ground, single-ended. A DPDT switching a bipolar signal against ground cannot multiply by −1; it multiplies by 0. **All five probe expectations and all four experiment outcomes fall with it.** | Build the stated netlist. Fix requires either a differential output filter (`R_LPF` from `demod` to `output`, second `R` from `demod_n` to `output_n`) or a demodulator that inverts rather than grounds. Cross-check against any switched-capacitor chopper reference: Enz & Temes, *Proc. IEEE* 84(11), Nov 1996, §III (the demodulator is a ×(±1) multiplier, not a ×(1,0) sampler). |
| **M08 L03** — probe `ampsq`: "Peak-to-peak amplitude of `V(amp_out)`", `expect: '2.02'` V, why: "The extra 20 mV is the offset, **sitting as a DC shift on top of it**." | **Self-refuting in one sentence.** A DC shift adds exactly zero to a peak-to-peak measurement. The correct answer is **2.000 V pp**. Even granting the (wrong) premise, 100 µV × 100 = 10 mV, so the number would be 2.010, not 2.02. Two independent errors stacked. `tol: 0.05` is wide enough to hide the first but the `why` text teaches the misconception explicitly. | Elementary. Measure pp of `(2 V square) + (10 mV DC)` in any simulator. |
| **M08 L03** — probe `ripple`: `expect: '80'` µV pp, why: "attenuated by 20000/159 = 126: **10 mV becomes 80 µV**", repeated in prose as "10 mV × 159/20000 = **80 µV**… a 125-fold improvement". | **Wrong by ~3×, from two compounding errors.** (a) The chopped offset at the demodulator swings ±10 mV, i.e. **20 mV pp**, not 10 mV — amplitude and peak-to-peak are conflated, and the probe label explicitly asks for peak-to-peak. (b) `f_c/f` is the attenuation of a *sinusoid*; the input is a square wave. Correct result: for a square wave with T ≪ τ (T = 50 µs, τ = RC = 1 ms), the output is a triangle of pp ripple = V_pp·T/(4τ) = 20 mV × 50 µs / 4 ms = **250 µV pp**. The fundamental-only estimate, (4/π)(10 mV)/125.7 × 2 = **202 µV pp**, agrees to the harmonic content. The true answer fails the widget's own `tol: 20`. | Standard RC ripple result, e.g. Horowitz & Hill, *The Art of Electronics* 3rd ed. §1.4.3 / §9.x on ripple in RC-filtered switching waveforms; or Fourier decomposition of a square wave (4/π fundamental) into a 1st-order LPF. Simulate: 20 kHz ±10 mV square into 10 k / 100 nF. |
| **M08 L03** — experiment 2 `why`: "**The rejection is a ratio of two frequencies and contains nothing about the amplifier**, so you improve it by chopping faster or filtering harder, not by buying a better op-amp." | **False as stated, and the flagship claim of the lesson.** (a) The *attenuation factor* is a frequency ratio; the *residual ripple* is that factor times A_v·V_os, which is entirely "about the amplifier". (b) The residual DC offset of a real chopper is set by charge-injection mismatch, clock feedthrough, input bias-current asymmetry and switch-charge/settling — none of which is a frequency ratio, and all of which *worsen* with f_chop. (c) The amplifier must settle within a half chop period; if GBW is inadequate the signal gain drops and a residual offset appears. The sentence generalises an ideal-switch, ideal-VCVS model into a claim about real parts. The following sentence half-retracts it ("what eventually stops you is charge injection") without withdrawing the claim. | Enz & Temes, "Circuit techniques for reducing the effects of op-amp imperfections: autozeroing, correlated double sampling, and chopper stabilization," *Proc. IEEE*, vol. 84, no. 11, pp. 1584–1614, 1996 — §IV on residual offset in choppers. Also any modern chopper datasheet's residual-offset vs. chop-frequency curve. |
| **M08 L03** — experiment 3: "60% duty instead of 50% → **A DC error reappears… around 2 mV**… at 60/40 the chopped offset averages to 0.2 × 10 mV = 2 mV." | Arithmetic is right for the ideal model (10 mV·(2D−1) = 2 mV) and the mitigation named (divide an oscillator by two) is real practice. **But** it is presented as the reason duty symmetry matters in real parts, which it is not — commercial choppers already derive the chop clock from a divider, so duty asymmetry is a solved problem and residual offset is dominated by charge injection. Also note the framing overstates the damage: 2 mV on a 1.000 V output is 0.2%, still 5× better than the 1% unchopped case the lesson uses as its baseline. | Same Enz & Temes reference; plus a chopper datasheet's offset-error budget (e.g. LTC2057, ADA4522-2) showing what actually sets V_OS. |
| **M08 L03** — precision op-amp table: "Chopper — V_os **0.1–1 µV**, Bandwidth **10 kHz – 100 kHz**"; auto-zero row cites example ICs "OPA335, LTC2057". | **Contradicted by the parts the same page cites.** OPA335: V_OS 5 µV max, GBW 2 MHz. LTC2057: V_OS 4 µV max, GBW 1.5 MHz. ADA4522-2: GBW 3 MHz. The table's chopper bandwidth is 1–2 decades low and its V_OS an order optimistic. The SimCheck compounds it: "chopper amplifiers are specified in **nanovolts** of offset where an ordinary op-amp is specified in **millivolts**" — the page's own table says precision bipolar is 25–100 µV. | TI OPA335 datasheet (SBOS300) electrical table; ADI LTC2057 datasheet; ADI ADA4522-2 datasheet. All three are free and unambiguous. |
| **M08 L03** — trade-off box: "Signal bandwidth limited to **f_chop/10** typically", carried into the exercise (`expected: 5` kHz at 50 kHz chop) and the checklist. | Presented as a rule with no source. Modern chopper amps break it routinely by chopping around the input stage with a ripple-reduction loop — ADA4522 chops at 800 kHz with 3 MHz GBW, i.e. BW ≈ 4·f_chop. The rule describes the *specific* topology in the build table (chop → amplify → LPF), not choppers generally. | ADA4522 datasheet, §"Theory of Operation" (chopping + ripple-reduction loop). Also the interactive's own model `effVos = vos·(bw/fchop)` invents a *different* relationship than the SimCheck's `fc/fchop` — the two widgets on this page disagree about what sets residual offset. |
| **M08 L03** — probe `dcoff`: `expect: '0'` V, `tol: 0.001`, why: "the offset… has been **moved out of DC entirely**." | Two probes on the same node assert incompatible precision: `sig` allows ±10 mV on `V(output)`, `dcoff` allows ±1 mV on its distance from 1.000 V. Physically, "exactly zero" holds only for ideal switches, exactly 50% duty and infinite settling — the very assumptions experiment 3 goes on to break. No boundary condition is stated with the claim. | — |
| **M08 L03** — experiment 1: "hold VCLK at a constant 5 V… V(output) becomes **1.010 V**." | Undocumented assumption: which state the DPDT parks in. Held in the other phase the output is 0 V (`demod` sits on ground) or −0.99 V (with a corrected demodulator). The stated answer is one of two. | — |
| **M09 L04** — probe `freq`: `expect: '481'` Hz; `thigh` 1.386 ms; `tlow` 0.693 ms; `duty` 66.7%; and "1/(0.693 × 30 kΩ × 100 nF) = 481 Hz. The familiar 1.44/((R1+2R2)C) is the same equation with 1/ln 2 rounded to 1.44." | **Correct, and correctly derived.** 1/(ln2 · 30 k · 100 n) = 480.9 Hz; t_H = 0.693·20k·100n = 1.386 ms; t_L = 0.693 ms; duty 66.67%; 1/ln2 = 1.4427. The ln((1−⅓)/(1−⅔)) = ln 2 derivation is right, and the observation that V_CC divides out is right. The best-supported block in the five files. **One unstated boundary condition:** t_low = 0.693·(R2 + R_DS(on))·C and the capacitor discharges toward V_DS(sat), not 0 V, so `tlow` is systematically long — the lesson says "Discharge is through **R2 only**" and never names M1's on-resistance, even though experiment 2 later invokes V_sat. | Verified by inspection. R_DS(on) effect: NE555 datasheet (TI SLFS022) DISCH low-level output voltage table. |
| **M09 L04** — experiment 1: "Change VCC from 5 V to 12 V… **The frequency does not move**… **A ratiometric threshold is what buys supply independence** — the same trick a successive-approximation ADC uses to be **insensitive to its own reference**." | (a) The SAR analogy is **backwards**. A SAR ADC's output code is entirely proportional to 1/V_REF; it is maximally *sensitive* to its reference. What is reference-insensitive is a *ratiometric sensor measurement* where the bridge excitation and V_REF are the same node. As written the sentence teaches the opposite of the truth. (b) "The frequency does not move" is exact only in the ideal model. Real non-ratiometric terms: the discharge transistor's V_sat (does not scale with V_CC), comparator propagation delay, and threshold-comparator input bias current (~0.5 µA on a bipolar 555, which subtracts from the charging current and does not scale). None is mentioned. (c) "the frequency quoted to a couple of percent" over 4.5–16 V needs a datasheet number. | (a) Any data-conversion text, e.g. Kester (ed.), *Data Conversion Handbook*, ch. 3, on ratiometric operation. (b),(c) TI NE555 datasheet SLFS022: supply-voltage sensitivity spec (Δf/ΔV_CC), trigger/threshold input current, and the 4.5–16 V range. |
| **M09 L04** — experiment 2: "the high time **barely moves**, from **1.386 ms to 0.700 ms** — halving because R2 dropped out of the charge path too, **not because the discharge got faster**." | **Self-contradictory in one sentence** ("barely moves" = a 50% change), and the causal claim is wrong: the discharge *did* get faster, by 100× (0.693 ms → 6.93 µs). The frequency rise from 481 Hz to 1.41 kHz comes from both terms. The numbers (0.700 ms, 6.9 µs, 1.41 kHz, 99%) are all arithmetically right; the explanation attached to them is not. | Arithmetic only. |
| **M09 L04** — experiment 2: "the discharge transistor now has to sink **V_CC/100 Ω = 50 mA**, and a real 555's pin 7 is rated for about **200 mA** but its saturation voltage climbs enough at that current to **start shifting the lower threshold**." | Three problems. (a) **Wrong voltage.** The voltage across R2 at the start of discharge is the capacitor voltage, ⅔V_CC = 3.33 V, not V_CC. Peak sink current ≈ 3.33/100 = **33 mA** (plus 0.5 mA through R1), not 50 mA. (b) The 200 mA figure is the NE555's **output** (pin 3) rating; pin 7's discharge rating is a different, lower and part-dependent number. (c) **Wrong mechanism.** V_sat does not shift the lower threshold — the ⅓V_CC tap is set by the internal divider and is independent of the discharge transistor. What V_sat changes is the *asymptote* the capacitor discharges toward, which lengthens t_low. | TI NE555 datasheet SLFS022: absolute-maximum ratings (output current) and the DISCH pin's low-level output-voltage/current characteristic. |
| **M09 L04** — experiment 4: "**This is why the 555 is a sub-100 kHz part** no matter what R and C you choose, and why the datasheet frequency error grows from 1% to **tens of percent** across its range." | **Both halves are false.** (a) The TLC555 datasheet headline is "operating frequency up to **2.1 MHz**" — 21× the claimed hard ceiling — and the same page's Design Tips box already mentions the CMOS 7555, so the lesson contradicts itself. Bipolar NE555 designs run to several hundred kHz. (b) No 555 datasheet specifies "frequency error vs. frequency"; the quoted 1%→tens-of-percent curve does not exist. (c) The experiment's `expect` field contains **no number at all** ("does not rise by a thousand times… falls short"), and with the ideal op-amps the build actually specifies, the frequency *will* rise to ~481 kHz — the widget's own header claims "every number below is derived from the values in the build table above", and this one is not derivable from it. (d) "a few hundred nanoseconds" of comparator delay inside a bipolar 555 is not a datasheet parameter. | TI TLC555 datasheet SLFS029 (front page, max operating frequency). TI NE555 SLFS022 (there is no frequency-accuracy-vs-frequency spec). |
| **M09 L04** — experiment 3: "If you ever do see the two overlap, the divider taps are in the wrong order and **the circuit will latch up** instead of oscillating." | Unsupported. With the taps swapped both comparators assert between the thresholds, driving S and R high simultaneously — the SR latch's forbidden state. What happens next depends entirely on the latch implementation (NOR vs NAND, race behaviour on release); "latch up" is a specific and different phenomenon (parasitic SCR conduction in CMOS). Also, at t = 0 with C1 discharged, `rstq` is high for an extended interval, so "two **narrow** pulses" is not what the run starts with. | Any digital-logic text on the SR latch forbidden state, e.g. Wakerly, *Digital Design*, §7.2.1. |
| **M09 L04** — the interactive's capacitor-voltage plot: `vCap = 0.33 + 0.33·(1 − exp(−t/(tHigh·0.69)))` charging, `0.66·exp(…) + 0.33·(1 − exp(…))` discharging. | The drawn curve is not the circuit's waveform. A 555 capacitor charges *toward V_CC* and is interrupted at ⅔ — the visible curvature over ⅓→⅔ is that of an exponential aimed at 1.0, not at 0.66. The discharge expression asymptotes to 0.33 rather than 0. A dead `const tau = (R1 + R2) * 1; // Normalized` sits unused above it. The plot teaches the wrong shape for exactly the thing the lesson says is worth seeing. | Derive V_C(t) = V_CC + (V_0 − V_CC)e^(−t/RC) and plot the ⅓→⅔ segment. |
| **M25 L03** — "0.941 V lands in the **0.70–1.16 V band**"; "**1.31–2.04 V band**"; "the default band, which ends at **0.61 V**". | **Verified correct.** USB Type-C Cable and Connector Spec R1.3, **Table 4-32 "Voltage on Sink CC pins (Multiple Source Current Advertisements)"**: vRd-USB 0.25–0.61 V; vRd-1.5 0.70–1.16 V; vRd-3.0 1.31–2.04 V. Divider arithmetic also checks: 5·5.1/27.1 = 940.96 mV; 5·5.1/15.1 = 1.68874 V; 5·5.1/61.1 = 417 mV. No fault found in the band values themselves. | USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification, Release 1.3, Table 4-32, p. 203. |
| **M25 L03** — experiment 4: "665 mV… lands in the **gap** between the default band, which ends at 0.61 V, and the 1.5 A band, which begins at 0.70 V, and **the standard does not say what a sink should do there**. **Two compliant implementations may legitimately disagree**." | **False, and refuted by the very table the bands come from.** Table 4-32 has a third column, **"Threshold"**: 0.2 V (vRa), **0.66 V** (vRd-USB → vRd-1.5) and **1.23 V** (vRd-1.5 → vRd-3.0). The spec puts the decision points in the "gaps" precisely so implementations *do* agree. 665 mV is not in an undefined region; it is **5 mV above the specified 0.66 V threshold**, so a compliant sink reads it as **vRd-1.5 (1.5 A)**. The real hazard — that 5 mV is inside any real comparator's offset and hysteresis, so the reading is marginal — is a better story than the one told, and the lesson misses it. The 665 mV arithmetic itself is right (5.1k ‖ 10k = 3.3775k; 5·3.3775/25.3775 = 665.4 mV). | USB Type-C Spec R1.3, Table 4-32, "Threshold" column, p. 203. |
| **M25 L03** — probe `margin_lo`: "when R_p and R_d are each **±5%** parts. **Work the corners: 22 k high with 5.1 k low gives 0.867 V and the other corner gives 1.020 V**, both still inside — but the low corner has only 167 mV left. That margin is why the **standard specifies 1% resistors** on this pin." | **The tolerances are invented and the conclusion is the opposite of the truth.** Spec **Table 4-21**: Rd is **5.1 kΩ ±20%** (cannot read the advertisement) or **5.1 kΩ ±10%** (can). **Table 4-20**: Rp is 22 kΩ **±5%** / 10 kΩ **±5%** / 56 kΩ **±20%**, and the pull-up rail is **4.75–5.5 V**, not 5.000 V. There is **no 1% requirement anywhere in the specification.** Redo the corners properly (Rp 22k ±5%, Rd 5.1k ±10%, V 4.75–5.5 V): low corner = 4.75·4.59/27.69 = **0.787 V** (87 mV of margin, half the claimed 167 mV); high corner = 5.5·5.61/26.51 = **1.164 V** — which *is* the 1.16 V band edge. The band limits in Table 4-32 **are** the worst-case corners. The lesson's "comfortable margin" is an artefact of using tolerances tighter than the spec's and a rail fixed at exactly 5 V. | USB Type-C Spec R1.3, Table 4-20 (p. 197) and Table 4-21 (p. 198). Note Table 4-20 also permits a **current source** (80 µA ±20% / 180 µA ±8% / 330 µA ±8%) and a **3.3 V ±5% pull-up** with different resistors (36k/12k/4.7k) — three legal implementations, of which the lesson models one without saying so. |
| **M25 L03** — probes `cc` (`expect '940.96'` mV, `tol: 10`) and `cc2` (`expect '1.68874'` V, `tol: 0.02`). | False precision at war with the component tolerances stated two probes later. | **M25 L03** — experiment 3: "Put Rd on both CC lines at once, as an **incorrectly wired sink** would… the source **cannot tell which way round the plug is**." | **Wrong: Rd/Rd is a defined, detected state.** Spec §4.5.1.2 and **Table 4-10 "Source Perspective"**: Rd/Rd = **"Debug Accessory Mode attached"**; Ra/Ra = "Audio Adapter Accessory Mode attached"; Rd/Open or Open/Rd = "Sink attached" with orientation from which pin. | **M25 L03** — experiment 2: "Short cc to ground… the source sees it as **vRa**… **Below about 0.2 V** the source reads the pin as an electronically marked cable's Ra rather than as a device… **A shorted CC line presents as a cable rather than as a fault**." | The *conclusion* is correct and citable (Table 4-10: Open/Ra = "Powered cable without Sink attached" → no VBUS). | **M18 L09** — `const tempCoeff = -0.12; // ~-0.12%/°C for InSb, -0.04%/°C for Si`, surfaced as "Temp Coefficient **-0.12%/°C**" and reused as the premise of exercise ex3. | Hard-coded, and the code comment attributes it to **InSb** while every part, model and exercise on the page is silicon. | **M08 L09** — Design Equations: "**Dickson (N stages): V_out = (N+1) × V_in − N × V_F − I × R_out**", against the topologies table's "Dickson / **V_in × N**". | (a) The two statements of Dickson gain disagree (N vs N+1). (b) The standard result is V_out = V_in + N(**V_φ** − V_F) − N·I/(f·C), where **V_φ is the clock amplitude**, not V_in. The lesson silently assumes clock swing = V_in — while its own build table uses a **5 V clock with a 3.3 V input**, where the assumption is false by 50%. (c) R_out is left as the doubler's 1/(fC); for an N-stage Dickson it scales with N. | Dickson, "On-chip high-voltage generation in MNOS integrated circuits using an improved voltage multiplier technique," *IEEE JSSC*, vol. 11, no. 3, pp. 374–378, 1976. |
| **M08 L09** — "**Voltage Drop: ΔV = I_load × R_out + 2×V_sw**", implemented as `const Vdrop = Iload * Rout + 0.2; // 2x 0.1V switch drops`. | V_sw is defined nowhere on the page. A MOSFET switch has a *resistance*, not a fixed 100 mV drop — that is diode language applied to FETs — and any resistive component of the drop already belongs inside R_out (as R_FSL), so the term double-counts. The consequence is a visible contradiction: the interactive shows a **220 mV** total drop at the default 10 mA, while the build-table prose 300 lines below asserts **9 mV** for the same class of circuit and no switch drop at all. | Seeman & Sanders (above) for the correct R_FSL formulation. |

The table's per-topology percentages therefore cannot be derived from the page's own equation, and the 5-point doubler-vs-cross-coupled gap is unattributable: both have the identical ideal η = V_out/(2V_in); they differ in gate-drive and reversion loss, not in the fundamental limit. The header kv box adds a third figure, "Typically 80-90%". | Seeman & Sanders (above), §II. |

## Ranking: how badly a reader is misled

**Tier 1 — a reader who follows the instructions gets a result that cannot match the page, or learns something false about a real part.**

1. **M08 L03: the chopper build cannot produce any of its five stated probe values.** `SW_DEMOD` chops `amp_out` against ground, which half-wave-rectifies rather than demodulates. The output is ~0.505 V, not 1.000 V. Every probe, every experiment and the entire narrative rest on it. A reader who builds it correctly and gets 0.505 V has no way to tell whether they made a mistake or the page did, because the page presents its numbers as "what a correct build reads". This is the single worst finding in the set: it is not a wrong number, it is a wrong circuit.

2. **M18 L09: real part numbers with invented characteristics.** "DRV5053, 45 mV/mT, quiescent at V_CC/2 = 2.5 V, output 3.85 V at +30 mT." The actual part quiesces at 1.0 V, has negative sensitivity in most variants, and physically cannot output 3.85 V. This is the failure mode that survives the lesson and reaches a purchase order.

3. **M25 L03: "the standard specifies 1% resistors on this pin" and the ±5%/±5% corner analysis.** The spec says Rd is 5.1 kΩ ±20% (±10% if the sink reads the advertisement) and the rail is 4.75–5.5 V. Redone properly, the corners are 0.787 V and 1.164 V — the worst case *is* the band edge, with 87 mV of margin, not the comfortable 167 mV claimed. A reader doing a real tolerance stack from this page will under-budget by roughly 2× and reach the opposite conclusion about whether the design closes.

4. **M09 L04: "the 555 is a sub-100 kHz part no matter what R and C you choose."** The TLC555's own front page says 2.1 MHz. The claim is stated as a hard physical ceiling, attributed to a comparator delay figure that appears in no datasheet, and supported by a fabricated "datasheet frequency error" characteristic. The lesson also contradicts itself by discussing the 7555 two cards earlier.

**Tier 2 — the number is right but the physics attached to it is wrong, so the reader generalises incorrectly.**

5. **M08 L03 "the rejection… contains nothing about the amplifier"** and **M08 L09 "regulation improves by switching faster, not by any component being better."** The same rhetorical move, twice, in the same module: an ideal-component result promoted to a statement about real hardware. Both are false in the regime that matters — chopper residual offset is set by charge injection, and charge-pump R_out floors out at the fast-switching limit set by switch resistance. Both sentences are formatted in `<strong>`, i.e. flagged as the takeaway.

6. **M25 L03: "665 mV… the standard does not say what a sink should do there. Two compliant implementations may legitimately disagree."** Refuted by the Threshold column of the very table the bands come from: the spec puts decision points at 0.66 V and 1.23 V precisely to prevent this. 665 mV reads as 1.5 A. The lesson teaches "the standard has a hole" where the truth is "the standard has a threshold and you are 5 mV from it" — which is the more useful lesson and the one it displaces.

7. **M25 L03: "Rd on both CC lines… the source cannot tell which way round the plug is."** Rd/Rd is Debug Accessory Mode, a named state in Table 4-10. The claim also contradicts the lesson's own "Critical" notice about fitting Rd to both pins, two cards earlier.

8. **M08 L03: the 80 µV residual (true ~250 µV pp)** and **M08 L09's Dickson equation with V_φ silently replaced by V_in.** Both are derivations with a missing step that changes the answer.

**Tier 3 — wrong numbers a careful reader will catch, and internal contradictions.**

9. M08 L03's 2.02 V pp (a DC offset added to a peak-to-peak measurement); M18 L09's "most of a tesla" (0.167 T) and ±480 mT (should be 460); M09 L04's 50 mA discharge current (should be ~33 mA) and V_sat "shifting the lower threshold" (it shifts the asymptote); M09 L04's "barely moves, from 1.386 ms to 0.700 ms."

10. Graded exercises whose keyed answer is wrong or ambiguous: M25 L03's "CC reads 0 V → missing pull-down" (missing Rd gives 5 V, per the same file's own probe) and its two-correct-answers PDO question.

11. Widget models that contradict the SimCheck on the same page: M18 L09's "Hall Voltage" readout off by the ×50 gain; M25 L03's Rp-only current classification that ignores the pull-up-voltage slider; M08 L09's 0.2 V switch drop vs. the prose's zero; M08 L03's `effVos = vos·(bw/fchop)` vs. the SimCheck's `fc/fchop`; M09 L04's fake capacitor exponential; M08 L09's self-normalising ripple plot.

**What was checked and found sound.** M09 L04's frequency, high/low times and duty cycle, including the ln 2 derivation and the 1/ln 2 → 1.44 remark — the strongest block in the five files. M25 L03's three threshold bands (0.25–0.61 / 0.70–1.16 / 1.31–2.04 V), verified verbatim against Table 4-32, along with all the divider arithmetic (940.96 mV, 1.68874 V, 417 mV, 665 mV, 937 mV) and the "shorted CC presents as a cable" conclusion (Table 4-10). M18 L09's five probe values and the 1.667 V loading result. M08 L09's three practice exercises and the 50 ns non-overlap timing. M25 L03's discharge-resistor and power arithmetic. The problem in these files is rarely the arithmetic — it is the sentence that follows it.
